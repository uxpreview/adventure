# SPLITROCK CANYON — land spec

*Session 11. A hole in the page, and one man at the top of it.
`THE-WAITS.md` §4 is binding: **the marks up the wall are a list, in the
order things would float, and the game never says so.***

Rect: x 230 → 380, z −280 → −100. Wash `WASH.canyon`. Voice: struck
metal, low register, with the only TAIL in the game — *stone struck once
by stone a long way off, and the cut keeps it* (`Audio.ts`, `LAND_VOICE`

## 0. THE DECISION THIS SESSION TOOK FIRST, BEFORE A SINGLE PROP

**THE TEAR WAS REAL AND IT WAS IN THE WRONG PLACE.**

Session 4 authored a good cut: `tearX(z) ≈ 338`, a floor at −10.8, lips
standing proud, walls that refuse a walker. But 338 is six units from
where the world's curled east margin starts lifting (`curlE` ramps from
x = 344), the tear only existed between about z = −272 and z = −132, and
**the canyon trail ran at x = 255..305 and never came within thirty
units of it.** The land called SPLITROCK had its split jammed into its
own east gutter. It was a scatter of mesas with a slot behind them.

The prompt allowed either answer. This session took the expensive one:

**THE TEAR IS AT x = 300 NOW, WHICH IS THE MIDDLE OF ITS OWN RECT**, and
every consequence was audited rather than assumed.

| what moved | why, and what it cost |
|---|---|
| `tearX` base 338 → 300 | the land's one feature is now in the land. Sixty units of walkable bench west of it and thirty east, and both of them are places |
| `tearMouth(z)` | the mouth is a **sixty-two-unit ramp** (nothing at z = −124, full depth by −186) and the head is a **fourteen-unit wall** (full to −254, closed by −268). A tear starts shallow and deepens: that is why there is no stair to author anywhere in this land |
| the amplitude, −13.0 → −13.75 | `check-terrain` measures the floor at the axis, and the accumulated `smax` bias and the local cockle differ at x = 300. **It still prints −10.8.** `THE-STRANGERS` S5 is an errand about that number and it was not this session's to spend |
| the floor's width, 12 → 18 units | eighteen is the narrowest a slot can be and still hold a boat, a shed, a man and a walker without any of them standing on a cliff |
| `RIVER[0]`, (318, −108) → (296, −116) | the river still rises at the canyon's MOUTH, six units south of where the rip begins. That relationship is `THE-WAITS` §4 already true in the height field and it was never a bug |
| the canyon trail, all of it | see §2 |
| `tearFloorK` → `terrain.ts` | the channel floor is painted as a dry bed rather than as canyon, so a walker on the west lip can see that the channel is a channel |

**What did NOT change: the depth, the vocabulary, the bearing.** `tearX`
is a function of z, so the canyon runs north–south and the camera's law
is obeyed by the ground itself. That is most of why this landform was
worth moving rather than re-cutting.

## 1. THE SHOT

**Stand on the channel floor at (305, −222) and look north.** You are
thirty units from the end of the walk and everything in this land's
fable is in one frame, at four heights, and not one word of it is
written anywhere.

| layer | what |
|---|---|
| **foreground** | the west wall's hatching running out of the left of frame, and a slab leaning against its foot. The floor is pale, sorted, and dry |
| **near** | **THE BOAT**, keel up on trestles, oiled — a pale horizontal in a land with no other horizontals in it |
| **subject** | **THE MARK SLAB**: twenty units of wall face at the head of the channel with four chalk strokes up it. Bottom to top they are the boat (1.2), the trestles (2.3), the shed's ridge (4.8) and **the doorstep (12.6) — which is the height of the lip, and the lip is right there beside it** |
| **middle** | **THE SHED**, forty units down the channel, whose ridge is the third mark. **HOLT**, beside the boat, bent over it with a rag |
| **far** | above the wall's top edge, on the rock, **a fifth chalk mark** — `THE-STRANGERS` U20: *one mark is above the lip, which means it is not a flood mark* — and behind it, on the rim, **HOLT'S HOUSE** |
| **accent** | none. **There is no blue anywhere in this frame**, in the one land whose whole subject is water |
| **moving** | one bird turning fifteen units above the slot; grit drifting north up the floor; Holt's rag |

**They are a list, in the order things would float.** The frame says it
by lining four heights up with four things you can see from where you
are standing, and a fifth up with a roof. Nothing labels the marks.
There is no scale, no date and no number anywhere in this land.

**After you have rowed the river, salt to source, under all three
bridges** — which nothing else in this world has done — the same frame
has one difference: the trestles are empty and the boat is down on the
floor of a dry channel, right way up, bow north, oars shipped. **The
game never says whether that is madness or readiness.**

**THE ONLY ROAD IN SPLITROCK IS A RIVERBED, AND THAT IS THE LAND'S FABLE
IN A POLYLINE.** The Penwood said its own in `layout.ROADS` (Session 10)
and this land can say its own in the same place. The trail comes up out
of the Flats, rounds the head of the river, drops down the mouth, and
then **every point from z = −136 north is `tearX(z)` sampled at
eight-unit intervals** — it is not following a route, it is lying in the
bottom of a channel, and paper does not tear straight. It ends, dead, at
a wall. The map draws it. Nothing else says it.

**And the geography teaches one fact by making you walk it:** the east
bench is thirty units away across the channel and the only way onto it
is all the way back to the mouth and round. Holt's house is on the west
rim above the head wall, and his boat is a hundred and twelve units of
channel and a walk round the mouth away from it, every day. Amos next
door walks forty units each way every night. **Neither land mentions the
other's walk and neither man knows about it.**

## 3. COMPOSITION PLAN

**The rule that replaces the draft's scatter:**

> **NOTHING IN THIS LAND IS PLACED IN THE OPEN. IT IS PLACED AGAINST A
> WALL, ON A LIP, OR ON THE FLOOR OF THE CHANNEL.**

Three registers, and every drawing knows which one it is in:

- **THE WALL'S TOE.** Nine slabs in a hundred and twelve units, standing
  on the FLOOR at 8.2 units off the axis, no taller than the wall behind
  them, turned about sixty degrees toward the channel. *(Round 1 of the
  gate stood twenty-one-unit panels every thirteen units up both walls
  at half their height and built a tunnel with the ends bricked up —
  see §10.)*
- **THE SKYLINE.** Seventeen stacks, none taller than thirteen units,
  none within twenty-six of another, standing OUTSIDE the lip and never
  on the wall. The gaps between them are most of what makes a skyline.
  Six more, further back and smaller, are the haze layer.
- **THE FLOOR.** Dry-bed decals down the axis; blocks against the toes
  where they landed and never in the middle, because the middle is where
  the water ran; scree at the toes; grit that moves; four dead scrubs
  wedged on the insides of bends, because that is where a river drops
  what it is carrying.

**Density gradient:** heaviest from z −180 to −250 (the corridor's
second half, where the walls are full height and the wait is), thinning
to almost nothing at the mouth so the arrival is an opening rather than
a room. **The east bench is a composed void** — six stacks and one
cairn in sixty-six units by a hundred and thirty — and its job is to be
the far silhouette for every framing on the floor and on the west rim.

**Cut from the draft:** six mesa cutouts along the north edge, four
along the east, forty-six boulders and fourteen cacti on a Poisson
scatter, and an arch standing in the middle of a field. `mesaTexture`,
`archRockTexture`, `cactusTexture`, `duneDecal`, `skullTexture`,
`tumbleweedTexture` and `palmTexture` are deleted from `textures.ts`.

## 4. INK TECHNIQUE

> **Everything in SPLITROCK is drawn in strokes DOWN the page.**

The terrain shader already hatches a cliff down its fall line, and a
torn edge of paper has no strata in it — it has fibres, and they run the
way the tear ran. So every drawing in `textures-canyon.ts` is FLUTING:
long vertical runs, close together, heavier at the bottom of the mark
than at the top, with the corners between faces left as the only breaks.
No horizontals. Not on the walls, not on the stacks, not on the fallen
blocks, not on the shed, not on the house.

**With five exceptions, and they are the point of the land.** The chalk
marks are the only marks in a hundred and fifty units of canyon that go
ACROSS anything, which is why they read as writing without a label.

**THE BLEACH FLATS next door are drawn in the other direction for
exactly the same reason.** The pair is a hole in the page against the
flattest ground in the world, and they are drawn at right angles.

| texture | canvas | variants | primitives |
|---|---|---|---|
| `wallPanelTexture` | 512×256 | 3 | torn top as straight runs with corners, `fluteFace`, `feather` |
| `stackTexture` | 256×384 | 3 | three convex PLANS, hard-cornered; every face planar |
| `blockTexture` | 192×128 | 3 | as above, angular |
| `needleArchTexture` | 512×320 | 1 | the one curve; the opening is taken out with `destination-out` |
| `markSlabTexture` | 512×384 | 1 | four chalk runs at authored heights; the crown is faded out so the face runs INTO the terrain behind it |
| `rimMarkTexture` | 160×96 | 1 | the fifth mark |
| `boatOnTrestlesTexture` / `trestlesTexture` / `boatRightedTexture` | 384×200 | 1 each | the two states plus the empty trestles |
| `holtTexture` | 112×176 | 3 | oiling, reading the wall, stopped |
| `holtShedTexture` / `holtHouseTexture` | 256×224, 320×288 | 1 / 2 (dark, lit) | |
| `dryBedDecal` | 256×256 | 3 | stain + runs + angular chips |
| `screeTexture`, `gritTexture`, `deadScrubTexture`, `slotShadowTexture`, `bootsTexture`, `cairnTexture`, `mouthPostTexture`, `turningBirdTexture`, `fallDustTexture` | small | 1–2 | |

Two shared helpers carry the file: **`stain()`** (a radial gradient —
never a filled polygon as a colour) and **`hardPoly()`**, which draws a
polygon edge by edge so its corners stay corners. `stroke` curves
through midpoints, which is right for a hedge and fatal for a tear.

## 5. MOTION & LIFE

- **the bird** — one, turning fifteen units above the slot on a
  forty-second circle, changing the set of its wings. The only thing in
  SPLITROCK that is above you.
- **the grit** — a field with a one-sided WAVE on it running north up
  the channel, which is the only wind that can get in here.
- **HOLT's day** — at the boat from six to eight, oiling; a stretch in
  the middle of the day at the foot of the wall with his head back; and
  at night he is not here and there is a light on up on the rim, which
  is the only lit window in the east half of the world.
- **PLAYER-RESPONSIVE: he straightens.** Come inside fifteen units and
  Holt stops work and stands up facing out. It costs one drawing, and
  it is a man stopping because somebody is walking up his channel, which
  out here has not happened in a while.

All per-frame except the grit's wave, which is shader time.

## 6. SOUND

Voice and bed unchanged (`Audio.ts`): struck metal, low, and the
quietest bed in the game so that the tail on everything else can be
heard. `TAILS.canyon` is 0.55 — **this land repeats you.**

| event | trigger | synthesis |
|---|---|---|
| `slot-wind` | on the floor, between z −132 and −258 | two noise bands a fifth apart, swelling and letting go, neither settling on a pitch. A slot is a pipe with one end open, so it has a NOTE rather than a hiss |
| `hull-rag` | within 34 units of the boat, six to eight | three or four filtered strokes with the pause of somebody working rather than a rhythm. **The only made sound in the land** |
| `stone-fall` | mostly 10:30–15:00, anywhere | one stone lets go, three follow it at falling pitches, then a slide already dying when it starts. `THE-STRANGERS` C19: *a rockfall you hear and do not see* — and it is never drawn |

Step zone `stone`, unchanged.

**The register is the constraint:** *short sentences. People out here do
not use two words* (`THE-WAITS` §4). These are the shortest notes in the
game and that is deliberate.

| THE MOUTH | 291, −134 | GO DOWN | *the page is only scratched here. walk north and it opens. there is no other way in and no way out at the far end.* |
| THE OVERLOOK | 276, −170 | — | *the whole cut, end to end, from the one place you can see it from. the other rim is forty units away and about an hour.* |
| THE NEEDLE ARCH | 300, −181 | STAND UNDER IT | *a hole worn through solid rock by weather and insistence. you stand under it. it holds.* |

**THE TRESTLES' label sits west of the boat at height 3.4**, and that is
Session 10's lesson applied before it cost anything: the skyline writes a
name above the tallest thing UNDER it and cannot know what is BEHIND it,
and behind this POI there is a fifteen-unit slab, a rim mark and a house,
all on one axis. Height does not solve that. **Angle does.**

## 8. PERFORMANCE BUDGET

Sixteen instanced fields (three wall variants, three stacks, three
blocks, three bed states, scree, shadow, grit, scrub) and about twenty
one-off standees. Every drawing is made ONCE and instanced — the whole
land is twelve canvases plus the one-offs, well inside the four-megabyte
budget Session 10's round 4 set. No per-instance canvases anywhere.

The update loop is three visibility flips, one position set for a bird
and one for Holt. Nothing in this land iterates a field per frame except
the grit, whose motion is in the vertex stage.

## 10. WHAT THE GATE FOUND, IN ORDER

Kept because the next land session will make at least two of these.

1. **THE TEXTURE SHEET FOUND EVERY DRAWING FAULT IN ONE LOOK, AS
   PROMISED.** Round 1 of `shoot-textures.mjs` came back with a canyon
   full of DOMES: `stroke` draws quadratics through the midpoints of its
   points, so every polygon of straight runs — stacks, blocks, the shed,
   the house, the mark slab — came out as a lozenge. `hardPoly` fixed
   all of them at once. **Paper does not tear along a curve is a rule
   about the drawing, not only about the height field.**
2. **A CURTAIN IS NOT A CORRIDOR.** Round 1 of the world sheet stood
   twenty-one-unit wall panels up both walls at half their height. They
   stand on a face whose gradient is two, so their feet were five units
   up the wall and their heads twelve over the lip, and eighteen units
   of channel cannot hold two of them and a walker. THE SHOT came back
   as a brown wall. **The terrain already draws these walls, and better
   than a cutout can.**
3. **A FACE AT THE HEAD OF A CHANNEL MUST NOT BE OUTLINED ON TOP.**
   Round 2's mark slab was ten units wide on a canvas twice as tall as
   it was wide, and it read as a TOMBSTONE. It is twenty wide, its two
   flanks are inked and its crown is faded out, so it runs into the
   terrain's own hatching instead of standing in front of it.
4. **AND THE FOURTH MARK VANISHED INTO THAT FADE**, which is the one
   fault in this session that would have shipped: the doorstep mark is
   the highest of the four and it sat inside the crown's erase. Draw the
   fade first and the marks after it.
5. **CHECK WHICH WAY YOU ARE FACING BEFORE YOU BOOK THE FRAMING.** Two
   of this sheet's framings stood NORTH of their subject and photographed
   the back of the walker's head. North is −Z.
