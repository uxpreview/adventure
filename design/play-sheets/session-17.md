## 1. Noon on the Downs. (`?hour=12`, 0:00)

Walk east from the crossroads. Past the bridge, at the field gate, **a
dog falls in beside you.** Keep going east along the road, or north up
the mill lane through the herd. Walk into the cows: they look up, then
they move over, slowly, the way cows do. Then walk to any edge of the
Downs — west back over the bridge to the Common is quickest. **The dog
stops dead at the edge and sits, looking after you.**

*Ask:* did you get the rule on the second animal, the way the critic
said you would? Is a dog on a road funnier or sadder than a goat? Did
the cows part, or did you walk through them?

## 2. Noon, still: the mill, and the Penwood. (2:00)

At the mill, somebody is going in and out of the door all day, and at
nine he carries a sack to the granary. Walk north into the Penwood.
**A heron stands at the water's edge.** Walk toward it. It goes up,
croaks twice, and is gone. Come back in a minute and it is standing
there again. On the ring, at noon, somebody is walking the round, the
way everybody does, and out the way they came in.

*Ask:* did the heron frighten you or please you? Was the person on
the round a person, or furniture?

## 3. Dusk in Brim. (`?hour=19.2`, 4:00)

Stand in the square. **The lamplighter comes out of the belfry yard
with a pole at five past seven** and does the four lamps in order:
south-west, south-east, north-east, north-west. Each one comes on as
he reaches it. Watch the windows: at nine the shutters go over, row
by row, and a shutter clacks if you are in the street to hear it. On
the Common, at eight, the bull lies down in the field. Walk in to it.
It gets up.

*Ask:* did you see the lamps come on one at a time, or all at once?
Did you wait for the fourth? Did the shutters make the town smaller?

## 4. The thing under the Wide Blue. (`?hour=19.3`, 6:00)

Walk out onto the sandbar to the far end, near the bell. **At twenty
past seven something surfaces**, fifty units north of you, for ten
seconds — a back, a fin, and the water going white — and goes under.
Nothing else about it. In the day, seals haul out on the bar; walk at
them and they slip in.

*Ask:* did you see it? Did it frighten you? Do you want to know what
it was? (Nothing here will ever tell you.)

## 5. Night, and the storm. (`?day=1&hour=22.7`, 7:30)

The second night has the first storm. Rain runs the drawing — the ink
bleeds down the page — and the whole frame flashes, and a second or
two later the thunder. Walk north from the Common into the Penwood,
and on into the deep pines at the north-east. **Everything stops.**
No pine-tick, no owl, no axe. Stand there. Once in a long while, a long
way off, a branch goes.

*Ask:* is the storm too much on a phone? Is the rain readable as rain
or as noise? Did the silence in the deep pines register as silence, or
as nothing happening?

## 6. The fog. (`?day=1&hour=5.9`, 9:00)

First light on the second day. Stand at the crossroads. **The four
lures are gone** — the keep, the smoke, the sea, the towers — and the
signpost points at nothing you can see. Wait. By seven the fog lifts
and they come back.

*Ask:* on a fresh page the lures are how you knew where to go. In a
fog, do you still? Should the fog ever be allowed on the first day?
(It is not: day zero is calm at noon and at dusk, with one shower in
the middle of the afternoon.)

## The unnamed, if you have longer

Every land has people in it now who are somewhere at a given hour and
indoors otherwise, none of them named, all of them on the clock. Some
you will only meet by being there:

- **The Common:** the fisher at the river bend before seven; the cart
  down from Brim's gate at half past; water drawn at the well at nine;
  three people arguing under the oaks at ten and at four — sit in the
  swing and watch who has the floor; the children on the fair ground
  after three; a fox after nine at night, which stops when it sees you.
- **Brim:** the sweeper before six; the delivery that finds the stall
  shut at half past seven; the warden counting the orchard at eight and
  at six; the children round the fountain after three; the cat on the
  south wall by the gate, which sits up if you RUN under it.
- **Greyweather:** the groom with a bucket at seven; the washerwoman at
  the moat pool through the morning; the pilgrims up the avenue at half
  past eight and at four; the sentry along the wall at dusk and before
  dawn; bats over the pool at dusk. And three rooks that roost on the
  keep and spend the day on a scarecrow in the Downs.
- **Maple Court:** the jogger at half past six; the first car backs out
  at ten past eight, drives to the end of the survey — there is nowhere
  else a car in this world can go — and comes back at ten to six; the
  post at ten; children on the green after school; the cat on Val's
  fence.
- **Longshore:** the beachcomber on the wrack at first light; the hut
  owner who opens the third hut at nine and sits outside it; two
  bathers who never go in; somebody fishing off the jetty in the
  evening; crabs on the wrack that go sideways when you come.
- **Greyline:** the rush at eight and at half past five; the sweeper;
  the delivery at the shutter; the window cleaner; the busker at the
  junction; and seven pigeons that all go up at once.
- **The Cubicle Mile:** the nine o'clock, from three cars to one door;
  the half-past-eight car in past the barrier; the smoker, three times;
  the courier at eleven who finds nobody in the hut; the cleaner at
  seven; the guard with a lantern at half past ten at night; a magpie
  on the muster sign; a fox across the car park after midnight.
- **Splitrock and the Flats** have three and two, on purpose: hikers
  down to the arch, somebody at the overlook, a figure on the far rim
  you cannot reach; and a walker who comes as far as THE HANDS, reads a
  post that points at everywhere but here, and turns back. Amos is on
  the clock too, every leg of his night.

## What I could not judge, and am not claiming

- Whether any of it is **fun**. That is the gate, and it is yours.
- Whether a routine READS as a routine, or as a figure that is
  sometimes there. The test is coming back at the same hour tomorrow.
- Whether the rain is right on a phone. It runs the ink; on a small
  screen that may be mud.
- Whether the storm's flash is too much for anybody.
- **The ear gate, still.** Fifteen new voices in `out/sound/` —
  thunder, a gust, the dog, the cows, the heron, the seals, the fox,
  the owl, the cat, the crabs, the pigeons, the thing under the water,
  a shutter, a car starting, a branch in the dark. Unheard, like the
  sixty-three before.
- **The feel gate, still.**
