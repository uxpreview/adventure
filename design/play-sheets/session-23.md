# PLAY SHEET — Session 23, THE INTERIORS

*Ten minutes: three doors, three rooms, and the three things the rooms
gave you to carry. The play gate is yours (`QUALITY-BAR.md` §2): no
tool in this repository can say whether walking into a house is a
pleasure, and this session did not run the gate. It hands you the
script.*

## 0. Val's kitchen. (`?hour=12`, 0:00)

Maple Court, the head of the court, the house with the porch. Walk up
the steps and **through the door** — it opens; it has never opened
before. The front of the house goes to pencil and the room is drawn
behind it: the boards, the rug, the range with the kettle on, a table
laid for one with the chair pulled out, the lamp, the hooks by the
door with one coat on them. She is at the range. **LOOK AT THE TABLE.**
Walk out and the house is a house.

*Ask:* did the roof coming off read as a drawing, or as a bug? Is the
camera too close, too far, or right? (It comes in four units on a
desktop and two on a phone, over about a second.) Come back at
`?hour=22`: the window is dark, the lamp and the range are lit, and
she is by the window with her back to the room.

## 1. Marget's house. (`?hour=22`, 2:30)

Brim, the back streets west of the square — a thatched house with a
door in the middle and a bunch of something dried over it. Go in. The
cloth is on the table, folded in four; the apron is on its hook; the
weights are on the shelf in the order she keeps them and the scale is
level. She is here. **LOOK AT THE TABLE.** Come back at noon and the
table is scrubbed and bare and she is at the square.

*Ask:* this is the sixteen hours a day nobody in the square has ever
seen her. Is that worth a door? Did you want to look at anything the
note did not name?

## 2. The loft. (`?hour=12`, 4:30)

Greyweather, through the barbican, and just inside the wall on the
right there is a low stone lean-to against the tower with a plank
door. Go in. A vat, a stool, and a rail with four pegs on it, three
bare. **LOOK AT THE RACK.** Then go to the moat pool: on the bank
there is a wet banner, wrung out. **PICK UP THE BANNER.** Now walk it
back to the loft. Try to run.

*Ask:* did you notice you could not run? Nothing says so. Hang it on
the peg. Read the rack again. (If you have put the king back on his
plinth, the loft reads differently and nothing is on the bank at all.)

## 3. The crate. (`?hour=12`, 7:00)

Brim, the market lane's west end, a crate in front of the terrace.
**PICK UP THE CRATE.** Carry it the length of the town to the square,
to Marget's stall. **SET IT DOWN BY THE STALL.** Then **LOOK IN THE
CRATE.**

*Ask:* it was light the whole way. Did you notice before the note told
you? Is that a joke, or is it the town?

## 4. The small bike. (`?hour=12`, 8:30)

Maple Court, the court's west side, a child's bike on its side on a
lawn. **STAND IT UP.** Then **WHEEL IT** — a shove at a time — east,
across the king's road, to the house with the car on the drive. It
leans on the wall and stays.

*Ask:* how many shoves? Did it feel like an errand or a chore?
(Fourteen or so, at a walk.)

## What moved, and what I could not judge

Three lands re-opened for the rooms — MAPLE COURT, BRIM, GREYWEATHER.
From outside every house is exactly the house it was: a room is drawn
up only as you go in, so nothing in a protected framing moved but the
two new fronts (Marget's house in the back streets, the loft inside
the wall) and one rock. `diff-sheets` has the numbers in `SESSIONS.md`.

- **Whether a room is worth walking into.** `check-verbs` §15 can say
  the door admits, the walls refuse, the front goes to pencil and the
  camera closes no faster than you walk. It cannot say whether you
  wanted to be in there.
- **The ear gate, still.** Three new voices (a latch, a crate set down
  on cobbles, wet cloth on a peg) in `out/sound/`, and indoors the rain
  and the wind come down by two thirds. Unheard, like the hundred and
  nine before.
