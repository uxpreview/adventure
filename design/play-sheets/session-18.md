# PLAY SHEET — Session 18, THE ROADS

*Ten minutes, on the roads, at two hours of the day. The play gate is
yours (`QUALITY-BAR.md` §2): no tool in this repository can say whether
any of this is fun, and this session did not run the gate. It hands
you the script — and, this time, a list.*

**The build:** the branch's Vercel preview,
[adventure-git-claude-next-session-t2pvsy-ryankm.vercel.app](https://adventure-git-claude-next-session-t2pvsy-ryankm.vercel.app).
Once merged it is [adventure.ryankm.com](https://adventure.ryankm.com).

**Your words this session answered:** *"It almost feels like a chore
going from one place to another."* And: *"There needs to be more
regions and activity to fill the gap."* So the session built the
roads: a tool that walks every one of them and fails on fifteen
seconds of nothing, fifteen roadside encounters on the clock, forty-five
districts, a bicycle, a paper plane, and the 8:15 every morning after
the ending.

**The address bar still works:** `?hour=6` for dawn, `?hour=15` for
the afternoon, `?hour=19.3` for dusk. Everything below says which.

## 0. THE LIST — the three worst silences, and a question

`tools/check-roads.mjs` walked every road at a walk, on a desktop and on
a phone, from noon, and asked at every point whether anything was in
frame or in earshot. **On eleven roads it found two silences of fifteen
seconds or more**, and the session put something at the halfway mark
of each:

| where | how long | what is there now |
|---|---|---|
| **the canyon trail, from the Downs' edge across the pan to the riverhead** (both rigs) | 22–24 s | a cairn with a rag on a stick, and a lizard on it that is gone before you are sure; and the dust devil now counts, because it crosses here |
| **Brack's round, the south-west arc** (phone only) | 20 s | the cutters' woodpile at the verge — four lengths, one on top |

**Walk both, at noon** (`?hour=12`). The canyon trail: stand at THE
HANDS in the Flats and walk north-east along the trail to where it
drops into the canyon. Brack's round: from THE OARS, walk the ring the
long way, anti-clockwise.

*Ask:* **was the tool right?** It measures whether SOMETHING is there;
you feel whether the walk is a chore. If these two walks still feel
empty with the cairn and the woodpile on them, the rule is too lenient
and the next session tightens it (what counts as *in frame* is written
at the top of the tool). If the rest of the roads feel like a chore
too — and the tool passed them — then the chore is not emptiness, it is
the walk itself, and the answer is the walk speed or the bicycle, and
that is a different session.

## 1. The king's road at ten, and at three. (`?hour=10`, 0:00)

Walk north from the crossroads into Brim. **On the king's road south
of the square there is a cart with a broken wheel and nobody near it**
— the wheel off, on the road beside it. Note the time. Then `?hour=14`:
somebody is bent over it. Then `?hour=15.3`: it is whole, and he is
pushing it home up the back streets. At four, the road is empty.

*Ask:* did you read the cart as a thing that happened, or as
furniture? Did coming back at a different hour and finding it changed
feel like the world had a day of its own — or like a trick?

## 2. Dawn on the coast road. (`?hour=6`, 2:00)

Start at the crossroads and walk WEST toward the sea. **A dog falls in
beside you** somewhere past the fair ground. Keep walking. It follows
you all the way to the beach. Now turn round and walk back east,
through the crossroads, toward the field: **it stops dead a little past
the signpost, and sits, and looks after you.** There is no border
there. Nothing says why.

*Ask:* you have met the goat and the Downs' dog, which stop at borders.
This one stops at a line that is not one. Did that read as the rule
told a third way, or as a bug? (It is the rule. The line is authored.
The point is that a player who knows the rule now wonders what the line
is, and the game will never say.)

## 3. The bicycle. (`?hour=12`, 4:00)

Go to Maple Court — south from the crossroads down the king's road,
past the bridge. At the mouth of the court, on the verge, **a bicycle**.
Walk up to it: GET ON. Ride. Ride down main street, ride round the
court, ride up to Val's fence and **press the key while you are moving:
the bell.** The cat sits up. Ride to the green after three and ring it
at the children: they stop and look at you. Ride north up the king's
road toward the Common **and it stops dead at the edge**, with you on
it. Stop, press the key: GET OFF. Walk across. Look back: the bicycle
is where you left it, and it is there tomorrow.

*Ask:* is the bell fun? Is it fun the fourth time? Did the bicycle at
the border read as the rule, or as a wall? Is it fast enough to be
worth getting on, or too fast for the camera? (The rig was built for a
walk; the bicycle is the fastest thing you have ever steered in this
world, and the feel gate has never been run on it.)

## 4. The paper plane. (`?hour=12`, 6:00)

Go to Splitrock, to THE OVERLOOK on the west rim (the trail in from the
Flats, then up the west side). On the rock: PICK UP THE PLANE. Walk to
the lip — east — and while you are still walking, press the key. **It
glides the whole cut** and comes down on the far rim, which the note
told you is about an hour. Go and get it (round the mouth, down the
floor, up the east bench). Throw it back. Set it down while standing
still and it is set down at your feet.

*Ask:* did the throw feel like a throw, or like a cutscene? Was the walk
to fetch it the joke, or the chore? (Session 11 wrote that the plane
must not shorten the walk round the mouth. It does not. Whether that is
right is yours.)

## 5. Dusk on the coast. (`?hour=19.3`, 8:00)

Longshore, the south sand, between the end of the promenade and the
boat resting. **A fire is lit, and nobody is at it yet.** Wait. At
twenty past eight two come down the promenade and sit at it. Out at the
tarn in the Penwood at the same hour, **rings on the water**, one every
ten seconds, never where you are looking, and a plop if you are close.
And in Shelter Cove after nine, **a light on the water that is not the
mark.**

*Ask:* the fire with nobody at it — did you wait, or walk on? Did the
rings frighten you? (Nothing will ever surface.) Is the light in the
cove a thing you want to know about, and would knowing spoil it?

## 6. The day after the ending. (only if you have finished, 9:30)

If the 8:15 has come for you once, come back the next morning at a
quarter past eight to any stop on the line. **It comes again.** Nobody
is on the platform. The doors stand open the same thirteen seconds.
Get on. It stops twelve times and ends in the car park, and you can
walk off into the day.

*Ask:* is a train with nobody waiting the right day after? Should the
people who left be gone from their lands? (That is the standing debt
Session 21 decides, and it is yours.)

## The encounters, if you have longer

Fifteen of `THE-STRANGERS` Part Three were built this session, on the
clock, each with an aftermath for whoever comes at the wrong hour:

- **Brim:** the broken cart (all morning; mended at three); somebody
  walking the other way down the king's road at dusk who does not look
  up.
- **The Common:** the dawn dog; two people carrying a ladder from the
  oaks round the well to the fair ground at half past nine and back
  after four — between the trips it leans at the fair ground.
- **Longshore:** a hat going east along the coast road three times a
  day, faster than you, that stops at the Common's border like
  everybody else and lies there; three people combing the tideline at
  low water, first light and before dark, spread out, silent; the fire.
- **The Wide Blue:** a gull on the sandbar's crest where it bends west
  that will not move — it opens its beak at you — so you go round; the
  light in the cove.
- **The Penwood:** a felled pine across the track's last diagonal with
  the saw left in it, all morning, and by three it is two lengths at
  the verge; the rings on the tarn.
- **The Downs:** at three, a funeral — four in file, slow, up the mill
  lane from the farm to the ford and back — and inside forty units of
  them the land's every sound stops; at half past four seven sheep on
  the east road walking west that step off the road as you reach each
  one and back on when you have gone.
- **The Flats:** in the middle of the day, from the canyon trail, a
  stand of palms out on the pan that you can see from sixty units and
  not from twenty-five. The oasis, from the wrong direction, and it is
  not there.
- **Maple Court:** a car on a drive on main street, from six until
  nine in the evening, with its lights on and its engine off, and
  nobody comes out to it.

Twelve of the twenty-eight already existed (the delivery, the
lamplighter, the braziers, the field working, the rockfall, the boots,
Amos, the sprinkler, the man in the rush, the four green lights,
Dennis at the board at eight, the car park's cars). One is Session
19's: Wick, halfway up the avenue at dawn, resting, because Wick has
no drawing yet and his wait is next session's.

## The districts

Every land now has three or four named districts. Walk across any land
and the small card comes up at each — THE SQUARE, THE BACK STREETS, THE
ORCHARD CLOSE, THE WOOD GATE in Brim; THE AVENUE, THE BAILEY, THE MOAT,
THE RIDGE at Greyweather; THE MOUTH, THE FLOOR, THE EAST BENCH in
Splitrock; and so on — and the map draws them inside every land you
have stood in.

*Ask:* is crossing a land three arrivals now, the way §7 wanted, or
three interruptions? Is the small card the right size on a phone?

## What I could not judge, and am not claiming

- Whether any of it is **fun**. That is the gate, and it is yours.
- **Whether the fifteen-second rule as measured is the rule you
  meant.** The tool counts a standee you can read, a person, an
  animal, a thing, a bridge, water beside the road, a placed sound, a
  silence that is a place, an event with a place. It does not count
  grass, trees, or a crowd drawn as a field, or the land-wide lark. It
  found the roads mostly alive. You found them a chore. One of those
  is the wrong measure, and §0 above is how to tell which.
- **The bicycle on the feel gate.** It is faster than anything the
  camera was tuned for. `check-camera` still passes on foot; nobody has
  checked it on a bicycle, and nobody can but you.
- **The ear gate, still.** Two new voices in `out/sound/` — the bell,
  and a paper plane coming down. Unheard, like the seventy-eight before.
- **Whether the encounters read as encounters**, or as more people.
  The test is coming back at the hour the cart is mended.

Everything above is measured where it can be (`tools/check-roads.mjs`,
`tools/check-verbs.mjs` section 9, `tools/check-fields.mjs`) and
photographed both ways (`tools/shoot-session18.mjs`). None of that is a
verdict.
