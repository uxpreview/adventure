# STORY CONCEPTS — three storylines for INKLANDS

*Written 2026-09-12 against the owner's phone notes in `PROMPT.md` §2b:
the story does not work, people will not understand or care, rethink it
from scratch, nothing built is sacred, and the ballpoint is the style and
never the subject. Three concepts, each with a bearing first (tone, who
you are, the reference whose whole shape to steal, the trailer in three
shots), then the cold open, the main line, the side quests, the choices,
the ending, and the cost. A recommendation at the end.*

---

## 0. Why the 8:15 did not work, and what all three fix

The 8:15 is a good sentence and a bad story. Four reasons, all fixable:

1. **The walker wants nothing.** Every game the owner named gives you a
   want in the first minute (a package to deliver, a farm to fix, a
   goose that wants to ruin a man's day). Ours is a messenger for other
   people's metaphors.
2. **The goal is an abstraction.** "Waiting" is a theme, not a goal. You
   cannot walk toward a theme. You can walk toward a river, a castle, or
   a missing person.
3. **The payoff is silent.** The turn was designed never to be said. A
   turn nobody says is a turn nobody gets.
4. **The first job is a reading exercise.** Read a signpost, cross a
   border, read a milestone, bring a word back. Four steps to learn that
   a number is a time.

Every concept below obeys six rules, and the rules are the fix:

- **A cold open you do not control.** Skyrim's cart, GTA's opening:
  carried through the world while someone talks, control arriving in
  stages (look, then walk, then act), a spectacle that dumps you at the
  bull with one obvious thing to do.
  (`PROMPT.md` §2b lists "a cart-ride prologue" among the rejected
  pitches. This reads that as a rejection of that pitch's story, not of
  the device the owner named as the reference. If the device itself is
  out, Concept 3's cart and Concept 2's horse become a walk-in at dawn
  with the same talker, and nothing else in either concept moves.)
- **A first job of three words** that is finished in two minutes and
  needs no compass, because what you follow is drawn on the ground.
- **Someone to care about** who has a voice and is in trouble by the
  end of the first minute.
- **A goal you can point at**: a source, a gate, a person.
- **Choices said out loud and drawn permanently**, New Vegas style, and
  an ending that reads every one of them back in one continuous shot.
- **A shareable frame.** One moment per concept a person screenshots
  and sends. That is the Webby test and it is stated for each.

Sizing assumption: a Webby or Awwwards juror plays thirty minutes, and a
shared link is played for ten. Each main line below is sized at two to
three hours with side content around it, not fifteen. Twelve lands, one
road, the river, the mounts, the twelve named people, the Vikings, the
aliens, the surfers, the barista, the sprint and the monsters are all
kept as material. What each concept throws away is listed.

## CONCEPT 1 — DOWNSTREAM

> **Twelve centuries share one river, and this morning it stopped.**

### The bearing

| | |
|---|---|
| **tone** | dry comedy that turns political, with one moment of awe |
| **who you are** | the boat person. You live on the river, the one thing besides the road that crosses every land. You have been past everywhere and in nowhere |
| **shape to steal** | *Fallout: New Vegas*: one prize, every faction wants it, you decide who gets it and the ending reads it back land by land. Crossed with *Chinatown*: somebody took the water, and it is played straight |
| **trailer, three shots** | 1. a rowboat drifting down a painted river through a castle's shadow, a walled town, a field of sheep, someone in the bow talking. 2. the water thins to gravel under the hull and the boat grounds, and a bull lifts its head. 3. a wall of ink-blue water coming down the whole sheet, camera craning up, past a mill and a fountain and a sprinkler and a coffee cart, and stopping dead at one land's border |

### The cold open

"Set out" puts you in the stern of a rowboat on the river, already
moving. **FENN**, a ferryman, is in the bow, facing you, talking: about
the fare, about how the river is the only honest road, about how it has
never once stopped. The lands go by on either side and he names them the
way a bus driver would. Stage one of control: the lens is yours (drag to
look), nothing else. Stage two, thirty seconds in: the current slows and
he tells you to take an oar (W). Stage three: the water goes light and
thin over gravel, the hull grinds, and the boat stops at THE RIVER BEND
on the Common with the river gone from under it. Fenn: "That's never
happened." Then the bull, from the field beside the bend, and one obvious
thing: **RUN.** Nell slams the gate. Fenn is left on the gravel with his
boat.

### The first ten minutes

Nell: "The river's never stopped. Not once. You came down it. What did
you do?" First job, three words: **FIND THE WATER.** The riverbed is a
drawn line in the ground that runs east across the sheet, and you follow
it. No compass, no map needed. On the way the world is already reacting:
the ford in the Downs is dry and the sheep are standing in it, the mill's
sails have stopped, Joan is at the race with a bucket. Ten minutes in you
reach the canyon mouth and the riverbed goes up into the tear, and the
canyon, which was dry, has water in it. Holt is at the top with his boat
the right way up. He built a dam. It worked.

That is Act I's turn, and it lands inside the first hour: the river did
not dry up. It was taken, by the one man in the world who had waited for
it the longest, and he is the most sympathetic person you have met.

### The main line

**ACT I — FIND THE WATER.** The bend to the dam. Teaches walk, run, talk,
the map (Nell pins the bend, the ford, the canyon), the horse (Joan lends
it because the ford is dry and the road is long), and the first choice:
Holt asks you not to tell anyone. You can promise or not, and the world
reads it back within a minute (Nell's next line depends on it).

**ACT II — TWELVE TAKES.** Every land takes a little of the river and
always has, and Holt's dam was only the take that tipped it. The moat
pool at Greyweather is a dye vat. Brim's fountain. The Common's well.
Maple Court's sprinklers. The tarn in the Penwood. The mill race in the
Downs. Amos's cistern in the Flats, filled from the oasis. The cut at
Longshore that keeps the harbour open. Greyline's hollow, a storm drain
that feeds the city's one fountain. The Cubicle Mile's atrium water
feature, installed last quarter, with a sprint run on it. And the Wide
Blue, where the current has stopped and the regatta is becalmed. Holt's
canyon is the twelfth.

The sluice at the dam will share the river, but only if there is enough
river to share, and there is only enough if the takes close. So Act II
is twelve negotiations, in any order, each a named job with a person, a
turn, and a choice card with a loser on both sides:

| land | the job | the choice, and its cost |
|---|---|---|
| GREYWEATHER | THE RED IN THE MOAT | close the vat and the banners go grey for good, or keep it and Wick keeps dyeing |
| BRIM | THE FOUNTAIN | turn it off and the square's crowd thins and Marget's stall loses its trade, or keep it |
| THE COMMON | THE WELL | Nell's well, the one you shout down. Dry, it does not answer |
| MAPLE COURT | THE SPRINKLER WAR | Val's lawns versus the whole road. Close them and the green browns, house by house |
| THE PENWOOD | THE STILL WATER | the tarn is a take nobody dares turn off. Brack says leave it. What is in it comes up if it drops |
| THE HARROW DOWNS | THE MILL RACE | the only honest take. Joan closes it herself if you ask, and the bread stops |
| THE BLEACH FLATS | THE CISTERN | Amos has the only stored water in the world and everyone downstream wants it. A siege, played as comedy |
| LONGSHORE | THE CUT | the surfers' wave is the river meeting the sea. No river, no wave, and Pye's harbour silts |
| THE WIDE BLUE | BECALMED | the Vikings cannot row out to the mark. They offer to row up the river and take the dam by force. You can let them |
| GREYLINE CITY | THE HOLLOW | the barista's cart has no water. Names called for orders nobody can make |
| THE CUBICLE MILE | THE WATER FEATURE | Dennis and the sprint. They will decommission it if you fill in the survey. The survey is the toy |
| SPLITROCK | THE MARKS ON THE WALL | Holt. Whether he opens the sluice at all is Act III |

Each closed take is drawn permanently: a dry fountain, a brown lawn, a
grey banner, a cart with no queue. The notebook keeps the list under one
heading, THE TAKES, with a count.

**ACT III — THE SLUICE.** Back to the dam with whatever you have. Three
doors, all visible before any is taken:

- **OPEN THE SLUICE.** The river comes down the sheet in one continuous
  shot, the camera craning up to follow it, and it flows exactly as far
  as the takes you closed allow. It stops dead at the border of the first
  land that kept its take, and stays there. That is the ending reading
  back every choice, physically, and nobody has to say a word.
- **BREAK THE DAM.** Everything back the way it was. The canyon dries.
  Holt turns his boat over and puts it back on the trestles. Every take
  you closed stays closed, because you closed them for nothing.
- **LEAVE IT.** The canyon keeps its river. Fenn's boat stays on the
  gravel. You can walk the world dry for as long as you like.

### Side quests

Strangers-shaped, met in one land and resolved in another, with a turn:

- **THE FARE.** Fenn wants paying, and the only currency in the world is
  a bucket of water. Where you get it is the joke. It ends with his boat
  on a hand cart, which is a mount.
- **THE RED WATER.** The moat pool's red runs downstream for two days
  after Wick dyes. Brim thinks it is an omen. Follow it up and it is
  laundry.
- **THE EIGHTH POT.** Pye's pots are on dry mud. He sets an eighth one
  further out anyway, on a bearing he has never rowed, and asks you to
  row it. The Wide Blue is still wet.
- **HOW DEEP.** Odd's line, already built. In a world arguing about water,
  the one man who wants to know how much there is.
- **THE RAINFALL TABLE.** Amos has a table ruled for rain with no
  entries. The sprint in the Cubicle Mile has a dashboard for the water
  feature with no data. Carry one to the other.
- **THE LONGSHIP.** The Vikings' raid on the dam, if you let them: a
  three-beat comedy where four hundred years of waiting for a wind ends
  with a portage.

Toys and collections kept: stone skimming (needs water, so it comes back
with the river, a reward you can feel), the horse, the bicycle, the boat
as a mount that opens up land by land as the river returns (Breath of the
Wild: traversal that changes the map), twelve stamps.

### Who you are, and why it holds

Fenn calls you "the one who was on it when it stopped", and so does
everyone else. Nobody accuses you and everyone blames you a little. The
want is simple: put the river back under your boat. What the story does
is make that want cost somebody every time.

### Keeps, throws

Keeps: the twelve lands, the river and its three bridges and the ford,
the boat, the twelve named people and their postures, the Vikings, the
sprint, Odd, the monsters (the tarn and the moat pool are both takes).
Throws: the 8:15 as spine (the stop stays as a place with a timetable and
a joke), the twelve waits, the border rule, Nell's fourth name.

### The Webby moment

The river coming back. One shot, the whole map, the wash flowing in and
stopping where the world made it stop. Different for every player. The
screenshot is the map afterwards with the river inked as far as it got.

### Risks and cost

- The river is painted into the wash and the shader reads it. Turning it
  on and off per segment is a mask on that paint, not a fluid sim, and
  the crane is the camera pillar's work anyway. Medium cost.
- Twelve negotiations can read as twelve fetches. The cure is the table
  above: every one has a loser, and the loser is drawn.
- The politics could go heavy. It stays deadpan or it stops being this
  game.

## CONCEPT 2 — THE KING'S ROAD

> **An old man rides into the Common saying he is the King of Greyweather,
> home from a war nobody remembers. Walk him home.**

### The bearing

| | |
|---|---|
| **tone** | warm road comedy with a companion, and the saddest thing in the world underneath it, never pointed at |
| **who you are** | nobody. The person who caught his horse. He calls you "squire" from the first line and never stops |
| **shape to steal** | *The Straight Story* (an old man's slow journey home), *Anastasia* (is he who he says?), and the companion games: *Ico*, *The Last Guardian*, Wheatley. One voice at your side for the whole game, reacting to everything |
| **trailer, three shots** | 1. a horse at full gallop down the king's road with two riders, the old one shouting over his shoulder, a suburb and a car park going by. 2. the same old man standing in Brim Square in front of a statue of himself, toppled, looking at it. 3. the castle gate, banners up, one man on the wall, and the old man below with his hand raised |

### The cold open

"Set out" and you are on the back of a horse behind an old man in a
cloak, and he is talking. He found you asleep in a ditch. He has been
riding since dawn and the road has got very strange: "There is a town of
glass on the road, squire. Nobody would stop." Stage one: look. The horse
takes the king's road at a gallop past Maple Court's porches and cars,
and it is played completely straight. Stage two: he tells you to hold on
(any key, and the camera follows the horse rather than the road). Then the
horse shies at the bull's field, throws you both, and bolts. You are on
your feet in long grass with an old man drawing a sword on a bull. One
obvious thing: **RUN.** Nell slams the gate. The old man is inside it
with the bull, and Nell has to open it again.

### The first ten minutes

He is **OSRIC**, and he says he is the king. He says it to Nell and Nell
laughs. First job, three words: **CATCH THE HORSE.** It teaches the
whistle (H), the map (the horse is a pin that moves), and the mount. Then
he points north, and the castle is on the skyline, which is Breath of
the Wild's opening: you can see it, go there. He walks beside you. He
talks about everything: the traffic, the hedge, the cart, the sunflowers,
and none of it is what he remembers.

Then Brim's south gate. The town lets you in and will not let him: a
guard's post, a standing rule about vagrants on the king's road, and
Teg's sealed complaint about the clock, which he has been waiting to hand
to the castle for years and now hands to a man who says he is the king.
Osric reads it with complete ceremony and does not comment. Then the
square, and the toppled king on his plinth, and he stands in front of it
for a long time. That is the first ten minutes' last beat and it is the
one people will screenshot.

### The main line

**ACT I — THE TOWN.** Catch the horse, get through Brim, see the statue.
Brim's clock has two hands that disagree, and he knows what hour it was
when he rode out, and he calls the market from the cross, which is what a
king is for. THE HOUR OF BRIM, re-hung on him. Marget opens her stall.
The first choice: whether you back him in front of the town. Nell hears
about it either way.

**ACT II — THE PROOFS.** Greyweather's gate is barred and Wick will not
open it. Wick knows him instantly, and says so, and still will not open,
because the old law says a king is a crown, a sceptre and the hour, and
the law is what Wick has instead of a decision. Three proofs, from five,
in any order, and every one is in a different century:

| proof | where | the episode |
|---|---|---|
| **THE CROWN** | the Wide Blue | the Vikings took it four hundred years ago and are the one land that recognises him. They roar. He cannot swim, and you row him out |
| **THE SCEPTRE** | Greyweather's ridge | the rooks took the bright thing off the plinth. It is on the ridge wall. He has to climb, and he is old |
| **THE HOUR** | Brim | the market called. Done in Act I if you backed him, a second visit if you did not |
| **THE NAME** | the Cubicle Mile | his name is on the timetable at the 8:15 stop, spelled the old way, and Dennis has been saying it wrong for thirty years. The sprint interviews him as a user and pins up a persona with his face |
| **THE HORSE** | the Harrow Downs | a king's horse is shod. Joan puts him to work for it, and it is the only place in the world he is happy |

Each land's episode is what that century thinks a king is. Maple Court
thinks he is in costume and invites him to a barbecue. Greyline does not
stop for him, and he stops at the junction beside the man who stands
still, which is FOUR SECONDS with a king in it: the city stops. The
Penwood at night, the pine-tick stopping, and he is afraid, and he is
brave for you. The Flats, the aliens, and he is not fazed at all: "We had
those."

**ACT III — THE GATE.** The proofs to Wick. The banners come down for the
first time in the world. The gate opens on an empty castle: the bailey,
the well, the hall with a chair in it. Osric at the threshold.

### The choice, and the ending

The last card is yours, not his, because you are the squire: **"Is this
your home, sire?"**

- **YES.** He goes in. The banners are down, Wick is relieved and sits
  down for the first time in the history of the world, and there is a
  king in an empty keep. The road reads it back: Brim's guard post
  salutes, Dennis says the name right, the persona comes off the wall.
- **NO.** You ride him back to the Downs, where Joan lays a second plate,
  and he works. The banners stay up. Wick stays on duty, forever, changing
  them, for a king who is three lands away eating bread. The road reads
  it back too: the statue stays toppled, and Maple Court's barbecue has
  a regular.

Both endings have a loser and both are drawn permanently. Nothing says
which was right. Osric is the king. The game says so, out loud, in Act
I. What it never says is whether that matters.

### Side quests

His small wants along the road, each a stranger with a turn:

- **A DECENT MEAL.** Brim's market, the Downs' bread, the barista's
  cart. He eats a wrap at the junction and pronounces it the finest thing
  since the war.
- **THE SWORD.** Sharpened in Brim by a man who has never seen one, using
  the surfers' wax. It is worse afterwards. He does not notice.
- **THE BATH.** The moat pool. Wick's dye. The king is red for two days
  and every land has an opinion.
- **THE PORTRAIT.** The sprint makes him a persona poster. He asks for it
  framed. It goes on the wall of the keep at the end, and it is the only
  picture in the castle.
- **THE STANDING COMPLAINT.** Teg's, already written. Osric nails it up
  beside the proclamation, and the procedure is followed exactly.
- **THE LIGHT ON THE PORCH.** Val's. He knew her street when it was a
  field. The gap in the hedge is cut so the chairs face his castle, and
  he sits in one.
- **THE TARN.** Brack's forty units. Osric walks straight through them,
  because he is the one person in the world older than the fear.

Toys and collections kept: the horse with two riders (the toy of the
game: he has opinions about your riding), the bicycle (he will not get on
it, then does), the office chair (he will), stone skimming, twelve
stamps, and the hats, because he loses his and tries every land's.

### Who you are, and why it holds

You want to get him home because he asked, and because he is funny, and
because by the Downs you have worked out that home is a thousand years
gone and he has not. The want is his. The care is yours. That is how
every companion game does it and it is why they are the ones people
remember.

### Keeps, throws

Keeps: everything drawn, all twelve named people with their jobs re-hung
on the king, the Vikings as the one land from his century, the sprint,
the toppled king, the fifth banner, the timetable's name. Throws: the
8:15 as spine, the twelve waits, the border rule for him (he is from
before the borders, and he is the only inhabitant who crosses, which is
the point). The 8:15 stop stays as a joke with his name on it.

### The Webby moment

The companion. Three hundred hand-lettered lines from a man who reacts
to a car, a sprinkler, an office chair, a Viking, and his own statue,
played straight. Twelve centuries on one landmass and nobody finds it
odd, except him. He is the witness the world never had, and the comedy
engine runs on that for the whole game.

### Risks and cost

- Companion AI: follow, mount, wait at doors, cross water on the boat,
  never lost. The highest engineering cost of the three. The horse is
  built; the second rider is not.
- Dialogue volume. Three hundred lines is a writing job and the lines
  are the game.
- The road is three hundred units long and the castle is visible from
  the start. The proofs are what make the walk a game, and they must be
  episodes with turns, not fetches. The table is the bar.

## CONCEPT 3 — WHAT TOOK TAM

> **Something took the carter in the Penwood, and the horse brought you
> out. Every land has a different name for it. Find Tam.**

### The bearing

| | |
|---|---|
| **tone** | frightening at night, funny by day, warm at the end. The world's monsters as the spine |
| **who you are** | Tam's passenger. He picked you up on the wood road at dusk and would not stop talking, and you liked him |
| **shape to steal** | *Over the Garden Wall* (two travellers, one strange land per episode, a Beast in the wood, played for comedy by day), *Stranger Things* season one (the missing friend, the search, the thing that is one thing seen in many places), and *Untitled Goose Game* for what you do with a lantern |
| **trailer, three shots** | 1. a cart on a forest road at dusk, a lantern swinging, a man on the seat talking, and behind him the pines going dark one by one. 2. the same cart running empty through Brim's night streets under the lamps, the horse wild, and you in the back. 3. the tarn, black, still, and a lantern floating on it, lit |

### The cold open

Skyrim's cart, exactly. "Set out" and you are in the back of **TAM**'s
cart on the wood road at dusk. He talks: about the load, about the bull
he is delivering to Nell, about the Penwood, about how something in here
ticks and stops when you stop. Stage one: look. The pines go dark behind
you. Stage two: he hands you the lantern (hold it, one key) and the light
is yours. Then the tick stops. The horse bolts. The lantern goes out. The
seat is empty. Stage three: the cart runs, and you have the lens and
nothing else while it takes the wood gate, Brim's dark streets under the
lamps, the south gate, and overturns in the Common at dawn. The load was
the bull. One obvious thing: **RUN.** Nell slams the gate.

### The first ten minutes

Nell: "That's Tam's cart. Where's Tam?" First job, three words: **FIND
TAM.** Things fell off the cart the whole run, and they are drawn on the
ground back the way it came: his hat at the crossroads, a wheel in
Brim's lane, the lantern at the wood gate. A trail you follow with your
eyes, no compass. Each is a pin as you find it. By daylight the Penwood
is quiet and the pines are only trees. The trail ends at the tarn, and
there is nothing in it, and Brack will not come within forty units to
say so.

The turn of the first ten minutes, on the tarn's shore: the lantern is
gone from the wood gate when you go back for it. Something came and took
it. In daylight.

### The main line

**ACT I — THE TRAIL.** The Common to the tarn, by day. Teaches walk,
run, talk, the map as a trail, the carry (the lantern), and night: the
first night falls with a shout while you are in the Penwood, and the
tick starts, and you learn that light stops it and lamps and doors are
safe. Nell, Brack and the lamplighter in Brim are the first three people
with something to say about it.

**ACT II — TWELVE NAMES FOR ONE THING.** Every land has a name for what
took Tam and a thing they do about it at night, and none of them has
compared notes. This is the twelve waits rewritten as twelve fears, and
fear is legible where waiting was not:

| land | its name for it | what the land does at night | the job |
|---|---|---|---|
| THE COMMON | the thing in the reeds | Nell shuts the gate | SIT UP WITH NELL |
| BRIM | the reason for the bell | the lamplighter's round, four lamps behind | THE LAMPLIGHTER'S ROUND |
| GREYWEATHER | the thing in the moat | Wick lights the braziers and tends nothing | THE RED WATER |
| MAPLE COURT | nothing, out loud | the porch lights, every one, all night | THE DARK HOUSE |
| THE PENWOOD | the tick | Brack's forty units | THE FORTY UNITS |
| THE HARROW DOWNS | a fox | Joan does not go out. The only land that is right | THE FOX |
| THE BLEACH FLATS | it came from the sky | the aliens' lights over the Pale. They are afraid of it too | THE LIGHTS OVER THE PAN |
| SPLITROCK | the echo that comes back wrong | Holt shuts the shed | THE ECHO |
| LONGSHORE | the cut lurker | the surfers' dawn patrol saw it. They have a name for it and a hand sign | DAWN PATROL |
| THE WIDE BLUE | a sea thing | the Vikings row out to it and roar. They cannot land, and it does not come | THE ROAR |
| GREYLINE CITY | nothing. The city does not stop for it | the barista's night shift, and the one name called that nobody has ever collected | THE NIGHT SHIFT |
| THE CUBICLE MILE | a risk | the muster point, the drill, a laminated procedure | THE DRILL |

Each job is a night job in that land with a turn, and each ends with one
thing the land lost: a lamp, a bell clapper, a porch bulb, a hat, a
helm, a bicycle bell, the sceptre off the plinth. The turn of Act II,
assembled from six of the twelve: it does not take people. **It takes
lights and bright things.** Every land is missing one. Tam went in after
the lantern.

**ACT III — THE TARN.** With enough of the world's lost things placed on
a map that only you hold, the lairs line up: the pine thing, the cut
lurker and the pier thing are one animal, and its way in is the tarn.
You row out with a lantern lit, and the tarn is not still, and the world
inverts: white ink on black paper, the blot palette that has been parked
since Session 1, a cave under all twelve lands with a hoard in it. Every
hat the monsters ever took. The buoy's clapper. Val's bulb. The sceptre.
Nell's cap. And Tam, alive, with his lantern, telling a very large, very
old, very shy animal about the bull.

### The choice, and the ending

Three doors at the hoard, all visible:

- **TAKE IT ALL BACK.** Every land gets its thing, and the ending walks
  the world by night with the lights coming back on one land at a time:
  the porch, the braziers, the bell that rings again, the lamplighter
  catching up. The animal has nothing left and does not come out again.
  The nights are quiet. The Vikings have nothing to roar at.
- **TAKE TAM.** The hoard stays. The lands stay as they are, one thing
  short each, and the nights stay frightening, and the thing is still
  down there with its collection, and you know.
- **BRING IT UP.** Tam's idea. It comes out into the Common at dawn and
  the world reads it back: the Vikings finally have something to land
  for, the sprint runs a session on it, Maple Court's porch lights go
  off because they are not needed, and Nell shuts the gate on it out of
  habit. It is the largest thing that has ever crossed a border.

### Side quests

- **THE LANTERN.** A carry that the thing follows at night. Dangle it
  and run. The game's Goose toy, and the way you learn it wants light
  before Act II tells you.
- **THE FIFTH LAMP.** Brim's lamplighter is four lamps behind every
  night and has been for years. Walk his round with him and find out
  why. It is the second-best jump scare in the game.
- **THE SECOND OAR.** Hallows's twelve oars, already written. The one
  boat that goes on the tarn needs two.
- **THE PORCH.** June's gate on the latch, already written, re-hung: she
  is not waiting for the man at the junction. She is waiting for the
  thing. She has seen it on the lawn.
- **THE MUSTER POINT.** The Cubicle Mile's drill, run at night, with
  Dennis reading the procedure by the shelter's light while the light
  goes out. Deadpan institutional horror.
- **THE HELM.** The Vikings' helm on the foreshore, already written.
  Thrown at the thing. They want it back.
- **THE FOX.** Joan's. There is a fox. It is just a fox. The only land

### Who you are, and why it holds

You want Tam back because he was kind to you for ten minutes in the
dark, and that is enough. Everyone else wants their thing back and does
not say so. The animal wants the lights because it is dark where it
lives. Nobody in this story is wrong, and one of them is a monster.

### Keeps, throws

Keeps: the three monsters (as one), the tarn, the moat pool, the Pale,
night falling with a shout, lamps and doors as safety, every drawn land,
the twelve named people with their postures. The blot palette comes off
the shelf. Throws: the 8:15 as spine, the twelve waits (rewritten as the
twelve names), the border rule (the thing crosses, and at the end so does
Tam's cart).

### The Webby moment

The inverted world. One press and the page goes black and the ink goes
white, and the cave under twelve lands is the best-drawn thing in the
game. And before that, the cold open: an empty cart running through a
walled town at night under hand-drawn lamps, which is the trailer.

### Risks and cost

- Horror in a sketchbook can push the cosy audience off. The cure is
  the day: every land is funny in daylight, and the night is forty
  minutes long at most.
- The cave is a new land and the blot palette is unbuilt. Medium cost.
- The three monsters must become one design, and it must be seen whole
  exactly once, at the end. If it is seen earlier it is a goat.

## Recommendation

**THE KING'S ROAD**, and the reason is the owner's own diagnosis: people
will not understand or care. Understanding is fixed by any of the three
(a goal you can point at, a first job of three words). Caring is fixed
by a person, and a person who talks beside you for the whole game is the
strongest instrument a game has for it, and the cheapest in this one,
because hand-lettered speech, the horse, the twelve named people and
their jobs are all built. It is also the only concept where the world's
central fact, twelve centuries on one landmass and nobody finding it
odd, finally has someone to react to it. That is a comedy engine that
never runs out, and it is the thing an Awwwards juror will quote.

The honest cost is the companion: follow, mount, wait, cross water. It
is the highest engineering line in the table and it has to be solid
before anything else is written, because a companion who gets stuck in
a hedge is worse than no companion.

If the build risk is the deciding factor, **DOWNSTREAM** is the pick:
the lowest engineering cost, the strongest ending spectacle, and New
Vegas's choice structure whole. **WHAT TOOK TAM** has the best cold open
and the best single frame of the three, and its night jobs and the
lantern toy would survive as side content inside either of the others.

## Three questions for the owner, before one is picked

1. Is a companion who talks for the whole game what you want at your
   side, or do you want the world quiet around one walker?
2. How frightening is this game allowed to be at night?
3. Two to three hours for the main line, with the world open around it.
   Is that the size, or is fifteen still the target?
