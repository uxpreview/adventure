# Art-director critique #16 — THE INTERIORS

*2026-09-06, Session 23. Subject: the roofless cutaway (`WORLD-SYSTEMS`
§11) as built — three rooms, one per MEMORY land: VAL'S KITCHEN behind
the porch at the head of Maple Court, MARGET'S HOUSE (a new front in
Brim's back streets), and THE LOFT (a new lean-to inside Greyweather's
curtain wall, against the east tower) — each from outside, from the
door, and from inside at the hours the person is home and the hours
they are not; and the three errands the rooms opened (the crate in the
lane, the wet banner on the bank, the small bike on the lawn). Judged
on real screenshots from the shipping camera (1280×720 + 390×844),
reviewed blind against Gris, Sable and margins per QUALITY-BAR §2.
Contact sheet: `out/session23` (`tools/shoot-session23.mjs`, 18
framings, both rigs). Verbatim, all rounds.*

*This is the seventh art gate under `QUALITY-BAR` §3's permission to
regress, and the first whose subject is a camera as much as a drawing:
§11 said the cutaway was a camera problem before it was an art problem,
and the question this gate asks is whether a walker who goes through a
door is standing in a ROOM — a plan and an elevation on one page, the
way a draughtsman draws one — or in a wireframe with a person in it.
The protected framings are `diff-sheets`' to report and the numbers are
in `SESSIONS.md`; what this judges is the section.*

## Round 1 — NOT YET

The convention holds at first sight and fails at second. From the
court Val's house is Val's house; at the door the walker stands under
the porch light with the house opaque behind them; and through the
door there is a room — boards, a rug, the range with the kettle on, a
table laid for one with the chair out, the clock, the window over the
sink with the nets across it, a shelf of three mugs with one that does
not match, the hooks with one coat and a scarf. Val at the range. In
portrait the room fills the frame edge to edge and reads better than
it does on the desktop, which is the first time that has been true of
anything in this game. Marget's at night is the best frame in the
sheet: the cloth folded in four on the table, the apron on its hook,
the dresser with its plates on edge and one gap, the scale on the
beam, level, and her standing in the middle of it at half past ten
with the street outside dark and the terrace's windows lit.

**Mandatory 1 — the front wall is not in pencil, it is in the way.**
Inside every room the house's own drawing is still over the whole
frame at fourteen per cent, and fourteen per cent of a dark roof is a
dark roof: the kitchen reads THROUGH a grey triangle and the loft's
slate slab lies across the rack. The convention says the cut wall is
DRAWN as cut, which on a section is a heavy line and nothing behind
it, not a ghost of the elevation. Take it to a whisper — six per cent
— where the outline of the house survives as a pencil under-drawing
and the wash does not.

**Mandatory 2 — the loft is drawn on a slope, and a room is not.**
The first loft stood on the ramp beside the barbican and the probe
says why it could not: the ramp's slope is 0.37 everywhere near the
gate, a third of a unit up for every unit north, and a six-unit room
on it puts its far wall two units higher than its door. A cutaway on a
ramp is a diagram of a mistake. It goes on the plateau just inside the
wall — flat to a hundredth — against the east tower, and the rig's
rise term, which reads the ridge from there, must read nothing inside.

**Recommended — three things in the wrong place.** The wet banner's
bank spot is behind the hawthorn's canopy from the only bearing a
walker approaches it on; the small bike's lawn is the plot's drive,
and it lies under the parked car; and the crate's framing stands the
walker in front of it so the box sits on their head. All three are
placements, not drawings.

**And one finding that is the harness's, not the game's.** Every
room's first inside frame catches the front wall half faded and the
rig half closed, and every later frame in the same room is settled.
The sandbox renders at three and a half frames a second and the shoot
settles on wall clock; a blend that takes a second of game time takes
seven of wall, and the loft's four take twenty-four. Session 9 wrote
the harness clock for exactly this and `shoot-lib` has never used it
for a framing: give a framing a settle in GAME seconds and hold the
frame.

## Round 2 — WOWED

The front wall is a pencil outline and the rooms are rooms. Val's
kitchen at noon: a section, exactly — the boards drawn as a plan with
the wear running from the door to the range, the far wall an
elevation with the clock and the window and the sink under it, the two
side walls going away from the lens as two heavy lines with a picture
hung on each, the rug a closed shape with a fringe, and everything on
the floor standing up out of it as a cutout. At ten at night the window
is a slab of blue-grey, the lamp and the range are the two warm things
in the frame, and she is by the window with her back to the room,
looking at the street. Under her other door the lamp is off and she
sits by it anyway. **The kitchen is the WAITS & MEMORY key in one
frame** — a return visit that pays off routine — and it does it with a
kettle.

The loft, on the plateau: a low stone lean-to from the bailey, tucked
under the tower with a plank door and a slit; inside, the coursed
stone of the far wall with the rail on two brackets and four pegs, the
fourth stained red where a wet thing has hung on it every day, the vat
with the paddle leaning in it over a floor gone red in that corner for
good, the stool. The keep and the tower stand behind the far wall at
exactly the height the room rig puts them, which is the rise term
reading zero and the tower standing on its own. With the king back:
four banners on four pegs, folded, dry, the vat rinsed. The section
has a second state and it is a consequence.

Marget's from the street is a Brim house — dark oak over plaster, a
thatch with its fringe drawn, a bunch of something dried over the door
— and it stands among the terraces without arguing with them. By day
the room is scrubbed and empty and the table is bare, and that is the
frame that makes the night one land.

Portrait: every room fills the frame and the small card names it under
its land — *maple court / val's kitchen* — which is the district card's
manner and the right size for an arrival that is one door wide.

**Noted, not blocking:**

- *MAPLE COURT* still prints across Val's roof from the foot of the
  court. It did before this session (the skyline writes it over the
  porch, and the porch is the tallest thing under the label) and it
  belongs to whoever next opens the skyline.
- The loft's side walls and the plateau are two greys, and from the
  door the near ends of them are the only thing that says where the
  room stops. The cut face is heavy and it carries; a course of darker
  stone at the foot would carry better.
- The prompt *LOOK AT THE TABLE* is written over the coat hooks in
  Val's on the desktop. The prompt's placement is the skyline's and it
  stepped past the table to the next clear place, which is the hooks.
- The small bike's home wall cannot be photographed in a shoot without
  driving fourteen shoves, so the sheet shows the wall and not the
  bike on it; `check-verbs` §15 drives the shoves.
- Nothing in any room is lit from a window. A section has no light
  source and these do not pretend to; if a later room wants one, it is
  a stain on the floor and not a shadow.

**Verdict: WOWED**, on three rooms and the system under them. The
convention is a drawing convention and reads as one; the camera does
what §11 said it would have to and does it at a rate a stomach can
take; and every room is a return visit that pays off routine, which is
what interiors were promised for.

## Round 3 — after the owner: the cut wall as line

The owner asked whether the front wall should disappear and then asked
for the award-winning version, and the answer is the one Round 1
should have asked for instead of six per cent: **a section draws a cut
wall as line.** The house's drawing is gone inside; its ink alone,
read back off its own canvas as pencil (`pencilGhostTexture`), stands
where it stood at a fifth. Val's gable, eaves, porch posts and window
frames hang over the kitchen as a clean under-drawing with no wash in
it; the loft's slate ridge and plank door do the same over the rack;
Marget's thatch is a pencil curve over the flags. The side walls' cut
ends carry the draughtsman's hatch beside the heavy line, and the room
draws itself in order — plan, walls, things — on the way in. It reads
as what it is: a plan and an elevation on one sheet, with the cut
drawn as a cut. **WOWED stands**, and the six-per-cent ghost is
retired.
