# Critique 3 — THE PAPER HAS A SHAPE

*Session 4, 2026-08-28. The art-director gate per `QUALITY-BAR.md` §2,
run on real screenshots from the running game, **desktop (1280×720) and
portrait (390×844)**, which is the first session where portrait is a
gated viewport rather than a courtesy check.*

**Under review:** terrain elevation and the shading that draws it; the
redesigned camera; and the four lands that already hold the bar — THE
COMMON, the Brim south face, the Brim interior, CASTLE GREYWEATHER —
re-audited on the new ground, with Greyweather moved onto a real ridge.

**Standing brief:** *rejects anything that looks procedural,
placeholder, or like a tech demo wearing a style. Blind against Gris,
Sable, and margins itself. The bar: they cannot tell which world had an
art budget.* A WOWED land that elevation regresses is this session's
problem to fix, not to note.

---

## Round 1 — NOT YET

> "You have built a dune sea and told me it is paper.
>
> Look at the crease shot. That is an airbrush. Two enormous smooth
> swells, a soft brown stain sprayed across the near one, a horizon made
> of a sine wave. There is not one mark on that hillside that a hand
> made. Your whole moat — the thing nobody else can copy — is that every
> silhouette in this game is ballpoint on paper, and you have just put
> two hundred thousand triangles of *rendered* ground underneath it. The
> line work now looks like stickers on a landscape.
>
> Second: **the river is climbing a hill.** It goes up and over the far
> ridge, in blue, in the middle of the frame. I do not need to say more
> about that one.
>
> Third: you have confused amplitude with scale. Your note says 'low
> amplitude, nought to twelve.' Twelve units is nothing on a sheet seven
> hundred and sixty across — and it is a MOUNTAIN in a frame fifty-five
> units wide, which is the only frame the player ever gets. A five-unit
> swell over ninety units is a hill. Cockle is something you feel
> underfoot. You made it something you climb.
>
> Fourth: the world's rim. The curl is a sixty-unit ramp. Paper does not
> ramp. Paper lifts in the last handspan and then it ENDS, and the
> reason to walk to the edge of your world is to stand on that lifted
> edge and see the desk under it. And your geometry falls away sixteen
> units before your paint does, so there is a strip of grass hanging in
> mid-air off the side of the page.
>
> Greyweather I will not even score: the wall is a staircase of
> stand-ups at nine different heights and the keep is nowhere."

**Mandatory:**
1. Cut the cockle to texture, not landform.
2. Water gets beds. Nothing that is blue may go uphill, ever.
3. Draw the fold instead of shading it.
4. The curl is a lip, not a ramp; and the page's edge must be where the
   page's paint stops.

## Round 2 — NOT YET

> "The river behaves. Thank you.
>
> But now every slope in the world has corduroy on it. Your 'hatching'
> is a three-unit sine wave in world space, which on a hillside seen at
> a grazing angle stretches into drapery — it looks like a curtain, or
> like a landslide, and it is on ground with a gradient of a fifth,
> which is not a slope at all. A pen does not shade a gentle rise. It
> draws its EDGE.
>
> And that edge is missing. Your fold has no line down the bottom of it.
> That single dark line is the entire difference between a crease and a
> dent, and your cupping term is divided by three and a bit so it never
> reaches the threshold that would draw it.
>
> The tear is a sand gully. If the page is torn, show me what is under
> the page — that is the ONLY reason to tear a page."

**Mandatory:**
1. Hatching for cliffs only, finer, broken, and it stops with distance.
2. The crease gets its line.
3. The tear shows the desk through it.

## Round 3 — NOT YET

> "Better. The crease reads. The tear has a bottom you can see down into
> and it is worth walking to.
>
> Greyweather now stacks — barbican, then curtain wall, then keep, each
> clearing the one in front, which is the shot Session 3 had to win with
> texture width and now wins with GROUND. That is the session justified.
>
> Two things stop it.
>
> **The camera has walked backwards through a wall.** When the ground
> ahead rises your camera retreats — correct, distance is how you reveal
> a hill, not pitch — but it retreated twenty-five units and ended up on
> the far side of Brim's own north wall, and now I am watching the whole
> castle through the back of a town wall. Your fade rule handles the
> arch and not the wall.
>
> **And the walker is falling out of the bottom of the frame.** You
> raised the aim to open the ceiling and forgot the subject."

**Mandatory:**
1. A wall the walker has already passed must let go. The camera only
   ever looks north; anything behind the walker can only obstruct.
2. Re-solve the framing so the walker sits about four-fifths down and
   the keep about a third from the top, and make the avenue and the
   town stop crowding each other.

## Round 4 — NOT YET

> "The stack is right and the walker is in the frame.
>
> But your curtain wall is floating on the skyline, and I can tell you
> exactly why: **your light is in front of the page.** Every slope that
> faces the camera faces the lamp, so every hillside the player is
> actually looking at comes back paler than flat ground. The castle
> scarp — the whole point of this session — is LIT. It reads as haze.
> Put the lamp behind the page where it belongs and the face you are
> looking at is the shaded one, which is how anybody has ever drawn a
> hill.
>
> And your hatching gives up at sixty units, which is roughly where a
> cliff starts being a cliff."

**Mandatory:**
1. Light from behind and left. Shade what faces the camera.
2. Hatching carries to distance, with the stroke pitch opening out so it
   stays the same size on the page.

## Round 5 — NOT YET

> "The scarp is a cliff now — I can see the stone in it, and the east
> face of that ridge is the best thing in the session.
>
> Remaining, and these are small:
> the wall still sits on the skyline with a straight line for a foot and
> nothing under it; the bailey is a bald plateau; the four crag
> stand-ups are still there, standing in FRONT of the ridge they used to
> stand in for, and one of them is parked in the middle of the moat-pool
> shot; and inside the bailey the camera stands exactly where the
> curtain wall is, which at your ten-per-cent fade paints a grey
> rectangle across the whole frame."

**Mandatory:**
1. The crags go. The ridge is real; its toe is fallen stone.
2. Something at the wall's foot.
3. Floor the bailey.
4. Fades go to zero, not to a tenth.

## Round 6 — NOT YET

> "The scarp is stone and the ridge is real. Two things and I am done.
>
> **Your hatching runs along the contours.** On a cliff face seen in
> perspective that squashes into strata, and the west scarp reads as a
> smudge with stripes in it. A hand drawing a cliff draws DOWN it. You
> have the gradient at every vertex — use it, and the strokes will run
> down any cliff whichever way it faces.
>
> And a fold you are standing in still has no line, because you set your
> threshold so high that the only thing that qualifies is a wall. A
> crease is legible because the pen went down the bottom of it once."

**Mandatory:**
1. Hatch down the fall line, at a pitch that keeps the stroke the same
   size on the page at every distance.
2. The crease gets a narrow strong line over a wide weak settling.

## Round 7 — **WOWED**

> "Yes.
>
> The thing I want to say first is the thing I did not expect to be able
> to say: **the ground is drawn.** Stand in the fold on the east road
> and there is a line down the bottom of it that a hand put there, and
> the walls of it are shaded the way a page is shaded, and none of it is
> an airbrush. Walk to SPLITROCK and the page is torn and you can see
> the desk through the tear, which is the single best image this project
> has produced. Walk to the far east margin and the paper LIFTS, and
> ends, and there is wood under it. Every one of those is the metaphor
> paying rent. Nobody else's open world can do them, because nobody
> else's open world is a piece of paper.
>
> **Greyweather is the session.** Session 3 won the skyline contest with
> texture width and a note in the handoff saying not to make anything
> taller. This one wins it with GROUND: the banner avenue climbs, the
> barbican sits low on the ramp, the curtain wall stands on the brow it
> found by asking the page where the brow was, and the keep is on the
> plateau above all of it. Three beats, each clearing the one in front,
> and the reason it works is that they are at three different heights
> because the world has heights now. The four crag stand-ups that used
> to *represent* that ridge are gone, and what is left at its foot is
> fallen stone, which is what is actually at the foot of a ridge. And
> the scarp refuses you everywhere except the avenue, so the way in is
> the way in because the paper says so and not because a designer put a
> wall there.
>
> **Portrait is the surprise.** I have been reviewing it under protest
> and this is the first sheet where it is the better viewport. The
> castle approach in portrait is a vertical stack — walker, road,
> barbican, keep — and it is a stronger composition than the landscape
> one. The poster works: the lettering owns the top, the whole vista is
> in the middle, and SET OUT is at the bottom on its own scrap of paper
> where a thumb already is. That is not a port. That is a design.
>
> The four protected lands are intact and two of them are better. THE
> COMMON's road now climbs toward Brim, which is what its poster was
> always pretending. Brim Square is untouched, correctly — a market
> square is flat and you did not go looking for a hill to put it on.
>
> WOWED. Go and build the coast on this."

**Also noted, not blocking, for whoever takes them:**
- The bailey is still a large flat yard. Cobbling it helped; it wants
  one more authored thing in it when the story arrives (Session 7).
- SPLITROCK's cut is real ground now but its mesas are Session 1 scatter
  standing on it. That land is Session 9's.
- The beach/castle wash border reads as a hard diagonal on the west
  approach. A wash-field problem, not an elevation one.
- The desk seen past the curled rim is correct but flat. When time of
  day arrives (WORLD-SYSTEMS §7) the lamp will give it something to do.

| | |

*Frame rate: the sandbox has no GPU, so `tools/shoot-fps.mjs` reports a
software rasteriser's milliseconds and no honest fps figure was
obtainable here. What can be said truthfully is the budget: elevation
added one static mesh and no per-frame CPU work, no new draw call per
prop, and no new texture fetch in the terrain shader's common path. The
first device with a real GPU in front of it settles the rest.*
