## Q1 · Does Act I teach its three facts without stating them?

*everybody is waiting · nobody can leave · you can*

**Fact one still lands, and it lands sooner.** `critique-story-2` gave
this to Nell's straighten, Marget's empty cloth and Wick's rolled
banner. Session 16 puts the first instance inside the first minute:
Nell is at a gate with her back to you, and the moment she does
anything at all it is because a bull is coming — she shuts a gate on
it — and then she leans on the gate again. That is a woman who has
been at this gate a while, and nothing says so.

**Facts two and three have a second teacher now, and it does not
depend on the road.** MANDATORY 1 asked for a rule rather than a beat:
*anybody the walker is travelling near stops at their own border, on
any road out of any land.* `src/world/company.ts` is that rule, and the
goat is its first instance: it falls in beside you on the Common, it
follows you west, north, east or south, and it stops two units inside
the Common's rect on whichever road you take. `tools/check-verbs.mjs`
asserts the stop on two roads — the Brim gate and the east edge — with
the walker forty units past it on each. I.7's repetition is a matter of
time now rather than of route, exactly as the finding put it.

**And the bull is the same rule from the other side, in the first ten
seconds.** It stops at the hedge. It is not a companion, but it is a
thing that comes at you as hard as anything in this world will and
cannot cross a line you just crossed, and the player sees that line
held before they have read a single note. A rule shown twice in the
first minute — once by a thing that would hurt you, once by a thing that
loves you — is taught.

**What the read cannot do:** say whether the goat's stop READS as a
rule or as a goat losing interest. The drawing is the Penwood's, the
pose at the border is *stopped and looking back*, and the sound is a
bleat. Session 7's gotcha applies (*a silent system still has to be
visible*): there are three signals — the pose, the bleat, the distance
opening — and the play gate decides whether three is enough.

## Q2 · What does the Common believe?

The prompt's question, and the honest answer from a reader who has
played only the first hour: **the Common believes that every road
starts here and none of them is taken.**

The evidence a first-hour player has, none of it stated: a signpost
with four names and one of them a time; a woman at a gate who has been
there a while, with a cart behind her that is *almost* loaded; a goat
that will go anywhere with you until the edge; four things on the
horizon and a road to each. `THE-WAITS` §13 gives the Common's piece as
*one of the four roads is not a road*, and a reader who has read the
signpost and the field gate's note has both halves of that — the fourth
name that is a time, and the cart that has waited on it — without
either being said. They do not yet know they are the only one who can
take any of the roads; they know the goat cannot. That is the correct
amount to know at the end of hour one.

**What a first-hour player cannot say**, correctly: why Nell has never
left. The note says three of the four names she could go to tomorrow
and stops. The fourth name is the far corner of the sheet, and the
Common is the first land and resolves from the furthest, on purpose
(§9's last line). The turn is not said. Rule 5 holds.

## Q3 · Does the opening say the turn, now that it is allowed to be loud?

`THE-FUN-PASS` §2.5 relaxed *the world never explains itself* for
wayfinding and kept it absolute for the turn and the ending. Checked
against every new word in the first hour:

- the region card says a district's name — a name, not an instruction;
- the fair's board says THE FAIR and NEXT — a sign saying what it is
  for, which §2.5 licenses, and the joke is that it says nothing else;
- Nell's note says which way the cart is going *depends on the fourth
  name* — an invitation, and the loudest thing in the hour. It does not
  say the name is a time, that she cannot leave, or that she could
  have. §9's three *never says* hold;
- the card, with the name: *it was only ever waiting on which way it
  was going* — the wait's own turn, said to a player who has walked to
  the far corner and back for it. That is Act II's payoff, not Act I's
  statement, and it is offered once.

Nothing in the hour says the rule. The bull and the goat show it.

## Q4 · Does the first hour work if the player does it out of order?

A player who walks straight through the gate and away meets the goat
anyway; a player who never meets Nell meets the bull, which is at the
spawn and not optional. A player who wakes and stands still gets
charged, balked at, snorted at, and left alone — a bull that loses
interest — which is funny and teaches nothing worse than *it will not
touch you*. A player who goes over the stile back into the field gets
chased again. There is no order in which the first hour fails to show
the border rule at least once; the only order in which it shows it
zero times is standing in the long grass forever, and the bull comes
to you there.

## Q5 · Is the fright a fright, or a fail state in fancy dress?

Adversarially: a bull that never touches you is a bull the player
learns to ignore, and the third time it balks in their face the fright
is a joke. **That is the design and it is the correct one.** §2.3's
threat without a villain is fear as *a sound, a distance and a thing
that moved*; the first charge is ten seconds of not knowing the rule,
and every charge after is Goat Simulator. The Common is WAITS & THE
UNSEEN and is meant to be frightening once; it is the Downs and the
deep pines that get to keep the fear. Nothing counts the balks, nothing
punishes standing still, and nobody says the bull will not touch you.

**The one thing the owner has to play:** whether ten seconds is ten
seconds. On the harness the charge closes to two strides in a second
and the gate is shut at 6.6; on a phone with a thumb finding the run
for the first time it may be fifteen, and fifteen seconds of a bull at
your heels may be one fright too long. The play sheet asks.
