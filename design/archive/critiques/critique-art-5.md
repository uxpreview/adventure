# Critique 5 — TRAVERSAL & TIME

*Session 6, 2026-08-29. The art-director gate per `QUALITY-BAR.md` §2,
run on real screenshots from the running game, desktop (1280×720) and
portrait (390×844).*

**Under review:** a systems session, so the contact sheet is the whole
build — `shoot-traversal` (the session's own forty-one framings),
`shoot-first-minute`, `shoot-oldworld`, `shoot-coast` and `shoot-shape`,
**each of the protected sheets shot at two hours of the day**, because
the day cycle is not done until dusk is as good as noon.

**Standing brief:** *rejects anything that looks procedural, placeholder,
or like a tech demo wearing a style. Blind against Gris, Sable, and
margins itself. The bar: they cannot tell which world had an art budget.*

---

## Round 1 — NOT YET

> "Two of your four items are not built. One of them is a filter. And
> the last one is a bathtub.
>
> **The day cycle is a SEPIA FILTER.** Half past seven in the evening
> and I am looking at a monochrome orange page: the greens went brown,
> the greys went brown, the ballpoint went brown. You have graded the
> whole frame by the colour of the light at full strength, which is the
> single most common way a day cycle fails, and you have failed it
> exactly. Understand what you have done here — you have taken the one
> thing this project cannot compromise, *the line work is ballpoint*,
> and made it optional after teatime. There is no version of this that
> is acceptable at any strength.
>
> **The rowboat is a brown bathtub.** It is the most saturated object in
> any frame it appears in, on a sheet where everything else is a pale
> wash under a pen. And you have parked it in the middle of THE COMMON,
> in the middle of my contact sheet, because your harness left the
> player aboard and the boat followed them across the world. I am
> looking at Session 2's protected composition with a brown wedge in
> the foreground.
>
> **And the sprint does not exist.** You have three frames labelled
> STILL, WALKED and RUN and they are the same frame. Four dots each.
> Whatever you have built, nobody on this earth would notice it, and
> your session brief for it is one sentence — *your speed is legible in
> the marks you leave behind you*. It is not legible. It is not there."

**Mandatory:**
1. The ink is ballpoint at every hour of the day.
2. The boat is a drawing on this sheet, in this sheet's technique.
3. The prints must say the speed, or the item is not built.
4. Get the boat out of THE COMMON.

## Round 2 — NOT YET

> "Better. The page keeps its washes now and the pen is a pen. So let me
> tell you what I can see instead.
>
> **The haze is a TANGERINE SLAB.** You moved the hour's colour to the
> horizon, which is right, and then you put all of it there. Six in the
> evening and there is a flat field of saturated orange across the top
> third of every frame with the town cut out against it like a poster.
> The sheet is PAPER. A page under evening light goes cream-gold. It
> does not go to a photograph of a sunset, and you have spent five
> sessions refusing photographic ideas.
>
> **Your rowboat is a gondola.** Long, shallow, pointed at both ends,
> and the walker is standing ON it. Look at the frame: a person balanced
> on a gunwale, full height, legs visible through the hull. A dinghy is
> short and DEEP and the freeboard is most of what you see of it — the
> freeboard is what hides the legs of whoever is in it. And they are
> doing a walk cycle. In a boat. Jogging on the spot.
>
> **And now Brim.** You have lit the windows and I want to like it, but
> they are floating. Warm rectangles over roofs, over party walls, over
> nothing. You have generated a second drawing and hung it in front of
> the first and asked it to guess where the first one put its windows.
>
> One thing I will grant you: THE WIDE BLUE at dusk with the sandbar
> going gold. That is a real image."

**Mandatory:**
1. The haze warms; it does not saturate.
2. A dinghy, not a gondola — and nobody walks in a boat.
3. A lit window is in the window.

---

## Round 3 — NOT YET

*(This round was found by instrument rather than by eye, and it is worth
recording as an art-direction note because the eye could not have found
it. The gate asked one question — "prove the road carries" — and the
answer was a number.)*

> "Show me the carry. Not a screenshot of somebody standing on a road:
> a measurement.
>
> Aimed ten degrees off the king's road, at a run, the walker travels
> **ten degrees off the king's road.** Your feature deflects a walk by
> half a degree. You wrote three hundred words of comment about a
> steering system that is, in practice, switched off — and then you
> fixed the sign and it steered people INTO the verge: aimed ten
> degrees off, travelling sixteen.
>
> This is the one item in the session nobody can judge by looking, and
> that is exactly why it is the one you have to instrument. 'It has to
> be felt, not fought' is not a thing you can eyeball. Put a number on
> it, and put the number in the spec, or the next session will tune it
> by feel and undo you."

**Mandatory:**
1. Measure the carry, at several angles, on the road and off it.
2. Author the number. Write it down.

---

## Round 4 — WOWED

> "Now it is a session.
>
> **The day.** Eight in the morning to four in the afternoon is
> bit-for-bit the page four lands earned their verdicts on — not
> 'close', identical, because you made the neutral tint pure white and
> the neutral haze the paper's own hex, and a multiplier by one is a
> multiplier by one. Everything you added, you added at the two ends.
> And the two ends are worth having. Quarter to eight in the evening on
> the banner avenue is the best frame in this session and possibly the
> best frame in the game: the sand goes gold, the stone stays stone, the
> banners keep their red, and there are two small fires at the gate that
> only exist after dark. Somebody is keeping a fire in at a gate for a
> road nobody rides up. You did not write that down anywhere in the
> frame and I read it anyway.
>
> Ten at night in Brim Square is the other one. The page is lit and the
> room around it is not, the windows are on over the high street and a
> third of them are dark, and the whole thing still reads as a drawing
> on a desk. That is the metaphor paying for the feature, which is what
> WORLD-SYSTEMS promised and I did not expect to actually get.
>
> **The prints.** A walk is round dots evenly spaced. A run is dark,
> dragged, further apart. Side by side on the same ground, and I can
> tell which is which from across a room. That is the item.
>
> **The boat.** She sits in the water rather than on it, the walker is
> in her rather than on her, and she is drawn in the same pen as
> everything else. And the decision I actually care about is the one you
> wrote down: she does not leave the shore. You had the option to let a
> player row to the torn west edge in the first ten minutes and you
> refused it, on the grounds that the sandbar cost Session 5 a whole
> session and a boat that goes anywhere wet deletes it. Correct, and
> more importantly, *argued*.
>
> **What I am still not wowed by**, and it is not blocking: the boat's
> home framing. She is drawn up on the far bank across the water and the
> composition is a lot of sand. It is a first meeting and it should be
> a better one. Fix it when the coast is next open — do not re-cut this
> session for it.
>
> WOWED. Six lands, all six better at two hours of the day than they
> were at one, and nothing regressed."

**Verdict: WOWED**, round 4. Logged 2026-08-29.

**Standing note for a later session:** the rowboat's first-meeting
composition at THE RIVER MOUTH is the one thing this gate passed but did
not praise.
