# Critique 7 — THE DRY LANDS

*Session 11, 2026-08-31. The art-director gate per `QUALITY-BAR.md` §2,
run on real screenshots from the running game, desktop (1280×720) and
portrait (390×844).*

**Under review:** SPLITROCK CANYON and THE BLEACH FLATS — their places,
their waits (HOLT and AMOS) and their named inhabitants — plus the two
protected framings that look into this session's own ground
(`tear-lip`, `curl-rim`), at two hours of the day, with a DRIVEN framing
per land and both states of both waits photographed.

**Sheet:** `tools/shoot-dry.mjs`, fifty-one framings per viewport, every
one settled thirteen game seconds past the ink-in cascade on Session 9's
harness. Plus `tools/shoot-textures.mjs`, which this session ran FIRST
and which paid for itself in the first four minutes.

**Standing brief:** *rejects anything that looks procedural, placeholder,
or like a tech demo wearing a style. Blind against Gris, Sable, and
margins itself. The bar: they cannot tell which world had an art
budget.*

---

## Round 1 — NOT YET *(the prop box, on paper, before a single thing was
placed in the world)*

> "Six of these are not drawings of anything.
>
> **Your fin is an obelisk.** Beds at even spacing, all of them running
> the full width, ruled fractures between them, and a symmetric
> trapezoid round the lot: that is graph paper with a lid on it. You
> called the land SPLITROCK and drew a monument.
>
> **The chalk is invisible.** You have drawn five white marks on a wall
> the colour of the paper they are on. I have looked at this cell four
> times and I can see two of them, and only because one is next to a
> tick. The whole fable is in the SPACING of those marks and you have
> made the marks disappear.
>
> **The gravel is soap.** A hundred and fifty scribbled circles on a
> pale ground. A stone in a riverbed has been rolled — it is flat, it
> lies on its longest side, and it lies the same way as the one beside
> it, and none of that is a circle.
>
> **The track is fur.** It is the subject of the shot you say the Bleach
> Flats is staged around and it is a vertical smear of hair. A path worn
> by one person going both ways is two edges. Draw the edges.
>
> **And your skull has a face.** Two round sockets side by side over a
> mouth line. This game has one rule about faces and you have broken it
> on a cow."

**Mandatory:**
1. The fin, redrawn. Split rock is a block a fracture went through, not
   a spire.
2. The marks read, or the wait does not exist.
3. The gravel, the track and the strand line: draw the thing, not a
   texture of the thing.
4. The skull, in profile. Nobody has a face.

---

## Round 2 — NOT YET *(the world, first sheet)*

> "The whole of it is drawn half again life size and it is the only
> thing wrong that matters, because it is wrong everywhere.
>
> Your people are one point seven units wide, because Brack and Joan
> are. Your boat is eleven. Your needle arch is sixteen — in a channel
> that is twelve units across, so the first framing of your flagship
> land is a wireframe hoop filling half the screen with a canyon
> somewhere behind it. Your fallen slabs are thirteen units of brown
> lying about like tabletops. And there are eleven identical
> twenty-two-unit fins ranked along the rim, which from the floor read
> as **a row of shacks on the skyline** — which is very unfortunate,
> because there are also two actual shacks up there and they are the
> ones that matter.
>
> **The wall panels are scaffolding.** Eight flat cards standing at the
> foot of a slope with hard rectangle edges and nothing behind them.
>
> **And the boat is behind the walker.** You have staged the
> composition your land exists for and put its subject south of the
> viewpoint, in the one game I review where the camera cannot turn
> round. Session 5 lost two rounds to this. It is written on the wall
> in three separate files."

**Mandatory:**
1. Size everything in both lands against a person. Write the number
   down where the next session will find it.
2. The wall panels are on the wall or they are gone.
3. The boat, THE MARKS and HOLT'S PLACE are all NORTH of where the shot
   stands. In portrait too.

## Round 3 — NOT YET *(and this one is the session's own ground)*

> "Better. It is a canyon now: two walls, a corridor, a boat a long way
> up it and a house on the skyline. And then I looked at the walls.
>
> **They are herringbone.** Both of them, everywhere, at close range —
> big soft chevrons crawling over the rock in two directions at once.
> You know what this is. Your own `WORLD-SYSTEMS` §1 describes it, your
> own `HOLD_PLAN` exists because of it, and the comment directly above
> the offending line says *paper does not tear along a curve, it tears
> along its fibres* over eight units of value noise at a nine-unit
> wavelength. The fall line rotates from node to node, the shader has
> no direction to hatch down, so it hatches every direction and the
> wall is a smeared thumb.
>
> Nobody could see it before because nobody had ever stood on the
> floor. That is not an excuse; it is the reason the land needed
> building.
>
> **And THE EAST RIM is an empty beige frame with a label in it.** From
> where you have put the standing point the canyon is due west, and
> this camera looks north. The place is right. The place is ninety
> degrees from where you are standing in it."

**Mandatory:**
1. The tear is straight runs with corners between them, like the
   Holdfast and like paper. Keep the depth: `check-terrain` prints
   −10.8 and a stranger quotes it.
2. THE EAST RIM stands somewhere the cut runs away from it.

## Round 4 — NOT YET

> "The walls draw properly now and the corners do real work — you
> cannot see the whole canyon from inside it, which is worth more than
> anything you could have put in it.
>
> Three things left and they are all the same thing: **weight in the
> wrong place.**
>
> **THE MARKS is a monolith.** Eight units wide, fifteen tall, drawn at
> full ballpoint pressure, standing clear of the wall and breaking the
> skyline — it is the biggest and blackest object in the frame and the
> boat, which is what the land is ABOUT, is a thumbnail behind it. A
> record scratched on a wall is not a monument.
>
> **The fins are cellophane.** I am standing between two of them on the
> west bench and I can see the canyon through both. Your pines are
> translucent because foliage is translucent. Rock is not.
>
> **And the oasis is a swimming pool.** A flat blue ellipse lying on
> flat pale sand with five tufts beside it. You fixed this exact fault
> at the tarn last session and wrote down how."

**Mandatory:**
1. THE MARKS at the middle register, narrower, and its top BELOW the
   rim.
2. Rock is opaque.
3. The oasis sits in a hollow, with its waterline drawn.

## Round 5 — **WOWED**

> "Yes. And the thing I want to say first is about a number.
>
> **The fourth chalk mark comes out at two point six nine and the
> ground Holt's doorstep stands on is two point six nine four.** You did
> not write that anywhere in the game. There is no note about it, no
> prompt, no label; the only thing the game does is put the slab and the
> doorstep in the same frame from the channel floor and let a person
> notice that a line on a rock is level with a step forty units away and
> fifteen up. And then, if they go on noticing, that there are two marks
> at the bottom, thirteen units of blank rock, and three at the top —
> and that the bottom one is exactly where a boat would sit if it were
> on the ground, which it is not, because it is on trestles above it.
> **The bottom of that list describes the world after the wait, and the
> wait is what puts the boat there.** I have reviewed six of these
> sessions and that is the best piece of authorship in any of them.
>
> **SPLITROCK.** The corridor works because the GROUND works: nine
> straight runs with eight corners, so the walls hatch down real fall
> lines in parallel strokes and the canyon bends out of its own sight.
> It has two ends and no other way in, and neither of them is a designed
> entrance — a rip is shallow where it starts and shallow where it runs
> out, and you walk in at either. The mouth one is the better of the
> two and it is free: the water starts exactly where the dry bed does,
> so the walk up SPLITROCK begins at the point the river begins and goes
> up the bed the river is not in, and nobody says a word about it.
>
> THE LIP is the frame I would put on a poster. Standing on the east
> edge with the cut falling away at your feet and running north, getting
> deeper the whole way, with three pieces of driftwood lying on the rock
> at your shoulder thirteen units above anything that has ever been wet.
>
> **THE BLEACH FLATS.** The opposite land, and it is the better fable.
> Stand at the catch and look north and you have a man, a cistern, a
> track and a green smudge, in that order, receding, and the whole of
> `THE-WAITS` §5 is in it and not a syllable of it is written down. The
> note under the catch states three facts and draws no conclusion, which
> is the hardest thing in this game to do and the first time anybody has
> done it cleanly.
>
> And then the part I did not expect: **come back after dark and he is
> on the track with a yoke.** No prompt tells you to. No note mentions
> the hour. If you never come out here at night you will simply believe
> the cistern is full, and that is a *correct* belief, and it is not the
> truth. That is the day cycle finally earning what Session 6 paid for
> it.
>
> Two things I am passing and not praising, and I want them written
> down: **THE MARKS still reads as a standing stone rather than as a
> face of the wall** — it is a slab that came away, which is honest, but
> it is a compromise and you should know it is one. And **the west
> bench is a place a player can stand inside a cluster of rock and see
> four flat cutouts overlapping**, which is the oldest problem this
> engine has and this land does not solve it, it only avoids it.
>
> Nobody could tell which of these two lands had the art budget, and
> neither of them did."

## What this gate cost, and the two tools that decided it

Round 1 was run entirely on `tools/shoot-textures.mjs` — every drawing
in the prop box at actual size, on paper, with no camera and no land in
the way — and it found six faults in one look, four of which would have
taken a world re-shoot each to isolate. Session 10 called it "the single
most useful thing in this handoff" and it was right.

Rounds 2 to 5 were run on `tools/montage.mjs`. Three of the five
mandatory findings above (the row of shacks, the array of slabs, the
identical fin silhouettes) are invisible in any single frame and
obvious across six.

**Verdict: WOWED at round 5.**
