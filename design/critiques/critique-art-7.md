run on real screenshots from the running game, desktop (1280×720) and

their waits (HOLT and AMOS) and their named inhabitants — plus **a

## Round 1 — NOT YET, and it is the PROP BOX, not the world

*Shot first, per the handoff. The world was not rendered at all.*

> "I have not looked at your lands and I am not going to yet, because
> every drawing in both of them is wrong in the same way and it is a
> way you already know about.
>
> **Your canyon is full of DOMES.** Stacks, fallen blocks, the shed, the
> house, the mark slab, the cistern, the water can, the fence post, the
> signpost's arms — every one of them is a lozenge. You wrote *paper
> does not tear along a curve* into your own height field in Session 5,
> in capitals, and then handed a polygon of straight runs to a stroke
> function that draws quadratics through midpoints. It rounds every
> corner you give it. That is right for a hedge and it is right for a
> hull and it is fatal for a rock.
>
> **Your palms have no crowns.** You have invented a paling technique —
> ink at the foot, nothing at the top — and it is the right idea and you
> have run it at three times the strength it can survive. The trunk is
> at full ballpoint in black because it is near the ground, and the
> crown has gone entirely. A desert palm out here is a black pole with a
> ghost on it.
>
> **Your arch is a doughnut.** You cut an ellipse out of a blob and the
> result is a croquet hoop.
>
> **The shadow in the bottom of your one vertical land is drawn in
> horizontal lines**, because `hatch` takes an angle and zero is across.
> In a land whose entire ink technique is *everything runs down the
> page*, you have put the only cross-grain marks in it in its darkest
> corner.
>
> **And your two men are stick figures.** Look at Joan in the next
> land: a filled coat, a real head, limbs with pen in them. Yours have a
> pea on a wire. There are no faces in this world, so the body is the
> entire performance, and yours are not giving one."

Every one of those was found in four seconds, on paper, with no camera
and no land in the way. **The texture sheet is the single best tool this
project owns and this is the second session in a row to say so.**

## Round 3 — NOT YET, and now the world

> "**You have bricked up your own canyon.**
>
> Your best composition — a hundred and twelve units of corridor with a
> boat and a wall of marks at the end of it — came back as a BROWN WALL.
> You stood twenty-one-unit panels every thirteen units up both faces,
> at half their height, turned toward the middle. They stand on a
> gradient of two, so their feet are five units up the wall and their
> heads are twelve units over the lip, and eighteen units of channel
> cannot hold two of them and a walker. You have also broken the oldest
> placement rule in your own engine: **nothing stands on a scarp.**
>
> The terrain draws these walls. It hatches a cliff down its fall line
> and it does it better than a cutout can. What the floor needs from you
> is the near layer the ground cannot give it, and that is a handful of
> slabs that have come OFF the wall and are leaning against its foot.
>
> **And the whole land is brown.** Rock fill at seven tenths on every
> standee, fifty stacks up to twenty-two units tall on both lips, and a
> canyon wash under all of it. There is no sky in your skyline. Take the
> fills down, take two thirds of the stacks out, and let the paper
> through.
>
> **Two of your framings are photographing the back of the walker's
> head.** North is minus Z. Your arch is at z = −181 and you stood at
> z = −198 to look at it."

## Round 4 — NOT YET, and one of these would have shipped

> "That is a canyon. I can see the mouth, the arch, the corridor, the
> head. Four things.
>
> **The slab is a HAYSTACK.** You made it a face and then outlined its
> top, so it stands on the skyline as a lump in front of the wall rather
> than being the wall. Ink the two flanks and let the crown fade into
> the terrain's own hatching.
>
> **AND THEN YOUR FOURTH MARK DISAPPEARED INTO THAT FADE.** There are
> four chalk marks on that wall and the highest of them is THE DOORSTEP,
> which is the height of the lip, which is the entire point — and you
> erased it with the crown gradient you drew after it. I count three
> marks. **This is the one thing on this sheet that would have shipped
> wrong**, because it is a thing that is missing rather than a thing
> that is ugly, and nobody who did not know it was supposed to be there
> would have asked.
>
> **The rain-catch is standing in the lens of its own land's SHOT.** You
> put it on the track's line eight units north of the cistern. The
> camera sits fourteen units behind the walker. Everything behind the
> walker is in front of the camera.
>
> **Your man reads as a dog.** A metre and a half tall, bent at the
> hips, head below the hips, at forty units. Stand him up for most of
> the day; there is not much to do to a machine that works.
>
> **And I cannot see your track**, which is the one worn thing in a land
> whose whole subject is that one person walks it."

## Round 6 — WOWED

> "**Go and stand on the floor of that canyon and look north.**
>
> There is a boat, upside down on trestles, oiled, in a place with no
> water in it. Behind it there is a wall with four chalk lines up it.
> The lowest is the height of the boat. The next is the height of the
> trestles under the boat. The third is the height of the ridge of the
> shed that is standing forty units away on your left. The fourth is the
> height of the lip of the canyon, which is right there, and you can see
> that it is the same height because the lip is in the frame. And then,
> on the rock ABOVE the lip, where no flood has ever been, there is a
> fifth mark — and behind THAT, on the rim, is his house.
>
> I worked out what the list was before I read a word, and there is not
> a word to read. There is no note on the marks, no number, no scale,
> and no label. **You have put a man's entire private arithmetic into a
> sightline.** I have not seen a game do that.
>
> And you did the same thing twice, in a different key. Next door there
> is a machine in better order than anything else in this world, forty
> units from the only water, and a strip of ground one person wide
> between them, and at night there is a man on it with two full cans
> **coming the other way**. Nothing tells me he carries it. The
> geography tells me: the tank is on the rim of the only dish in the
> land and the water is at the bottom of it, so every can he has ever
> carried came UPHILL, both ways, and neither of us will ever mention
> it.
>
> The two lands are drawn at right angles to each other and I did not
> notice until I put them side by side, which is the correct order to
> notice a thing like that in. The canyon is nothing but verticals and
> the five chalk marks are the only horizontals in it, which is why they
> read as writing without being told to. The Flats are nothing but level
> broken dashes and the one ruled rectangle in a hundred and fifty units
> is the thing a man keeps in perfect order for a rain that has never
> come.
>
> **What I still do not like:** WHERE THE ROAD STOPS is two posts and
> some cracked ground at the end of the longest road in the world, and
> it is honest emptiness rather than composed emptiness. It passes. It
> is not praised, and you should know which of your six places is there
> because you wanted six.
>
> **And one thing I will say for the ground:** the mouth of that canyon
> is a ramp because a tear starts shallow and deepens, so there is no
> stair anywhere in the land and nobody had to author one. That is the
> metaphor paying you back, which is the third time this project has
> been paid by it and the first time it has been paid in TRAVERSAL.
>
> WOWED."

**Verdict: WOWED**, round 6. Logged 2026-08-31.

### `tear-lip` — 72%, and it is the whole frame

**This framing stands at (312, −140), inside SPLITROCK CANYON, which is
this session's scope, and this session MOVED THE THING THE FRAMING IS
NAMED AFTER.** There is no honest way to report that as a small number.

Its verdict is `critique-art-3`'s — **the sheet's ELEVATION**, awarded on
the tear reading as a torn page: lips standing proud, walls that refuse,
and a floor you can see a long way down to. Shot side by side against
the base:

- **base:** the walker stands beside a mesa cutout on flat ground with
  the old tear behind and to the right, mostly out of frame. You can see
  a slot. You cannot see into it.
- **head:** the walker stands on the **east lip** of the new tear. The
  cut falls away on the left with its floor visible the whole length of
  it, the Needle Arch spans it forty units north, and the stacks on the
  far rim are the skyline. It is still a lip. It is the same landform in
  the same vocabulary at the same depth — **`check-terrain` prints
  `floor -10.8`, as it has since Session 4** — in a place where the
  framing's own name is finally true.

**The verdict was awarded on the tear, and the tear is what moved.** It
is not a regression of the page's shape or of its vocabulary; it is the
page's shape being re-authored in the one land whose whole subject it is,
which is what a land session is for. `PLAN.md` now carries the rule this
produced: *a landform in the wrong place may be moved; a NUMBER somebody
else's content stands on may not.*

### `curl-rim` — 4.19%, and its own ground is untouched

The framing stands at (370, 16), **inside THE BLEACH FLATS**. Nothing in
`elevation.ts` reaches it: THE PAN is bounded hard at x = 332, thirty-
eight units short of the walker's own footing, and `curlE`, `curlN` and
the sheet's edge bands are not touched anywhere in this session. **The
ground under this framing is the same ground, to the unit.**

What moved is what is drawn ON it and BEYOND it, and both are the Flats:
the draft's sixty dune decals and forty-two cacti were deleted and the
land's new ground script laid in their place, and the old canyon's mesa
cutouts have come off the horizon and been replaced by the new one's
stacks a hundred and forty units north. Side by side, the curl is the
same curl — the same rise, the same shoulder, the same sheet standing
proud of the desk beyond it, the same river ribbon at the far left.

### `crease-east-road` — 88 pixels, and the cause is provable

Eighty-eight pixels out of nine hundred and twenty thousand, max delta
39/255, in the right half of the frame at distance. **This is the only
change in this session that reaches west of x = 230**, and it is not a
prop, because every prop either session authored is east of x = 236.

It is the river. `RIVER[0]` moved from (318, −108) to (296, −116) so the
water would still rise at the canyon's mouth after the tear moved, and
that shortened the polyline from **753.0 units to 750.0** — which

## Standing notes for a later session

- **WHERE THE ROAD STOPS passed and was not praised.** It is the sixth
  place in a land that had five, and it looks like it.
- **The lit window in Holt's house is a nice idea at four units of
  standee width.** From the channel floor at night it is one warm pixel

  a night pass should give it a glow rather than a fill.

  sixty-six units by a hundred and thirty, with six stacks and a cairn
