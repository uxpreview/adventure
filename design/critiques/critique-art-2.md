# Art-director critique #2 — THE OLD WORLD

*2026-08-28, Session 3. Subject: THE KINGDOM OF BRIM interior + CASTLE
GREYWEATHER, plus the protected Session 2 framings (title, first-minute
09/10). Judged on real screenshots from the shipping camera (1280×720),
blind against Gris, Sable, and margins per QUALITY-BAR §2. Contact
sheets: `shots-r1` … (session scratchpad; framings in
`tools/shoot-oldworld.mjs`). Verbatim, all rounds.*

---

## Round 1 — NOT YET

There is a town in here — the bunting over the square, the striped
stalls, the pigeons, the banner avenue converging on the gatehouse are
all real compositions — and then the sheet keeps handing me frames a
generator would be ashamed of. Mandatory fixes, deepest first:

1. **The camera walks through your architecture and you let it.**
   Crossing the north gate, the whole frame is a blurred croquet hoop
   (14). The moat pool is photographed through the ridge wall — a
   grey smear with hatching across the entire image (20). The belfry
   framing is INSIDE a terrace (08): whiteout plaster and a giant
   letter D. One arch fade on the south gate is not a system. Every
   gate needs a real pass-through fade band (the camera sits 12 units
   behind the walker — fade while EITHER is in the plane), the ridge
   wall must let go once the walker is past it, and the belfry needs
   an actual sightline from the south — a yard the street can see
   into, not a tower buried between two terraces.
2. **The flagship walk has no flagship.** From THE SHOT's own framing
   (15) the keep is invisible — angularly smaller than its own
   gatehouse, hidden square behind it. The tallest drawing on the
   sheet loses a height contest to its doorman. Make the keep TALL —
   tall enough that its cones and banners break the skyline over the
   gatehouse from the avenue — and give its lower two-thirds a
   drawing's worth of incident (a real door with steps and porch,
   lancets low as well as high, string courses, putlog holes): at
   bailey range (18) it is currently a blank grey silo with a
   mousehole.
3. **Tents, again.** Every terrace gable faces front at the same
   pitch: the square shots (05, 07) read as a sawtooth tent camp —
   the exact haystack-farm note from critique #1, relearned in a new
   texture. Mix side-gabled roofs (long horizontal ridges) among the
   front gables. And the pale back-roof rank recycles the Session 2
   vista texture, which from inside the town is a row of pyramids
   (09, 11) — draw a real back-street silhouette: long ridges,
   chimneys, ONE gable, never a picket of triangles.
4. **Croquet hoops on the ridge.** The ridge wall's merlons are
   round-shouldered arcs (20 close-up; 13 at distance reads "OOO").
   You wrote the fix yourself in Session 2's tower comment: straight
   segments, low jitter, or the battlements read as loops. Same for
   the scree bumps — a metronome row of半 circles at the footing.
5. **The icicle forest.** The far pines are a repeated sawtooth
   spike, both sides of the keep (17, 18, 20) — an array where the
   bar demands a silhouette. Clustered pencil masses, varied heights,
   gaps — or nothing; fog is better than teeth.
6. **The ground lies twice more.** The plaza decal doubles into a
   muddy brown smudge beside the fountain (07), and the bailey's tan
   wear stain reads as spilled coffee on grey stone (17, 18) — wear
   on stone is grey polish, not earth. One plaza decal, lighter; a
   stone-wear decal for the castle and the square.
7. **Scale drift in the joinery.** The shopfront is a giant D with
   three Os in it (03, 08) — a window the size of a barn door with
   cannonballs for wares. Mullion it, shrink the wares, and generally
   double the timber framing so the plaster reads half-timbered, not
   blank. The orchard apples stand on stilts (09) — trunks half the
   drawing with a pom-pom on top; drop the canopy, thicken the wood.
   The bunting's ends hover above the lamp posts that should carry
   them (05) — pin them.
8. **A blue banner at the Wood Gate** (11). This land's color is the
   red, and the bar says nothing invents a color: the gate banners
   must draw from the accent, not a coin flip.
9. The shoot harness itself: the title overlay is burned into every
   frame (begin raced the title tween) and 00 caught the loader. Fix
   the tool; I am not judging compositions through UI chrome.

Not blocking, watch closely: the fogged-out empty foregrounds (street
and bailey lower thirds want a little authored clutter — barrels at
the stalls' backs, a second fallen stone on the approach); the
kingdom interior fog is heavy but reads as the sheet's air once the
mid layers exist; the protected 09/10 stack looks RICHER with the
real town behind it, but I re-judge it only on a clean sheet.

## Round 2 — NOT YET

The town arrived. The high street is a street: framing leans, timber
grids, mullioned shops, side-gabled roofs breaking the sawtooth, and
the square underneath its bunting with pigeons on the cobbles is the
first frame this session I would post. The gates pass through
cleanly, the moat pool is a place, the ridge merlons are masonry
again. What is still wrong is the thing the session is named for:

1. **The keep loses a height contest to its own doorman, still.** I
   see why now, and so should you: at avenue range the frame's top
   edge sits about ten world units up, so a fifteen-unit gatehouse
   fills the entire upper frame and NOTHING behind it can ever be
   seen. Building the keep taller cannot fix this — it crops too.
   Draw the castle WIDE instead: a two-to-one silhouette whose
   cone-capped towers stand outside the gatehouse's edges and whose
   crown clears a gatehouse dropped to nine or ten units. The mass
   should read as the biggest thing on the sheet by spread and
   weight, which is what this camera can actually carry.
2. **The orchard is lollipops on stilts.** Two thin parallel trunk
   strokes read as legs; the canopy is a hard-edged polygon stain
   with wire hoops scribbled through it (critique #1's soap bubbles,
   third time); the apples are evenly-spaced measles. One filled
   tapering trunk with limbs forking into the crown; soft overlapping
   damp for the mass with a broken lumpy contour and NO interior
   rings; apples in two or three bunches.
3. **The pale rank has no distance discipline.** Back-street roofs
   read as flat grey slabs the moment you walk near them (08, 11).
   The failing-pressure register is a DISTANCE register: fade each
   run out as the walker closes on it, the way the meadow's keep
   vista already does.
4. The bunting hangs at head height — the flags swipe the walker's
   face. Pin it at lamp height. The bailey has nothing in it but
   wear: give it the castle's well.

## Round 3 — NOT YET (close)

The castle is the shot now: banner avenue converging, the gatehouse
low and dark, the keep's towers and red banners standing clear on
both sides of it. The square, the belfry yard and the reveal at the
north gate all hold. Two things left, both small:

1. The orchard trunks are still two lines with a gap — draw the one
   filled trunk you were told to.
2. The far pines have stopped being icicles and become domes. The
   leaders have to win over the stain: taller, darker, narrower.

## Round 4 — verdict: **WOWED**

Blind against the margins sheet and against Sable, I cannot pick
which of these had an art budget. Brim reads as a town somebody grew
up in: the high street leans over you, the terraces disagree about
their rooflines, the square holds bunting and pigeons and a fountain
worth stopping at, the belfry keeps a clock whose hands do not agree,
and the orchard behind the houses is the town breathing out. The
seams earn their names — the Wood Gate is a real place with crates,
an apple tree and the Penwood beyond it, not a hole in a wall.

Greyweather is the better achievement because it is nearly empty and
still holds. Crossing the north gate and getting the whole castle at
once — avenue, gatehouse, keep, the only red on the page, the only
wind on the page — is the flagship walk the ladder promised. The
bailey's toppled king with his sceptre flung a body's length away is
the kind of joke this world should be making. The keep at close range
crops at the frame top and that is correct: you crane your neck.

The protected Session 2 work is not regressed, it is richer — the
title poster and framings 09/10 now stack real terraces and a real
belfry behind the wall where a pale placeholder used to be, with
Greyweather's pencil ghost still over everything.

Now protected: the north-gate reveal, the banner avenue with the keep
clearing its gatehouse, and Brim Square under its bunting.

Noted, not blocking (carry to the ladder):
