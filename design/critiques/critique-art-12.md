# Art-director critique #12 — THE ROADS

*2026-09-04, Session 18. Subject: the roads — fifteen encounters built
as routines with a turn, forty-five districts, two authored midpoints,
the bicycle, the paper plane, and the 8:15 on the day after the ending.
Judged on real screenshots from the shipping camera (1280×720 +
390×844), reviewed blind against Gris, Sable and margins per
QUALITY-BAR §2. Contact sheet: `shots-s18` (`tools/shoot-session18.mjs`,
36 framings, both rigs, at the hour each encounter is on and once at
the hour it is over). Verbatim, all rounds.*

*This is the third art gate under `QUALITY-BAR` §3's permission to
regress. The protected framings are `diff-sheets`' to report and the
numbers are in `SESSIONS.md`; what this judges is whether a road with
an encounter on it is a road with a story on it, or a road with more
furniture.*

---

## Round 1 — NOT YET

The premise holds, and it holds harder than Session 17's did: an
encounter is a routine WITH A TURN, and the turn is what makes it read.
The broken cart at ten is a cart; the same cart at three with a man
bent over it is a story; the same road at four, empty, with the road
pushed home, is the world having had a day. That is the whole claim
and it survives the sheet. Eight frames do not, and six of them are
the camera's fault and not the world's.

1. **Five framings stand on the wrong side of their subject.** The
   camera only ever looks north, and the funeral (19), the woodpile
   (27), the airborne plane (33), the cove light (14) and the mirage
   (21) were all photographed from a spot that put the subject behind
   the lens or off its side. Session 5's law — *a thing they look at is
   north of where they stand* — was applied to every prop in the
   session and not to the sheet that photographs them. Re-framed, not
   re-built, except:
2. **The mirage stood where no road looks.** At (352, −50) the false
   oasis was forty degrees east of the trail's line of sight; a mirage
   nobody can see is a standee. Moved to (300, −86), dead ahead of the
   trail's run across the pan, where the encounter is *walking toward
   it and it is not there*.
3. **The cove light stood where the cove's own frame could not hold
   it.** Moved twenty units north, still thirty-five off the sand,
   still not the mark.
4. **The rings on the tarn were ink on ink.** The tarn at dusk is the
   darkest water in the game and the ring was drawn in the pen's own
   black; there is no ring in the frame. Pale, from the second round.
5. **The district card was in the frame and gone before the shutter.**
   It lives two and six-tenths wall-clock seconds and the harness's
   steps are wall-clock: a hundred and fifty frames after the crossing
   is longer than the card. Framed with the shutter as close to the
   crossing as the walk allows.
6. **The flock's baseline hugged the river's paint**, so two of seven
   sheep on the east road stood on the blue's near edge. Two units
   further south; the river is the other side.
7. **The broken cart's cant does not read at twelve units.** It is
   canted thirty degrees on its axle with the wheel on the road beside
   it, and at the sheet's distance it is a handcart with something
   near it. Passed anyway — the three-hour story reads (cart, man,
   road) — but the cant is a thing the walker only sees close, and the
   play sheet sends them close.

**What already reads, round one:**

- **The ladder** (07) is the best new drawing on the sheet: two people
  and one long thing between them is a sentence, and the drawing is a
  ruler with rungs. The leant ladder at the fair ground at noon (08)
  is the aftermath done right — a thing that says two people were
  here, and will be back.
- **The dawn dog** (05) beside the walker on the coast road at six is
  the Downs' dog and everybody knows it, which is the point: the
  third telling of the rule. At its line (06, second framing) the
  goat is in the frame too, past the line, and the dog is not, and
  the frame says the rule and the exception in one picture.
- **The fire** (11, 12, 13): lit with nobody at it beside the boat
  resting is the coast's loneliest frame; two sat at it at nine is the
  same frame answered; cold ash at noon is the morning after. Three
  hours, one thing.
- **The gull** (15) on the crest with the walker stopped beside it is
  a joke in one drawing, and the barrier that is the joke is the
  drawing.
- **The felled pine** (16) with the saw standing in the cut reads as
  work interrupted, and two lengths at the verge (17) as work done.
- **The bicycle** (28–31): parked on its stand on the verge it is a
  found thing; ridden, the rider sits over the frame and the front
  wheel leads; at the Common's edge with GET OFF over it (31) it is
  the border rule in a mount, and it is the frame the sheet was built
  for. The bell at the cat (30) reads on the rider's recoil more than
  on the cat, which is correct for a phone on silent.
- **The plane at the far wall** (34) is a small pale dart on the far
  slope of the cut with the walker on the floor below it, and it is a
  long way to walk.
- **The 8:15 the day after** (35, 36): the same train at the same
  stop, one with three in the windows and one on the platform, the
  other empty with the doors open the same thirteen seconds. The
  difference is the whole of what the daily transit means and it is
  visible without a word.

## Round 2 — PASSED, with three passed-not-praised

The seven fixes above re-shot; the changed frames re-shot in both rigs.

**The encounters read as things that happened.** The funeral (19) is
four figures in file on the far side of the river, small, going up the
lane, and nobody in the frame is doing anything else, which is what a
funeral looks like from across a field. The mirage (21) is three palms
dead ahead on the pan where there is nothing, and the walker will walk
toward them. The dawn dog at its line (06) with the goat past it is the
rule and its exception in one frame.

**The midpoints are midpoints.** The cairn on the trail (26) is a small
standing thing where there was nothing for twenty-two seconds, and it
is a thing to walk toward. The woodpile (27) is four lengths at the
verge on the round's south arc — and it took three framings to
photograph, because the pile is south of the road and a north-looking
lens standing north of it has it between the walker and the glass.
From the road it is the first thing in the frame.

**The ring on the tarn** (18), pale now, is a small spiral on the dark
water to the right of the walker, mid-fade; one every ten seconds, and
never where you are looking.

**Passed, not praised:**

- **The cove light** (14) is a faint warm point on the water at night,
  and on a phone in daylight it will be nothing. It is meant to be
  faint; whether it is too faint is the owner's phone's.
- **The mirage on a phone** (21, portrait): the narrower rig does not
  hold the palms from the trail's line; a peek finds them. On the
  desktop they are dead ahead.
- **The plane over the cut** (33) is NOT in the frame, in either of
  the two framings that tried for it: it is in the air for three
  seconds, thirteen units up, and the shipping camera's frame top is
  lower than that at the distances the floor allows. A contact sheet
  is the wrong instrument for it; the play sheet is the right one, and
  the plane on the rock (32) and at the far wall (34) are the still
  halves of the same thing.

**Verdict:** PASSED. The roads have things on them that happen, and
the things are in the house's hand. The protected framings moved by
what `diff-sheets` says they moved by, in `SESSIONS.md`.
