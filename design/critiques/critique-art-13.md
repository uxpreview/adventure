# Art-director critique #13 — THE NEW CAST, WEST AND NORTH

*2026-09-04, Session 19. Subject: the four re-opened lands — CASTLE
GREYWEATHER (re-drawn, with Wick, the portcullis and the moat), LONGSHORE
(the surfers, the horn, Pye and the pot line), THE WIDE BLUE (the
longship on the Holdfast, Wren and the second mark, the stone on the
bar), and BRIM's square as far as the fifth banner reads it. Judged on
real screenshots from the shipping camera (1280×720 + 390×844), reviewed
blind against Gris, Sable and margins per QUALITY-BAR §2. Contact sheet:
`shots-s19` (`tools/shoot-session19.mjs`, 34 framings, both rigs, at the
hour each thing is on, once at the hour it is over, and on the day the
moat is red). Verbatim, all rounds.*

*This is the fourth art gate under `QUALITY-BAR` §3's permission to
regress. The protected framings are `diff-sheets`' to report and the
numbers are in `SESSIONS.md`; what this judges is whether three lands
that have had their named person in a pose since Session 14 are places

## Round 1 — NOT YET

The premise holds. A Viking party that cannot land is a joke that gets
funnier by the roar, and the drawing carries it: seven shields, a
striped sail furled on its yard, and at the third roar the walker has
stepped back once and not again. Wick with a rolled banner across his
knees is a person with a job before he stands up. But six frames do not
read, and four of them are the sheet's fault and two are the world's.

1. **The board could not be racked, and the frame said the van's note
   instead.** The van's POI had a reach of eight and the rack stands
   five from the van, so a walker carrying the board to the rack was
   inside the van's reach, and the key opened the sticker. The same
   lesson as the well (15) and the plane (18): *a thing's home, and its
   goal, may not be inside another prompt's reach.* Van reach five; the
   rack takes the board at six and two tenths.
2. **The horn was a POI without a drawing.** BLOW THE HORN stood over
   bare rock on the point: the touch was built, the standee was not.
   Nothing in the world may be a prompt over nothing. The horn drawing
   (a curved horn on a flat stone, `hornTexture`) stands at
   (−233, −76.4) beside the cairn now.
3. **The fifth banner stood behind the gate tower.** Placed at the
   gate's own foot it was inside the tower's silhouette from every
   spot on the avenue that looks up at the gate, so the one thing the
   frame is for — Brim's red on Greyweather's wall — was a pole-top.
   Moved west along the ridge to (−68.5, −200), where the avenue's
   last bend looks straight at it and the gate tower is the frame's
   other edge.
4. **Wren's punt was drawn in the water.** At the bar's shoulder the
   height field is under the sea, and a punt drawn up is a punt afloat
   with nobody in it, which is a different picture. Moved to the bar's
   root, where the sand starts, on ground the walker can stand on.
5. **The keep's masonry was a wash.** `greyweatherKeepTexture` was a
   grey rectangle with a door in it, and the brief's first complaint
   about the land was *flat cards on empty ground*. It has courses now
   (a coursed-masonry pass, quoins at the corners, a plinth course,
   a damp stain at the foot), and the ground it stands on has a yard
   drawn on it and three things in the yard — a trough, a timber
   stack, two crates — that the walker cannot walk through.
6. **The roar and the horn's answer were never in a frame.** Both are
   sounds with a place and a duration shorter than the harness's
   shutter, so the first sheet photographed the longship beached and
   the walker standing still, which proves nothing. The DO table now
   samples per frame for the roar and blows the horn and waits the
   beat and a half; the assertions in `check-verbs` §10 do the same.

**What already reads, round one:**

- **The longship coming in** (01) at a quarter to seven is the best
  new picture in four lands: a striped sail on the offing, oars out,
  the promenade's end in the foreground with nobody on it yet, and the
  walker small at the sand's edge. **Beached** (02) she is a different
  drawing and the same boat — sail on the yard, shields along her,
  the cliff's foot behind. **Round the mark** (04) she is in the race
  under sail with the fleet, and the fleet reads as a fleet with her
  in it and not as four boats and a fifth.
- **The horn on the point** (05) is a small thing on a stone beside a
  cairn, with the prompt beside it and not over it (the QA pass's
  placement rule, applied), and it reads as a thing to pick up before
  the prompt says what it is.
- **The van at noon** (08) and **at dusk** (09): a van, a rack with
  boards in it, a wetsuit on a line, and at dusk one warm window in
  the van's side. It is the coast's first lit interior after dark;
  the jetty lamp, forty units south, does the same for the promenade
  and is not in this frame.
- **The surfers checking the water** (07): two figures at the water's
  edge at a quarter past six, facing the sea, not each other. The
  routine's whole meaning is in the facing.
- **Pye rowing the line** (12) reads as work — the boat on the line of
  buoys, a pause at each — and **at the pots** (13) he is bent at a
  pile of them on the sand with the boat drawn up behind, which is the
  same man on shore. **The eighth pot** (15) is a buoy alone, further
  out than the line, and it means what it means only if you took that
  door.
- **Wick resting at dawn** (21) — C12, the encounter Session 18
  deferred — is an old man on the verge with a rolled banner across
  his knees and the avenue going up past him into mist. **At the
  pole** (22) he is reaching up; **at the pool** (23) he is bent at
  the water with the pole's old cloth. Three frames, one job, and the
  job is the land's wait.
- **The keep stood in** (26) and **the bailey from the gate** (27):
  the yard is a yard now. A trough, a timber stack, crates, a well, a
  puddle, wear in the ground between them, and the keep's wall has
  courses that read at twelve units on a phone.
- **The portcullis down** (28) is a grid of iron across the gate's
  arch with the walker under it and the braziers guttering, and it is
  the castle's toy: it goes back up on its own.
- **The moat red** (29) on the second day is a red stain in a grey
  pool under a grey wall, and nothing else in the frame is red, which
  is why it reads. **At night** (30) the reeds are the only thing that
  moves.
- **The shape in the deep pines** (31) is a taller trunk than the
  trunks, at the frame's edge, at night, for under a second. It is
  not in the frame long enough to be anything, which is the law.
- **The stone skips** (33): the stone in the water off the crest and
  the walker on the sand with an empty hand, the longship going
  round the mark behind. The rings are faint at the sheet's distance
  and the sound (`stone-skip`, three notes) carries the count.
- **A rowboat in the fleet** (34): the walker's own boat among the
  regatta with the nearest racer bearing away from it. The fleet
  minds its own race.

## Round 2 — PASS, with the named figures small at dusk

All six items are addressed and re-shot on both rigs. The board goes
on the rack and the frame says THE BOARD, RACKED and not the sticker;
the horn is a horn; the fifth banner stands where the avenue looks;
Wren's punt is on sand; the keep has stone; the roar and the answer
are sampled and asserted.

**One thing stands, and it is not this session's to fix.** The named
figures — Wick on the verge, Pye at his pots, Wren beside the punt —
are drawn at the same scale as every figure in the game, and at the
dusk grade on a phone they are two line-heights tall. They read as
people. They do not read as *these* people until the walker is within
eight units, and the routine's turn (Wick standing up, Pye putting a
pot down) is what says who they are. This is the same note as
Session 17's on the life, and the answer is the same: the camera's
rise, not the drawing. It is on Session 20's standing debts, with the
king's-road fountain and the frame-rate dips.

**Verdicts:**

- **CASTLE GREYWEATHER — RE-EARNED.** The keep is a keep, the yard is
  a yard, and the land has a person in it with a day. The portcullis
  and the red moat are the two things the brief asked for as *toy*
  and *monster*, and neither explains itself.
- **LONGSHORE — RE-EARNED.** The van, the rack and the wetsuit make
  a place on a shelf of sand that was an empty shelf; the surfers'
  routine is the coast's second routine that faces the sea; the board
  is the first errand a thing has ever asked for. Pye and his pots
  give Shelter Cove a morning. The jetty lamp changes the promenade
  after dark.
- **THE WIDE BLUE — RE-EARNED.** The longship makes the Holdfast a
  district with something on it; the regatta is a race with a fifth
  boat in it; Wren's punt and the second mark give the bar's root a
  reason. The stone is a toy in the emptiest land and it is the
  right toy for it.
- **BRIM — HELD.** The square's note now reads the red cloth, and
  that is all that changed. The belfry was not touched; the fifth
  banner is Greyweather's frame, not Brim's. No verdict re-earned
  because none was at risk.

**Not judged here:** whether any of it is fun. That is the play sheet's
(`design/play-sheets/session-19.md`), and it is the owner's.
