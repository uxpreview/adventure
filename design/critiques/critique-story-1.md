## PART 2 — THE NOTES, READ ALOUD

*All thirty-four, in one sitting, in world order. **Winking is
inaudible on a screen and unmissable in the mouth**, which is why the
brief asked for this and why it caught things the grep could not.*

### The count was worse than anybody had said

`STORY.md` §8 rule 7 estimated "about a third of the thirty-five
existing notes." The session brief counted **17 of 33** with a grep.
Read aloud, it is **24 of 34** — over two thirds.

The grep undercounted because it looked for six phrasings (`the sheet`,
`the page`, `whoever drew`, `a drawing`, `the pen`, `drawn `) and the
offence has more ways to say itself than that. Things it missed:

| note | what it actually said |
|---|---|
| brim square | *"whatever a **scribble** can be argued into being"*, and *"since the town was **six lines old**"* |
| the needle arch | *"holding since **the second stroke**"* |
| the oasis | *"or **the artist** only had the one pen"* |
| the sandbar | *"**the wash went over this page** in one pass"* |
| the mark | *"since **the page was wet**"* |

Anybody auditing this rule again should read, not grep.

### The line the whole story hangs on

> **OLD** · the timetable says the 8:15 is coming. *the 8:15 is drawn
> nowhere on this sheet.* everyone waiting knows both of these things
> and has made their peace.
>
> **NEW** · the timetable says the 8:15 is coming. **there is no track
> here, and there is no track anywhere.** everyone waiting knows both of
> these things and has made their peace.

**The reasoning, because this one is owed a defence.**

What the sentence DOES is a three-beat move: a promise, then a bald
statement of the impossible delivered in exactly the same register, then
the deadpan. The middle clause has to be *total* — not "there is no
train today" but "there has never been anywhere for one to be" — and
the old line got its totality by pointing at the medium.

The swap keeps the beat count, the cadence and the closing clause
verbatim. It loses nothing in the first sentence and nothing in the
third. What it changes is where the impossibility comes from: **from an
assertion about the sheet to an absence at the player's own feet.**

And that is a gain rather than a trade, in one specific way. *"The 8:15
is drawn nowhere on this sheet"* is a claim only the narrator can make
and no player can check. *"There is no track here"* is a fact the
player is standing in, and *"and there is no track anywhere"* is a fact
that is only knowable by somebody who has been everywhere — which in
this world is exactly one person, and it is the person holding the
controller. **The line quietly became about the walker, and nobody says
so.**

STORY §1 calls this the best sentence anybody has written for this
project. It is still that sentence. It says it in the world now.

### The one judgement call, flagged rather than taken

Two surviving lines use handwriting as a **simile** rather than
asserting the world is a drawing:

- *riverbend* — "the river practices its cursive on this corner of the
  common. the reeds lean in to read it."
- *the tarn* — "still water, black as the good ink."

**They are kept, on this distinction: assertion versus simile.** A note
that says the world IS drawn has broken the rule. A note that compares
a thing in the world to handwriting is a figure of speech available to
any narrator in any book, and this world has ink and pens in it as
ordinary objects — Wick dyes cloth, Teg drafts a complaint, somebody
chalks a board. Ban the simile and you ban the voice.

**Owner: if you disagree, both are one-line changes and they are the
only two in the game.** Say so and they go.

### And one change that is not a voice fix

**THE CROSSROADS.** The fourth arm said HOME. `STORY.md` §4 says the
Common's signpost has four names on it and one of them is a TIME, and
calls it the hinge Act II turns on. The built game and the locked story
disagreed, and the story is binding.

It now says **8:15**, on the same board, in the same hand, at the same
size, with the note reading *"and one that says 8:15, which is not a
place."* Nothing else in the game refers to it.

### What survived untouched

Ten notes needed nothing and got nothing: **the arguing oaks**,
**riverbend**, **the scarecrow**, **the proclamation**, **the toppled
king**, **the moat pool**, **the painted huts**, **the cut**, **shelter
cove**, and the three-quarters of others that were already clean. THE
CUT and THE PAINTED HUTS are the best-written things in the game and
were not opened.

### What was lost, honestly

Three jokes died and they were good ones:

- *"apples, or cannonballs, or very patient hedgehogs"* (brim square);
- *"the apples are red because the pen only brought one other colour,
  and it was needed for the flags"* (the orchard close);
- *"the sound of a drawing bragging about being a drawing"* (the
  boardwalk).

Every one of them was a medium joke, which is the category being
retired. What replaced them is not funnier, and in two of the three
cases it is doing the story's work instead — brim square now states its
own wait as a flat fact, and the orchard close now plants a stranger
(S6, the thirteenth tree) three sessions before it is built.

**The humour has moved from the sentence to the structure**, which is
what STORY §8 rule 3 asked for: *deadpan and structural, never a gag*.
That is the right trade and it should be said out loud that it IS a
trade.

## PART 3 — THE STORY EDITOR *(a proposal — owner to ratify)*

### What is being proposed

A third standing critic, alongside the art director and the Awwwards
juror, with one job:

> **The story editor reads a wait BLIND — the person, the places, the
> turn and the change, and nothing else — and has to be able to say what
> that land BELIEVES. Without being told. And without the word
> "waiting" appearing in their answer.**
>
> If they cannot, the wait is a description and not a fable, and it goes
> back.

**Why the word is banned.** Every land in this game is waiting for
something; saying so is free and says nothing. Forcing the answer into a
positive claim about the world is what separates *"this place is waiting
for rain"* from *"this place believes elsewhere owes it something."*
The first is a summary. The second is an ideology, which is what
WORLD-SYSTEMS §10 asks every region to have.

**Why it is a proposal and not a fact.** It is a new gate on a project
that already has two, it is the first critic in this project that judges
WRITING rather than pictures, and the owner should decide whether the
project wants one. It is logged here because the session brief asked for
it to be run once and logged either way.

**Twelve for twelve, and not one of the answers needed the word.**

### The two notes

**MAPLE COURT.** The answer that comes out of the fable is *"a street
stays a street as long as one window on it is lit."* `STORY.md` §3 says
Maple Court believes *"that leaving is temporary."* Those are not the
same sentence.

They should not be. That gap is **the turn** — §3's line is what the
land says about itself, and the fable's line is what it turns out to be
about. Every one of the twelve should show that gap and eleven of them
do less visibly. It is worth writing down that a story editor reading
blind will produce the POST-turn belief, and that checking it against
`STORY.md` §3 is therefore a check on whether the turn moved anything.
On this reading, Maple Court's moved furthest.

**THE COMMON.** `STORY.md` §3 says the Common believes *"nothing yet —
which is why you start here"*, and the blind read produces a belief for
Nell. That is not a contradiction: the LAND is the player's blank page
and the PERSON at the gate is not. But it is the one row where the land
and its named inhabitant do not hold the same thing, and the owner
should know that before anybody builds it.

### The editor's own note, unprompted

> Two of the twelve — the Harrow Downs and Greyline City — resolve with
> no knowledge carried into them at all. You sit down, or you stop
> walking. Everything else in this game is somebody carrying a fact
> across a border they alone can cross, and twice it is just a person
> being there.
>
> I do not think that is a hole. I think it is the only place in the
> design where the story says what the walker actually is, and it says
> it in the shape of the content rather than in a line of prose. Leave
> it exactly where it is and never explain it.
