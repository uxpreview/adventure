## Round 1 — NOT YET

The bones are right and one thing is wrong enough to stop the round.

1. **The bull is never in the picture.** The first build ran the chase
   north through the long fence, and on a north-locked camera a
   pursuer is behind the lens from the first stride to the last: the
   wake frame has a bull, the charge frame has a hoof at the bottom
   edge, and the stop at the rails happens at the walker's back. A
   fright you cannot see is a sound effect. *(Fixed in the source, not
   the framing: the chase runs east to west now, the gate is in the
   field's west hedge, and the bull is on the walker's right the whole
   way and at the hedge, in frame, when Nell reaches for the leaf.)*
2. **The bull is a hole in the page.** A black slab at 0.78 with legs
   under it, in a world that is line over stain everywhere else. Half
   the stain and let the pen fill it.
3. **The goat is shut in with the bull.** It followed the walker's
   position through the open gate and was inside the field when the
   gate went. Funny once and a broken beat forever. *(`keepOut`.)*
4. **The wake card says THE RIVER BEND.** The district's rect reached
   into the field; a player wakes in wheat under a card naming a river
   they cannot see.

## Round 2 — PASSED, with three passed-not-praised

**The wake is the picture this session exists for**, and it works on
both rigs: long grass at eye level, the walker small in it, and a bull
at nine units to the right with its head up, in portrait's frame by a
degree. The hint under it (*hold shift to run*) is chrome and it is
the right chrome at the right second. Blind against Sable this reads
as an opening and not as a spawn point.

**The gate frame** — the walker just through, Nell with an arm out for
the leaf, the bull's mass at the hedge behind the bars, the goat
outside on the near side — is three signals in one frame, exactly what
`critique-story-2` asked for and did not expect to get.

**The lures from the crossroads.** On desktop all four hold at rest and
the frame reads as a place with four directions in it for the first
time since Session 2: the keep straight up the road, a plume right of
the town's roofs, five ruled towers at the far right in the palest
wash on the sheet, and a horizon of broken pencil and blue over the
oaks to the left. In portrait the keep holds alone at rest and the
peek finds the sea and the smoke, which is what the measurement said
it would, and the towers never. **That is the honest frame and it is
enough for a phone: a player who leans sees two more things, and
leaning is the control the arrival hint teaches.**

**The title poster** re-shot at noon and at 19.6 in both viewports: the
composition of critique-art-1 — walker low on the ring, signpost, the
road funnelled to the gate, the wall banding the haze, the keep's
ghost — is untouched, and the two new marks at its edges (the sea's
horizon left, the towers right) sit in the pencil register the keep
already set. The poster is the poster with a wider world behind it.
**Verdict re-earned.** The numbers are in `SESSIONS.md`.

**The fair ground** is a place: the ring, the pole, the board, the two
bales, and the card over it. **The district card** at the crossroads
— the land's name small, the district's big — is the right size for
a smaller arrival.

Passed, not praised:

- **THE FIELD GATE's label floats in the sky.** The skyline writes a
  name above the tallest thing within three and a half units, and the
  hedge column is five and a half tall at every cell round the gate,
  so the label sits above the hedge and, at two units from the lens,
  projects at the top of the frame. Same compromise class as READ THE
  PROCLAMATION and THE 8:15 STOP; belongs to whoever next opens the
  skyline.
- **The sea's glint is pencil in the sky.** Above the wall band, at
  the horizon's height, it reads as a far horizon on desktop and as a
  row of dashes on a phone. It is the weakest of the four and it is
  the one that is true. A weather system that puts fog on the vistas
  (Session 17) should put haze under it.
- **The goat at a border is small.** Two units of drawing at the edge
  of a frame, and its whole job is to be noticed stopping. The bleat
  carries it. The play gate decides whether the bleat is enough.

Noted, not blocking:

- The bull's ring does not read at any distance; it is one loop and
  it is under the muzzle's mark. Leave it: it is the joke played
  straight and nobody needs to see it.
- Nell's scarf is the only accent in the east seam and it is the same
  red as the poppies and the pennants. Correct.

**A land that has WOWED may not be regressed:** the title framing, the
gate stack and the well/poppy cluster stay protected compositions, on
the new frame. The spawn's own frame — the long grass, the bull — is
protected from here too.
