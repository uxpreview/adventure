# Critique 4 — THE COAST

*Session 5, 2026-08-29. The art-director gate per `QUALITY-BAR.md` §2,
run on real screenshots from the running game, desktop (1280×720) and
portrait (390×844).*

**Under review:** LONGSHORE and THE WIDE BLUE — the first two lands in
this world authored ON elevation rather than before it — plus the four
protected lands re-audited, because this session changed shared shading
to get its own cliff to read.

**Standing brief:** *rejects anything that looks procedural, placeholder,
or like a tech demo wearing a style. Blind against Gris, Sable, and
margins itself. The bar: they cannot tell which world had an art budget.*

## Round 1 — NOT YET

> "You have built a dune sea and told me it is a coast. Again.
>
> Start with the thing you were proudest of. You wrote — in a spec, in a
> comment, in the session's own title — that the headland is a piece of
> torn paper the wet edge went round. Then you painted it **the colour
> of sand** and gave it the same wash as the beach it stands on. So what
> I am looking at is a dune. Eleven units of cliff, and it reads as a
> dune, because you told the eye it was sand and the eye believed you
> over the geometry. Your one authority on where the ground is has no
> authority over what the ground is MADE OF, and on a coast that is the
> whole difference between rock and beach.
>
> Second: the boardwalk. It runs east to west. Your camera only ever
> looks north. So the arrival at the coast — the first thing anyone sees
> of this land — is two grey handrails lying across the middle of the
> frame like scaffolding, and a deck drawn at full ink pressure that
> comes out as a black comb. You have laid the one thing in this land
> that is a WALK at ninety degrees to the only direction anyone can
> walk and look at once.
>
> Third: your cliff path. Three twenty-two-unit stand-ups hanging across
> the headland at forty per cent alpha. Session 3 wrote the rule down —
> *a pale standee within sixteen units reads as a flat grey slab* — and
> here are three of them, and they are the largest objects in the frame.
>
> Fourth: THE WIDE BLUE. I will grant you the sandbar; it is a real
> idea and the metaphor pays for it. Then you stood four boats abreast
> at ten units, cropped by the frame edge, at the size of houses.
>
> Fifth, and this is the one that will still be here in three rounds if
> you do not take it seriously: **the shoreline is not a line.** Your sea
> fades in over forty-two units. That is not a coast, it is a gradient
> between two beiges, and no amount of wrack on it will save it."

**Mandatory:**
1. The Holdfast is rock. Paint it.
2. The boardwalk runs north or it is not a boardwalk.
3. The cut's rock is the ground's, not a stand-up's.
4. A shoreline is a line.
5. Stage the regatta where a north-looking camera can see it.

## Round 2 — NOT YET

> "The coast has a line now and the point has a colour, and I can see
> the walk. Good. Two things are wrong and one of them is serious.
>
> The serious one: **the point is a thumb print.** You have hatched it —
> correctly, down the fall line, which is what Session 4 fought for —
> but you hatched a DOME, and a dome's fall line rotates through three
> hundred and sixty degrees, so the strokes come out as concentric
> arcs. It reads as woodgrain. It reads as a fingerprint. It is the only
> thing in the frame that could not have been drawn by a hand, and it is
> forty per cent of the frame.
>
> The other: the promenade is nine floor mats. Your deck panels stop
> short of their own edges and each one is a rectangle lying on sand."

**Mandatory:**
1. The point must have faces. A pen cannot draw down a sphere.
2. A boardwalk is one boardwalk.

## Round 3 — NOT YET

> "You wobbled the radius. Now it is herringbone instead of a thumb
> print, which is not progress, it is the same bug with a different
> haircut.
>
> Think about what you have actually built. The hatch phase is a global
> ramp read through a LOCAL direction. Where the direction is constant
> you get strokes. Where it rotates you get the caustics of a rotating

> that; it is the construction.
>
> And the walls are still there. Third time. Three grey sheets."

**Mandatory:**

2. The stand-up walls go. All of them.

## Round 6 — WOWED

> "Yes.
>
> Stand on the promenade in portrait and look north: planks going away
> under you, the sea holding the left of the frame, four painted huts on
> the right, and at the end of the walk a grey headland with a thread
> across its face and a cairn on top. Nothing in that frame is
> explaining itself. It is a place, and you can see the next place from
> it, which is the only navigation this game has ever wanted.
>
> The cut is the shot. I want to be clear about why, because it is not
> the posts and it is not the rope. It is that **the ground is doing the
> work.** The wall on your right is the page. The drop on your left is
> the page. The reason you are walking single file is the page. There
> is one drawn thing on that ledge — the chisel marks, lying flat, where
> they cannot float — and it is enough, because it is the only thing the
> height field genuinely cannot say. Four rounds of stand-ups had to die
> for that and it was worth every one.
>
> The bar is the best idea in the session and I nearly missed it because
> it looks so obvious in retrospect. Open water is a place you can look
> at and not a place you can be, and you did not solve that with a boat
> or a swimming verb. You solved it with a **miss in the wash** — a strip
> of page the paint never took, which is therefore dry, which is
> therefore walkable. It is the metaphor paying rent. And the step
> changes underfoot when you cross onto it, which is how a player learns
> it without being told anything.
>
> Two things I would still like, and neither is a condition:
> the point's face keeps a small smudge of stroke that is doing less
> work than the clean rock either side of it; and the far north of
> LONGSHORE decays honestly but the last groyne is doing all the work
> alone.
>
> The four protected lands are intact — I checked the avenue climb, the
> square under bunting, and the common's crossroads, because you changed
> shared shading to get here and I did not trust you. The castle scarp
> is better than it was: it takes strokes where it is a wall and none
> where it is a shoulder.
>
> **WOWED.** Ship it."

## What this session changed in shared code, and why the gate allowed it

Three changes to `terrain.ts`'s shading were made for the coast and
re-audited on the four protected lands:

1. **The fall line's direction is taken over a wide stencil and its
   magnitude over the grid's own.** On a planar scarp the two agree, so
   Greyweather is untouched; on a face whose gradient goes to zero at
   its brow the wide reading stops picking up cockle.
2. **The stored magnitude is scaled by the fall line's COHERENCE** — how
   much a point's fall line agrees with the landform's. Brows, corners
   and saddles, where the stroke field turns into caustics, now read as
   gentle and take no strokes.
3. **The hatch gate moved from 0.36 to 0.62.** This is the fix for the
   chevrons on sand, and it is not a new rule: it is Session 4's rule
   ("hatching is for cliffs") implemented at a number that means it.

Plus: the phase wander runs at a sixth of the stroke pitch instead of at
the same frequency (bundles drift, strokes stay parallel), the pitch is
open enough that a fifty-unit face gets twenty strokes rather than
eighty, and the crossing pass is coarse enough that the two sets cannot
beat against each other.
