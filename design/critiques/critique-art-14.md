# Art-director critique #14 — THE NEW CAST, EAST AND SOUTH

*2026-09-05, Session 20. Subject: the four re-opened lands — THE
BLEACH FLATS (the pattern in the Pale and the visitors in it, by day and
by night), GREYLINE CITY (the cart, the cups, the dog and its paws, the
bin, the pavement's card and the third lane), THE CUBICLE MILE (the
studio, the intercept, the stickies, the square flock, the chair, the
board's card), MAPLE COURT (the low dog, the ball, the chairs' card,
the gap, the light off and the street going dark) — and, inside them,
**four drawings that are the owner's own**, judged first on whether
they are the owner's drawings and second on whether they belong on
this sheet. Judged on real screenshots from the shipping camera
(1280×720 + 390×844), reviewed blind against Gris, Sable and margins
per QUALITY-BAR §2. Contact sheet: `shots-s20`
(`tools/shoot-session20.mjs`, 29 framings, both rigs, at the hour each
thing is on, with a DO table that pokes, pushes, peels, kicks, rides
and opens the cards). Verbatim, all rounds.*

*This is the fifth art gate under `QUALITY-BAR` §3's permission to
regress. The protected framings are `diff-sheets`' to report and the
numbers are in `SESSIONS.md`; what this judges is whether three
present-day lands that were arrangements are places with people in
them, whether the Flats' one elsewhere-thing is absurd by day and
frightening by night without a word of explanation, and whether four
notebook sketches survive the ballpoint.*

## Round 1 — NOT YET

The four drawings are the owner's. The dachshund is two lines and a
flag of a tail with a black nose at the end of a wedge, and the
neckerchief is shaded the way the sketch shades it; at three units on
the green it is unmistakably the sketch and unmistakably a dog. The
one-eyed thing is the best drawing in the session: the zigzag egg, the
almond eye with its heavy pupil, the grin with one tooth, the two
boots pointing opposite ways — and three of them standing in a ruled
scorch on the flattest ground in the world, turning to look at you, is
exactly *absurd, played completely straight*. The sheep are squares
with a black head hung off one corner, and six of them parked one to a
bay in a car park is the joke the owner's painting was already telling.
The spotted dog sits in its bow tie beside the cart with its tail
curled on the pavement and it reads as a dog that has been told to
stay. **The drawings pass. Seven frames do not, and five of them are
the sheet's fault.**

1. **Eleven of twenty-nine desktop frames were the loader.** `fresh()`
   waited for the title veil with a twenty-five second cap, and in
   this sandbox the loader's tween outlasts it; every frame shot in
   the first half-minute after a fresh page was INKLANDS / *inking the
   sheet*. The shoot list waits for `.loader.gone` first now, ninety
   seconds, then the title. A tooling fault older than this session,
   met because this sheet opens five fresh pages.
2. **The barista's dog was never in the walk-in frame.** At 6.86 the
   routine has it sixty units up mill lane; the frame at the junction
   looked at the barista's empty corner. The frame is at 6.9 from the
   junction's north-east, and the dog comes down the lane into it with
   the paws behind it.
3. **The pavement's card was answered before it was offered.** The
   frame stood the walker within nine units of the man for the four
   seconds of settle a frame takes, and the wait resolved the way
   Session 13 built it — so the press opened the pavement's note and
   the card was gone. Two fixes, one for the sheet and one for the
   world: the frame stands ten units off him; and **the four seconds
   do not count while a note or a card is up** (`UI.chrome.open`),
   because a walker reading is not a walker deciding — a player who
   opens the card and thinks about it for five seconds must not have
   the first door taken for them by the thinking.
4. **The chair could not be sat on from where a walker stops.** Its
   reach was two and six tenths and a walker who walks up to a chair
   stops about two and three quarters from its centre. Reach is three
   and six tenths. And its home was in the studio's own footprint: it
   rolled eight units and stopped at the table. It lives on the spur
   road's verge now, where a ride north crosses the forecourt and a
   ride east goes down the mile.
5. **The square flock was small.** At two and three tenths wide the
   sheep read as blank cards with a dot; the black head, which is the
   whole silhouette, was four pixels. Two and nine tenths, and the ram's
   horns and the long-horned one's face read from the road.
6. **Your lane was too faint to be yours.** Five passes of a pale
   stroke at eleven per cent on grey paving was nothing at the
   sheet's distance. Seven passes at twenty, wider, and it is a path.
7. **The low dog sat where it was when you left the green, not at the
   edge.** A walker who ran west left it thirteen units behind, sat, and
   out of the frame, which is the goat's rule not seen. It goes on
   toward a walker who has left, as far as the green's edge, and sits
   there looking after you — the rule as the goat does it.

**What already reads, round one:**

- **The pattern in the pan** (01): three squares inside each other,
  turned, eight rules from the middle with ticks on them, scorched
  into cracked ground, with the four wireless fence posts standing
  across it and the walker at its edge. It is the only ruled thing
  for eighty units and it reads as something that did not happen
  here. **The visitors by day** (02) is the session's picture: three
  of them in the rules, one big and two small, all looking at you.
- **The lights over the pan** (04, 05): three pale eyes at roof
  height over a dark pan, drifting, with the pattern under them and
  the palms black against the sky. The bodies are gone and the frame
  says so without a note.
- **The cups by noon** (07): a striped canopy that says COFFEE, a
  row of paper cups on the counter with scrawl on them, the barista
  with a hand up, the dog sat beside the wheel. The three of them are
  one thing.
- **The persona and the map** (13): DENNIS legible on an easel at
  eight units, the map beside it with its red line going down, three
  lanyards round a table, the atrium's doors behind. **The intercept**
  (14) is the frame the brief asked for: Dennis at the board at eight
  with a clipboard standing beside him. **The stickies by four** (15):
  the shelter's glass is a wall of yellow, pink and blue over D. HALL,
  and one on the apron in **peel one off** (16).
- **The chair ridden** (19): the walker in the chair, the chair's back
  in front of the body, thirty units up the forecourt from where it
  was, with the map board it stopped at.
- **The chairs' card** (26) and **the gap cut** (27): the hedge has a
  notch and Greyweather's ridge is in it. **The light off** (28): the
  one lit porch on the court is dark and the house beside it is still
  lit, which is the first day.
- **The low dog on the green** (23): the sketch, at three units, with
  the ball at its feet and the walker's prints behind.

## Round 2 — PASS, with the cart's label high and the paws small

The second sheet, both rigs, thirty framings. The seven are closed and
two more were found on the way and closed too: **the barista and the
dog walked in behind the shop row** (the straight line from the north
end's door to the cart runs through the row's drawing at x 128–146,
so the walk-in was a dog seen through a shopfront; they come down mill
lane and turn at the junction now, in and out), and **the chair's ride
was photographed from where the walker had been** (the camera walks
twelve behind and a thirty-unit ride outruns it; the frame settles
four seconds after the roll and rides east down the mile toward THE
CAR PARK, which is the ride the toy is named for).

**What reads, round two:**

- **The dog turns at the junction** (08b): the barista ahead with a
  hand up, the dog a stride behind in its stride drawing, the bin on
  its side at the corner — E19 seen before it is a prompt — and the
  four green lights. On the phone the same three are in a column.
- **The visitors by day** (02) on the phone: one of them fills the
  narrow frame with the pattern's rules going past its boots and THE
  PALE lettered beside the signpost; the other two are a peek away.
  **The lights** (05) on the phone: two of the three, and the pattern
  under them; the third is behind the camera and that is what makes
  it worse.
- **The cups by noon** (07) on the phone: the dog at the left wheel,
  the cups on the counter, the barista at the right, THE CART's label
  over the canopy and the district card above it. The cups' names are
  scrawl at this distance and legible at arm's length, which is what
  a paper cup is; the note is the list.
- **The chair ridden** (19): the walker's head over the chair's back,
  the bays running away, THE CAR PARK ahead, forty units from where the
  chair was. It is the mile's toy and the frame says why.
- **The low dog at the green's edge** (25): sat at the line with the
  ball beside it and the walker seven units past it on the grass, and
  the walker's prints between them. The goat's rule at the size of a
  lawn, in one picture.
- **The square flock** (17) on the phone: two in frame, one with its
  head up at you.

**Passed, not praised:**

- **THE CART's label** sits on the shop row behind the cart rather
  than over the canopy: the skyline's nearest top within the label's
  clearance is the row's. A `labelHeight` on the POI, or the cart's
  own cell, is Session 21's line.
- **The paws** are stamps a quarter of a unit long on a pavement
  photographed from ten units, and they read as a dotted line behind
  the dog rather than as paws. At arm's length they are paws. A
  larger stamp would be a larger dog.
- **The persona and the map** are legible on the desktop at eight
  units and a scrawl on the phone at the same distance; DENNIS reads
  on both. Accurate, for a persona.
- **The studio's three at dusk** are the folk's size and the grade
  takes half of them — Session 19's note, unchanged, and on the
  standing debts.

**Verdicts:**

- **THE BLEACH FLATS — RE-EARNED.** The Pale was cracked ground and
  four posts; it is a place something came to, with three of the
  owner's things standing in it by day and three lights over it by
  night, and not one word about what they are. The land's thesis —
  *the answer is elsewhere* — has its one elsewhere-thing, and it is
  stuck too.
- **GREYLINE CITY — RE-EARNED.** The junction has a person who stands
  still on purpose, a dog that does too, a second list of names, a bin
  that the pigeons go up for, and a wait that is a card with a lane of
  your own on the far side of it. The city walked round one man; now
  it has to walk round a cart as well.
- **THE CUBICLE MILE — RE-EARNED.** The forecourt has a sprint in it
  and the stop has a wall of stickies and an intercept at eight, which
  is the owner's field drawn straight; the overflow has six square
  sheep in it, which is the joke the owner painted; the chair is the
  best toy in the fun pass. The board is a card.
- **MAPLE COURT — RE-EARNED.** The green has a dog on it that is the
  owner's dog, and a ball, and a line it will not cross. The three
  chairs are a card, and one of its doors turns the street dark a house
  a day.

**Not judged here:** whether any of it is fun, and whether the four
drawings are the owner's to the owner's eye. Both are the play
sheet's (`design/play-sheets/session-20.md`), and the owner's.
