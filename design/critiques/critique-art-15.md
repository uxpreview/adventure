# Art-director critique #15 — THE SECOND DOOR

*2026-09-05, Session 21. Subject: the five lands re-opened for their
second door — THE KINGDOM OF BRIM (the belfry's card, the clock set to
either hand, the lamps lit in daylight, a market called round a stall
that never opens), SPLITROCK CANYON (the trestles' card, the boat
righted, the sea told: the boat dull, the marks weathered, Holt on the
rim), THE BLEACH FLATS (the catch's card, the lid off, the can carried
down and filled and carried up and emptied in, the track grown over by
day and empty by night), THE PENWOOD (the tarn's card from outside the
twenty, the boat without its oar, the oar in hand, the twelfth at the
lean, Brack still facing the water), THE HARROW DOWNS (the table's
card, the setting cleared, sat at a table for one) — and THE 8:15
reading the doors: the cans on the Flats' platform, the pots on
Longshore's, nobody on Splitrock's, and the morning after. Judged on
real screenshots from the shipping camera (1280×720 + 390×844),
reviewed blind against Gris, Sable and margins per QUALITY-BAR §2.
Contact sheet: `shots-s21` (`tools/shoot-session21.mjs`, 31 framings,
both rigs, a DO table that opens the cards, carries the can and the
oar, sits, and warps the train). Verbatim, all rounds.*

*This is the sixth art gate under `QUALITY-BAR` §3's permission to
regress. The protected framings are `diff-sheets`' to report and the
numbers are in `SESSIONS.md`; what this judges is whether a second
door's cost is a VISIBLE, PERMANENT change at the scale of its reward
— the bar `THE-FUN-PASS` §6 sets — in five lands where the reward was
already built, and whether a train stopping for two cans reads as an
ending and not as a bug.*

## Round 1 — NOT YET

The five cards read. Three doors on Brim's fits the card at both
widths (the first label wraps to two lines in portrait and is the
better for it: LET THE BELL RING THE LAMPS' / HOUR). The costs that
are drawings pass first time: the clock with both hands on eleven is
two lines and the difference is legible from the yard; **Marget's
stall shut under bunting in an open market is the best frame in the
session** — a bare cloth on a table at the head of a square full of
striped awnings and people, and nothing points at it; the marks gone
to dashes on the wall face with the boat dull under them is the
canyon's whole wait undone in one standpoint, and the walker at the
foot of the frame is the right size for it; Holt on the rim beside
the house is a small figure where there used to be nobody, which is
the point; the lid off is a black mouth; the track grown over is scrub
where the band was; the boat without its oar is three thwarts and
nothing across them, and the oar leaning on the hull is the palest
thing in the Penwood, which is what the tarn boat's oar has always
been; twelve at the lean, and the twelfth is the long one at the right
end and nothing points at it; Brack with his back to the lens at the
ring with the walker holding an oar he has not turned round for; a
table laid for one with the walker sat at it and Joan at the other
end at noon. **Seven frames do not pass, and four of them are the
sheet's fault.**

1. **The cans were behind a house.** The Flats' stop stands on main
   street at (40, 198) and the plot at (41, 211) stands between it and
   the pavement; from the pavement the train's thing was behind a
   roof and the frame showed a carriage with its doors open on nothing,
   which is the OTHER ending. Probed from the road's own verge, north
   of the house at (34, 206.5), the cans stand in front of the carriage
   beside the walker and the house fades under B2's rule. Both sheets
   frame it from there.
2. **The lamps lit in daylight were not lit.** The halo at half
   opacity on a page that is already light is nothing; at five in the
   afternoon the square looked like five in the afternoon. The halo is
   at full when the clock is wrong, and a warm blot with rays on a
   lamp standard in daylight is exactly the drawing of a man who went
   by the clock.
3. **The table's card had no title.** The seat has no note to borrow
   one from and the card lettered an empty line. `choice.title`.
4. **The walker was still holding the can in the grown-over frames
   and the oar at the lean.** The DO scripts stopped one press short
   of EMPTY IT IN and STAND IT WITH THE OTHERS, so the two frames
   meant to show the cost paid showed it still in hand. Sheet fault;
   the scripts press.
5. **The oar at the lean reads only if you count.** Twelve looms in a
   row at ten units and the right one is the one with the narrow
   blade; on a phone that is a difference of four pixels. Noted, not
   blocking: `THE-WAITS` §7 says nothing points at it, and the note
   says one of them is right, and a player who reads the note and
   counts gets it. That is the design.
6. **The pots on Longshore's platform are small.** Seven pots stacked
   is a heap a unit and a half wide beside a carriage twenty-two wide;
   from the road it is a dark cluster between the wheels. Noted, not
   blocking: it is the size a stack of pots is, and the morning-after
   frame, with no carriage behind it, is the one that carries it.
7. **The Penwood's card stands in a frame the pines have half eaten.**
   From twenty-three units the tarn is a black disc under the card's
   corner and the boat is a stroke at the edge of the water; the card
   is legible and the land behind it is atmosphere. Passed as the
   frame the card is offered in — a walker at that distance IS in the
   pines — and the boat's own frame at (146, −177) does the boat.
