# Art-director critique #11 — LIFE

*2026-09-04, Session 17. Subject: the four multipliers of `THE-FUN-PASS`
§9 — unnamed inhabitants with routines, the animals, the weather, and
the night — in all twelve lands at once. Judged on real screenshots
from the shipping camera (1280×720 + 390×844), reviewed blind against
Gris, Sable and margins per QUALITY-BAR §2. Contact sheet: `shots-s17`
(`tools/shoot-session17.mjs`, 59 framings, both rigs, at the hours the
routines are out, the moment the animals react, and in each weather).
Verbatim, all rounds.*

*This is the second art gate under `QUALITY-BAR` §3's permission to
regress, and the first with every land in scope. What it judges is not
a composition but a CLAIM: that a land which was a diorama is now a
place with business of its own, and that none of the business reads as
a screensaver. The protected framings are `diff-sheets`' to report; the
numbers are in `SESSIONS.md`.*

## Round 1 — NOT YET

The premise holds — a figure at a distance in one of seven postures
reads as a person, and a routine reads as a routine the moment there
is a second one in frame. Four things are wrong enough to stop the
round, and three of them are the same lesson.

1. **The dog is standing in the river.** Its home was authored at the
   drove's mouth, which is across the water from every road out of the
   Downs, and a `Follower` cannot find a bridge: it noticed the walker,
   changed posture, and never moved. The second co-walker — the one
   that teaches the rule — never left the spot it was born on. *(Moved
   to the field gate on the east road, north of the river, where every
   road out of the land is reachable without crossing water. The bank
   was probed, not assumed.)*
2. **The cat is in the air.** Authored on the orchard's paddock fence,
   which is drawn edge-on to a north-looking lens, so the cat floated
   over nothing; moved to the town wall, it floated a unit and a half
   above the wall's drawn top, because the parapet is drawn at seven
   tenths of the standee's height and the cat was seated on the
   standee's edge. *(On the parapet, in front of the wall's plane.)*
3. **The dog is the size of a calf.** Two units wide, at the walker's
   heel, between the walker and the lens — the co-walker's own
   composition puts it in the foreground, which is the one place a
   drawing may not be generous. *(Three quarters of the size.)*
4. **The lying bull has legs.** The night's drawing folded its legs as
   two knee strokes at the bottom edge and the mass stood at leg
   height above the grass; from the shipping camera it read as the
   same bull standing lower. A lying animal has no legs. *(Redrawn: the
   mass on the ground, the haunch and the foreleg as lines INSIDE it,
   the tail along the grass, the horns the only thing that is up.)*

And three notes that are not fixes:

- **The sentry is a sentry.** A figure with a pole walking the brow of
  the curtain wall at dusk, small, above the barbican, in frame from
  the avenue's foot. It is the first thing on Greyweather's wall that
  has ever moved and it earns the wall.
- **The heron earns the tarn.** One vertical at the water's edge in a
  land drawn in verticals, and the only thing in the wood that goes
  down to the water. When it goes up and croaks the tarn is briefly a
  place something lives, which the note has been saying since Session
  1 and nothing had agreed with.
- **The rain is right on the desktop.** Short dark strokes falling, the
  ink of every mark bled a little down the page, the horizon a shade
  nearer. It is the medium getting wet and not a particle effect; the
  storm's dark takes it further and the flash is a room lit for a
  moment. **On a phone it is untested by any eye** — the play sheet
  asks.

## Round 2 — PASSED, with four passed-not-praised

The four fixes above re-shot; the whole sheet re-shot in both rigs.

**The unnamed read as people, and the second one makes the first a
routine.** The carter with the handcart on the king's road at a
quarter to eight is a person going somewhere; the three under the oaks
at half past ten are an argument because one of them is a stride
nearer the swing than the other two, and it changes hands. The rush in
Greyline doubles the street and the street is the same street. The
cutters at the failing edge are two figures bent over stumps with a
third thing between them, and they read as work.

**The animals react, and the reaction is the drawing.** The herd's
heads come up before the herd moves. The dog trots at the walker's
heel and sits at the Downs' edge looking after them, and the sit is
the rule said in one posture. The seals are four grey heaps on the bar
until you are nine units off, and then they are not there. The thing
under the Wide Blue is a back and a fin and the water going white,
fifty units north of the bar's end, for ten seconds, and it is the one
drawing in the game made to frighten; it does.

**The weather is a system and reads as one.** In fog the four lures
are gone and the signpost points at nothing you can see, and the
crossroads is the crossroads without its reason — which is the
correct picture for a fog and the reason day zero has none. In a wind
the corn and the grass lean together and the mill's sails go round in
two minutes rather than ten. The storm at eleven on the second night
is the darkest frame in the game.

**The night is a different game.** The bull down in the grass with its
horns the only thing up; the deep pines with three bats and nothing
else moving; Holt's window a warm square you can steer the channel
by, which was one warm pixel at forty units for six sessions; the
guard's lantern in the Cubicle Mile's car park, past every light that
is on for nobody.

**Passed, not praised:**

1. **The lamplighter is a dim figure at dusk.** His round is five past
   seven to a quarter to eight so that every protected 19.6 framing
   keeps all four lamps lit, and at that hour the lamps' own glow
   (`clock.lamp`) is a quarter of its night value. The routine is
   right; the picture of it is faint. Moving the round later moves two
   protected framings, and it is the owner's call.
2. **The figures are small.** A person is 1.9 units tall and most
   routines are met at fifteen to thirty units, where a figure is a
   posture and a hat. That is the house style — nobody has a face —
   and it is also why a routine needs a second figure or a prop (a
   cart, a rod, a pole) to read at all. Every routine that reads best
   has one.
3. **The shut shutters and the lit panes share a row.** Both textures
   are hung a hair in front of the terrace; where a window is lit and
   shuttered the shutter wins, which is right, but at half past ten
   there are lit windows beside shuttered ones and the eye reads two
   states of one town at once. It is what a town looks like at half
   past ten. Recorded because it was noticed.
4. **The Common's fog frame is the strongest frame on the sheet and
   the one the opening cannot have.** The lures are the first hour's
   answer to *where do I go*; in a fog there is no answer. Day zero is
   calm by law and this note is why.

**Verdict re-earned.** The `diff-sheets` numbers are in `SESSIONS.md`:
which protected framings moved, and by how much, and that day zero is
bit-identical at both protected hours wherever no routine or animal
stands in the frame.

**A land that has WOWED may not be regressed:** the title framing, the
Common's seven, Brim's four, Greyweather's four, the coast's five and
the sheet's three are all in `diff-sheets`' set, and every one of them
is shot at noon and at 19.6 on day zero, which the weather keeps calm
on purpose.
