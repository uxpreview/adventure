# Critique 8 — THE NOW

*Session 13, 2026-08-31. The art-director gate per `QUALITY-BAR.md` §2,
run on real screenshots from the running game, desktop (1280×720) and
portrait (390×844).*

**Under review:** MAPLE COURT and GREYLINE CITY — their places, their
waits (VAL, and THE MAN AT THE JUNCTION), the two ends of
`THE-STRANGERS` S3, and **`THE-LINE.md` §3.2's rim composition**, which
is an authoring brief addressed to this session and the riskiest un-shot
frame in the game.

**Sheet:** `tools/shoot-now.mjs`, twenty framings per viewport on
Session 9's harness clock (twelve game seconds of settle, the chrome
swept, the bearing pinned), plus `tools/shoot-textures.mjs` on a new
prop box of thirty-one drawings.

**Standing brief:** *rejects anything that looks procedural, placeholder,
or like a tech demo wearing a style. Blind against Gris, Sable, and
margins itself. The bar: they cannot tell which world had an art
budget.*

**And one thing was shot before anything was placed**, per the prompt:
the rim. It is the frame every other decision in MAPLE COURT was made
against, and shooting it first is the reason the land has a shape.

---

## Round 0 — THE RIM, BEFORE A SINGLE PROP WAS MOVED

> "Stand at the end of the road and look north. §3.2 promises *two
> hundred units of dead straight road running away from you into haze,
> three units below your feet, with nothing standing on it — you cannot
> see where it ends, you can see that it does not stop.*
>
> **There is a castle at the end of it.**
>
> Not a real one. It is the meadow's pencil keep, the false-perspective
> poster Session 2 stood at (−52, −52) with the fog turned off, and its
> fade has a near bound and no far one, so it ramps up as the walker
> comes south and then holds at full opacity for the next two hundred
> and fifty units. From the south rim of the world it sits at the
> vanishing point of the king's road, and it answers the one question
> this composition exists to leave open.
>
> **Nobody has ever shot this frame.** The vista has been wrong since
> Session 2 and it took a session that was told to shoot the rim first
> to find out.
>
> Behind that: the road is a tan smear that gives up at sixty units, a
> signpost stands five units off the axis in the middle of the
> corridor, and there are houses on both sides all the way down."

**Fixed before anything was authored.** The vista lets go over the first
forty units of Maple Court. The signpost is gone. And the road got its
edges — see round 2.

## Round 1 — NOT YET, and it is the PROP BOX

*Shot first, always. The world was not rendered at all.*

> "Three of your thirty-one drawings are doing nothing at all and one of
> them is the one the session is about.
>
> **THE WORN PAVEMENT IS INVISIBLE.** You have drawn sixty-two pale
> walks on a transparent canvas and you are going to lay it on a page
> the colour of paper. Pale on pale is nothing. Look at your own sheet:
> the cell is empty.
>
> **Your hedge is a green card with bubbles on it.** The leaf mass stops
> two thirds of the way down and the bottom third is flat wash, and the
> canvas is 320 × 112 on a fifteen-unit run, so every scribble is
> stretched into an oval.
>
> **Your bench is a stack of horizontal lines.** There is no seat, no
> back and no frame — and the ONE detail that makes it that bench, the
> arm in the middle that stops anybody lying on it, is a squiggle in the
> middle of the lines.
>
> Also: your far skyline is heavier than the towers in front of it, your
> hopscotch is invisible, your lawn is a barcode, and every roof in the
> street is the same grey."

**Fixed.** The wear was rebuilt to contain its own stone and take it
away again (see round 3); the hedge went to 512 × 96 with leaf all the
way down and cut faces you can see; the bench got a body.

## Round 2 — NOT YET: the road does not read, and the walker is inside a house

> "The rim is better and it is not finished. **Two hundred units of road
> is still a smear.** You are trying to read a road from its SURFACE, and
> at a hundred units its surface is four pixels wide. A road at distance
> is two converging lines. Give it kerbs.
>
> And your street does not work: from the standpoint you chose for the
> money shot, there is a house eight units in front of the camera and it
> is the whole left half of the frame. Look at where your walker
> actually stands before you place anything on the plot beside it."

**Fixed.** `kerbRunDecal` — two converging lines, a decal so it may lie
in the corridor where nothing may stand, and it stops where the survey
did. The court moved fifteen units west and its houses were re-laid
round the circle so the axis a walker looks up is clear from the street
to the porch.

---

## Round 3 — NOT YET: the wait is invisible, and everyone is a giant

> "**Your man is not in the frame.** Then I find him: he is sitting on
> the bench, in the shot that is supposed to show him standing, because
> a framing eleven rows earlier in your own shoot script handed the
> walker the knowledge that resolves him. Your sheet cannot show me both
> states of a wait if the first one you photograph is the second one.
>
> **The pavement is still ordinary stone.** You fixed the drawing and
> then laid two plain paving squares on the corners of the crossing,
> and decals draw in the order they were made. You covered the wait.
>
> **And everybody in both lands is a foot taller than the walker.** The
> man is 2.75 units and the commuters are 2.6, against a walker of
> about 1.9. This is a game about a small figure in a large world and
> you have filled its two newest lands with giants."

**Fixed.** Knowledge framings moved to the end of the shoot list; the
paving squares that overlapped the wear are gone and the wear is one
decal that carries its own stone; every person in both lands is
1.95–2.15 units.

---

## Round 4 — NOT YET, and it is one word: PALE

> "The wear is still not there and you have now spent three rounds on
> it. The argument in your own comment is the problem: *wear is pale,
> because stone that a million shoes have been over loses its ink.*
> True. And this page is ALREADY the colour of paper, so a pale mark on
> it is nothing at all.
>
> Go and look at a pavement. What a hundred years of shoes leaves is a
> POLISH, and a polish is DARKER than the stone round it.
>
> While you are in there: your paving slabs are three and a half metres
> across. At that size the lanes are two gaps in a very coarse grid.
> Make them a metre and a half and the missing jointing becomes a
> shape."

**Fixed, and it is the correction the land turned on.** The lanes take a
dark polish and the slabs are slab-sized. The wear reads in both
viewports, at twelve units and at thirty.

## Round 5 — NOT YET: two flat cards and a floating skyline

> "Small things, and all three are the same mistake in different
> clothes: **a flat cutout seen edge-on is a card standing in a field.**
>
> The hedge's two returns are eleven units long, turned ninety degrees,
> and the camera only ever looks north — so they are two green
> rectangles hanging in a garden. June's fence is five panels laid
> north–south along the border, and it comes back as one grey streak
> with a woman standing beside it. *A thing you walk ALONG runs
> north–south; a thing you LOOK AT runs east–west.* Session 5 lost two
> rounds to this with a boardwalk.
>
> And your city's far skyline has the fog turned off, so from a rise in
> MAPLE COURT there are five pale blocks hanging in the air over the
> river with nothing hazing them. A skyline IS the haze layer."

**Fixed.** The returns are three units; the fence runs across the end of
the road with a single return post on the border; the skyline takes the
fog like everything else, and two of its panels moved east so they are
not visible from the neighbouring land at all.

## Round 6 — NOT YET, and this one was self-inflicted

> "You have hatched the fold in THE HOLLOW and it is worse than the
> airbrush it replaced. Forty identical decals at even spacing is an
> ARRAY — in the one quadrant of this world that already has a harrow
> in it — and it reads as corduroy. And your retaining walls are five
> units tall standing on a slope, so their feet are in the air at both
> ends, which is the rule Session 11 wrote in capitals: **nothing stands
> on a scarp.**
>
> Take both out. Put short panels at the toe where the ground is nearly
> flat, and then say plainly, in the log, that the fold is still shaded
> rather than drawn and that it is not a land's problem to fix."

**Taken.** The hollow ships with walls at the toe of the cut, and the
debt is recorded in `PLAN.md` and in the spec.

## Round 7 — **WOWED**

> "Yes. Both of them, and the pair is the point.
>
> **MAPLE COURT.** I did not expect a suburb in ballpoint to be worth
> looking at and it is, because you found a rule for it: *every mark
> closes.* The hedge is a loop, the lawn is a kerb that comes back to
> itself, and the street is a dead end with a circle on the end of it —
> a road you can walk the whole of and finish where you started. It is
> the street's belief drawn rather than said, and I did not need the
> note to feel it.
>
> **And the dusk shot is the best composition in the session.** Walking
> north up the court, eleven dark houses, one porch light, and the old
> world on the horizon behind it. That is a picture with a fact in it,
> and the fact is the wait: *the light is not for the people who left.*
> The other games on my desk light the whole street for atmosphere. You
> lit one house and it says more.
>
> **THE END OF THE SURVEY.** Two hundred units of straight empty road
> running into haze between two kerb lines that stop, three pegs, and
> gravel. No note. It is the most disciplined frame in this game and it
> is disciplined by SUBTRACTION, which is the hardest way to do it — and
> the density gradient that gets you there (dense at the Common, big
> plots, then kerbs and driveways with no houses, then nothing) is a
> density that MEANS something, which is what the bar has been asking
> for since §4 was written.
>
> **GREYLINE CITY.** The frame-top ceiling as the subject is the right
> idea and it is executed: near towers cropped, far towers whole, and
> the difference is what makes it a city. The opposite drawing rule
> against Maple Court is doing real work — I can tell which land I am in
> with the colour off.
>
> **And the junction is a wait I can read without being told.** The
> stone worn pale in two curves with a clean patch the size of a person
> in the middle of them; the man standing in it; the bench twenty units
> behind him that has no wear in front of it at all. Then you stand
> still for four seconds and the same frame has him on the bench and the
> wear exactly where it was. **That is a fable told in a paving slab**,
> and the note beside it has the sense not to mention him.
>
> **What is passed and not praised, and you should write it down:**
> THE HOLLOW. The bins and the fire escape are good and the ground they
> are standing in is shaded rather than drawn. You knew, you tried, you
> made it worse, you put it back and you said so. That is the correct
> outcome and it is still not a beautiful frame.
>
> **And one thing I want on the record because it is the strongest
> single move in the session and it is invisible:** the castle at the
> end of the survey road. Nobody was going to find that except a session
> that shot the rim before it placed anything, and it had been in the
> shipped build for eleven sessions."

**Verdict: WOWED**, with THE HOLLOW noted-not-blocking and carried in
`PLAN.md` as a standing debt.

## What this gate cost, and what it produced

Seven rounds; four of them found faults in DRAWINGS rather than in
placement, and `shoot-textures.mjs` found three of those four in four
seconds without rendering the world at all. The two rounds that needed
the world found things no texture sheet could have: a decal covered by
another decal, and a knowledge state leaking forward through a shoot
script.

**And the machine gained one assertion.** `tools/check-sightline.mjs` —
`THE-LINE` §3.2's protected corridor, read off every prop in the world,
with a drawing counted as inside it if any part of the DRAWING is. The
shipped draft broke that rule twice and no check in this repository
would ever have said so.
