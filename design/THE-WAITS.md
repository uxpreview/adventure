# THE WAITS — twelve fables, one per land

*Session 7. `design/QUESTS.md` Tier 1 is the brief: one named person,
two or three places that mean something different once you know, **a
turn**, and a visible permanent change. `design/STORY.md` §3 fixes what
each land waits for and what that makes it believe, and it is not
re-opened here.*

*The turn is the bar and it is the highest one on the list — Witcher's
bar, twelve times. **A wait without a turn is a description.** Each of
the twelve below was written until it had one, and four of them were
thrown away and rewritten because the first version was a mood.*

---

## 0. How a wait resolves, with no dialogue and no UI

One mechanism, and it is the whole content system:

> **A wait resolves when the player, holding the right knowledge,
> arrives at the right place. Nothing asks them. Nothing confirms it.
> The world simply does the thing, once, and keeps doing it.**

No prompt, no menu, no "you have learned…", no acceptance step. The
player carries a FACT out of one land, walks into another, and a stall
opens or a light comes on or a chair is moved. That is
WORLD-SYSTEMS §6 stated as an implementation: *places open because you
now know what you are looking at, not because a boolean flipped* — and
the boolean, when there has to be one, is a piece of knowledge with a
name a human could read.

Three consequences, all deliberate:

- **You can resolve a wait by accident**, on your way somewhere else,
  and that is correct. The world was always open.
- **You can walk past a resolved wait and never know it was a wait.**
  Also correct.
- **Nothing anywhere counts them.** No list, no tally, no percentage
  (QUESTS §7). The map records where you have been, not what you have
  finished.

### The knowledge each wait turns on

| land | needs | earned by |
|---|---|---|
| CASTLE GREYWEATHER | `fact:brim-red` | seeing Marget's stall cloth in Brim |
| THE KINGDOM OF BRIM | `fact:brim-hour` | reading the belfry clock at dusk, when the lamps settle the hour |
| MAPLE COURT | `name:greyweather` | standing under the castle |
| SPLITROCK CANYON | `route:the-river` | rowing the river, salt to source |
| THE BLEACH FLATS | `fact:the-fold` | walking the crease, both faces |
| LONGSHORE | `name:the-mark` | reaching the mark buoy in the Wide Blue |
| THE PENWOOD | `fact:the-tarn` | going to the tarn, which is the whole of it |
| THE WIDE BLUE | `route:the-bar` | walking the sandbar to its far end |
| THE COMMON | `fact:the-fourth-name` | reading the timetable at the 8:15 stop |
| THE HARROW DOWNS | *(nothing)* | **the only one that needs no knowledge.** You sit down |
| GREYLINE CITY | *(nothing)* | you stop walking, in the one land where nobody does |
| THE CUBICLE MILE | `route:the-line` | walking the line, gate to car park |

**Two of the twelve need nothing at all**, and they are the two that are
about the walker rather than about a land. That is not an oversight and
it is not symmetry for its own sake: it is the story admitting once,
quietly, in its own architecture, what the walker actually is.

## WAITS & MEMORY

*Waiting for something that has already gone. Warm, and the saddest of
the four.*

---

### 1. CASTLE GREYWEATHER — **WICK**

| | |
|---|---|
| **waits for** | a king who rode out and did not come back |
| **believes** | that loyalty is a thing you go on doing after it stops making sense |
| **register** | the oldest in the world. Nothing here is abbreviated |

**The person.** Wick changes the banners on the avenue, every day,
alone. Nobody has told him the king is not coming back. He knows.

**The three places, and what they become.**

| place | what it looks like | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| **the banner avenue** | a ceremonial approach, kept immaculate for a rider | a laundry line. Four banners, worked in rotation, one down and one up every day for a very long time |
| **THE MOAT POOL** *(built; has a note)* | a moat that was started, got as far as one satisfying pool, and was left | **a dye vat.** It is the only standing water inside the walls and it is why it was dug. The red in it is this week's |
| **THE TOPPLED KING** *(built; has a note)* | a statue that fell and was left where it landed | the one thing Wick will not put right. Setting a fallen king back on his plinth is a decision about whether he is coming, and Wick does not make decisions |

**THE TURN.** The loft has no spares in it. **There have never been any
spares.** There are four banners and there have always been four; Wick
washes and re-dyes and re-hangs the same cloth, and the red has gone
through pink and back to red more times than anyone has counted. He is
not keeping a vigil and he is not grieving. **He is on duty, and nobody
has ever come to relieve him**, and the last order he was given is
still, technically, current.

**The visible, permanent change.** You have been to Brim and seen the
cloth on Marget's stall — the same red, the same dye, the same trade,
a hundred and something years ago. Come back to the avenue holding
that, and the next morning there are **five banners**, and the fifth is
Brim's colours, and it stays up in every later save.

*Which is the first time in the history of this world that two lands
are visible in the same frame, and neither of them crossed anything to
do it.*

**Never says:** that the king is dead. That anyone else is waiting.
That the fifth banner means anything.

---

### 2. THE KINGDOM OF BRIM — **MARGET** *(authored end to end, Session 7)*

| | |
|---|---|
| **waits for** | market day, which has been next week for a while |
| **believes** | that if you keep the ritual exactly, the thing comes back |
| **register** | market-town plain. Practical, slightly short with you |

**The person.** Marget sets the stall out at dawn, lays the cloth, does
not open, and packs it away at dusk. Every day. She has been doing it
long enough that the square has arranged itself around her.

**The three places, and what they become.**

| place | what it looks like | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| **BRIM SQUARE** *(built)* | stalls set, bunting up, a market about to happen | a market that has been about to happen for years. The bunting is not new; it is simply never taken down |
| **THE BELFRY** *(built; has a note)* | a clock whose two hands disagree | the reason. **Market day is called from the cross when the bell strikes the hour**, and nobody in Brim can agree which hour the bell struck |
| **THE MARKET CROSS** *(built)* | a monument | a lectern. It is where the market is called from, and it has not been stood on in living memory |

**THE TURN.** The market has not been cancelled. **It has never been
called.** Marget is not waiting for a market and she is not waiting for
buyers. **She is waiting for one other person in the world to agree
with her about what time it is** — which is why she sets out at dawn,
every day, because dawn is the one hour nobody in Brim argues about.

**What the player has that nobody in Brim has.** A day. You have watched
the light go all the way round; you have seen the lamps come on. Stand
in the belfry yard at dusk and the two hands are not equally wrong —
one of them agrees with the lamps. **That is the fact**, and it is
earned by being somewhere at an hour, which is the only kind of
knowledge this world deals in.

**The visible, permanent change.** Come to the market cross holding it.
The bell rings the hour it actually is; **Marget takes the cloth off
and opens the stall**, and it is open at every hour of every day in
every later save. The bunting, which has been up for a market that
never happened, is finally up for one that did.

**Never says:** which hand was right. That anything was wrong. That the
market was ever cancelled — because it never was.

### 3. MAPLE COURT — **VAL**

| | |
|---|---|
| **waits for** | the people who left |
| **believes** | that leaving is temporary |
| **register** | the most modern warm one. Kettle-and-net-curtains |

**The person.** Val leaves the porch light on. It is on at every hour,
including the ones nobody is awake for.

**The three places, and what they become.**

| place | what it looks like | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| **the porch light** | a light left on for somebody coming home | a light left on for **the street**. A dark house on this road means the family went, and Val will not be the one who makes the road look like that |
| **THE GREEN** *(built; has a note)* | a swing set drawn mid-sway | in use. Not by anyone you will see; the swing is on the green because the green is where you put a swing, and Maple Court keeps its arrangements |
| **the three chairs and the hedge** *(the WORLD-SYSTEMS §10 tableau)* | three chairs facing a hedge, which is a joke about suburbia | three chairs facing a **gap** in a hedge that closed. Through it, on a clear day, you could see Greyweather's ridge — which is what every child on this road grew up looking at, and it is thirty units north of where they all went |

**THE TURN.** Val is not waiting for her people to come back. **She is
holding the street's line** — one lit window on a road of them, so that
the road still counts as lived on. The wait is not about the people who
left at all. It is about the ones who did not, and there is one.

**The visible, permanent change.** You have stood under Greyweather.
Come back holding its name and **the gap is cut back open**, and from
the three chairs the castle ridge is on the skyline of a back garden,
north, in frame, for the first time — and it stays cut.

**Never says:** who left, or where they went, or that they are not
coming. Nobody in Maple Court would be so rude.

**Authoring note for Session 12:** the chairs face **north**, because the
camera only ever looks north and the whole beat is a sightline. The
castle is 300 units up the sheet on the same axis as the line. Do not
put the gap on the east side toward the city, however much the fiction
wants it — a vista you cannot look along is not a vista.

## WAITS & WEATHER

*Waiting on something indifferent that was never coming for you.*

---

### 4. SPLITROCK CANYON — **HOLT**

| | |
|---|---|
| **waits for** | the river that cut this canyon, which is now somebody else's river |
| **believes** | that the world used to be more generous and may be again |
| **register** | short sentences. People out here do not use two words |

**The person.** Holt keeps a boat. It is upside down on trestles at the
top of the dry channel, and it is oiled.

**The three places, and what they become.**

| place | what it looks like | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| **the channel floor** | the bottom of a canyon | a riverbed with nothing in it, and the only flat walk in the land |
| **the marks up the wall** | flood records, the way every dry place has them | **heights.** Read bottom to top they are the boat, the trestles, the shed, the doorstep, the house |
| **the boat** | a boat that is a long way from any water | maintained. Recently |

**THE TURN.** The marks are not a record of floods that happened.
**They are a list, in the order things would float.** Holt has worked
out exactly how much river he would need and it is more than has ever
been here, and he has written it on the wall where he can check it, and
he keeps the boat oiled at the bottom of the list where it would go
first.

He is not remembering water. He is ready for it, which is a different
thing and a much longer one.

**The visible, permanent change.** You have rowed the river, salt to
source, under all three bridges — you are the only thing in this world
that has. Come up the canyon holding the route and Holt takes the boat
off the trestles and sets it **right way up on the channel floor**, in
the dry, and leaves it there. The game never says whether that is
madness or readiness.

**Never says:** where the river went. That it is not coming. That
anybody else has one.

### 5. THE BLEACH FLATS — **AMOS**

| | |
|---|---|
| **waits for** | rain |
| **believes** | that the answer is elsewhere, and that elsewhere owes you |
| **register** | flat, patient, faintly aggrieved |

**The person.** Amos has built a rain-catch: guttering, a fall, a
cistern with a lid on it. It is the only piece of engineering in the
Bleach Flats and it is in good order.

**The three places, and what they become.**

| place | what it looks like | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| **the catch and the cistern** | a system waiting for weather | maintained daily, to a standard nothing here requires |
| **THE OASIS** *(built; has a note)* | green, out here, is a rumour you can stand in | forty units away, and the only water in the land, and it is where the cistern's water comes from |
| **the track between them** | nothing. Bare flats | worn. By one person. Both ways. At night, because it is cooler |

**THE TURN.** The cistern is full. **It has always been full.** It has
never rained here in anyone's memory and Amos carries the water from the
oasis by hand, every night, forty units each way — and there is nobody
out here to fool. He is not faking a rainfall. **He is keeping the thing
that catches rain in working order**, and a full cistern is what working
order looks like, and the day it rains the system will already have been
proved.

**The visible, permanent change.** You have walked the crease — both
faces of the world's one real fold, which is where any water on this
sheet would actually go. Come back holding it and **the lid comes off
the cistern, and stays off.** He has decided to find out. The game does
not say whether that is despair or nerve.

**Never says:** that it will not rain. That he carries the water. That
anybody has noticed.

### 6. LONGSHORE — **PYE**

| | |
|---|---|
| **waits for** | the tide, which comes twice a day and is the most reliable thing anybody here knows |
| **believes** | that waiting is just a shape the day has |
| **register** | coastal, unhurried, comic timing without jokes |

**The person.** Pye rows out at the tide, comes back at the tide, and
keeps pots that catch nothing much.

**The three places, and what they become.**

| place | what it looks like | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| **SHELTER COVE** *(built; has a note)* | a boat pulled up past the wrack line by somebody who has done it a thousand times | correct. Exactly as far as it needs to go and not one foot further, every time, for years |
| **the chain and the padlock** *(the WORLD-SYSTEMS §10 tableau)* | a chained boat with a padlock rusted shut — a small tragedy | **the chain is long enough to row on.** The lock has not mattered since before it rusted. He never once needed to open it |
| **the pots** | seven pots that catch nothing much | seven **marks**. Running the line of them is how a man holds the shape of a day when the only clock is the water |

**THE TURN.** The pots are not for catching. Pye is not a fisherman
having a bad decade; he is a man who arranged his whole life so that
the tide, which is indifferent and enormous and arrives twice a day
whatever anybody does, would have somewhere useful to put him. The
padlock rusted shut is not a misfortune. **It is proof of how well the
arrangement worked.**

**The visible, permanent change.** You have been to the mark — the bell
buoy out in the Wide Blue, which nobody in Longshore has seen. Come back
holding its name and Pye sets an **eighth pot**, further out than any of
the seven, on a bearing he has never rowed. He will not go there. It is
the furthest thing in Longshore's world and it has his mark on it, and
it is there in every later save.

**Never says:** that the pots are empty. That he knows. That eight is
more than seven.

### 7. THE PENWOOD — **BRACK**

| | |
|---|---|
| **waits for** | whatever is in the tarn. Nobody goes near it and nobody will say why |
| **believes** | that some things should be waited out rather than met |
| **register** | very few words, and none of them about the tarn |

**The person.** Brack will not go within forty units of the tarn, and
moves so that he never has his back to it.

**The three places, and what they become.**

| place | what it looks like | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| **THE TARN** *(built; has a note)* | still water, black, with a one-oared boat on it | still water |
| **the forty units** | a fear, kept by a frightened man | **a job.** He has walked its circumference for forty years and it is the most consistent thing in the Penwood |
| **the forest track** *(built — the road on the map you walked in on)* | the way through the wood | **his path.** Worn by one man's caution, hardened into a road, then used by everybody after him, none of whom were afraid of anything |

**THE TURN.** Nobody in the Penwood avoids the tarn. **Everybody
follows the road** — and the road is one man's forty-year circle, worn
into the ground until it became infrastructure. The geography of an
entire land is a single person's superstition that outlived being a
superstition and became the way things are done.

**The visible, permanent change.** You go to the tarn, because you are
the only thing in this world that crosses lines, and here the line is
one man's, which is the harder kind. There is nothing in it. Come back
out holding that and **Brack turns his back on it** — one figure, one
quarter turn, and it is the largest thing that has happened in the
Penwood in forty years.

**The road stays exactly where it is.** A road does not move for a fact.

**Never says:** what was in the tarn. That there was nothing. That the
road is his.

### 8. THE WIDE BLUE — **WREN**

| | |
|---|---|
| **waits for** | the end of a race that has been sailed round the same mark since before anyone was counting |
| **believes** | that the point was never the finish |
| **register** | out of earshot of everything. Everything here is said twice, in case |

**The person.** Wren keeps the mark. Rows out, rings the bell, rows
back. The bell buoy has been ringing since Session 5 and nobody has ever
asked who rings it.

**The three places, and what they become.**

| place | what it looks like | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| **THE MARK** *(built; has a note)* | a buoy the fleet rounds | the only fixed thing in the Wide Blue, and the only thing anybody out here has ever agreed on |
| **the fleet** | boats racing | boats on a course with **no finish line**, because the fleet never agreed where to put one, and agreeing was never the fleet's strong suit |
| **the bell** | timekeeping | the only communication in a land where everyone is on a different boat and nobody can shout that far |

**THE TURN.** It is not a race and it never was. **It is how the fleet
is all in one place once a lap.** They round the mark because rounding
the mark is the only moment any of them is near any of the others, and
they have been calling it a race for so long that calling it anything
else would be a change. Wren is not the timekeeper of a competition.
**Wren is the reason there is a fleet at all.**

**The visible, permanent change.** You have walked the sandbar to its
far end — a hundred and eighty units of dry paper going out, which is
the one thing in this land that is not water. Come back holding the
route and Wren sets **a second mark**, at the bar's far end. Two marks
make a line, and a line has an end, and the fleet is under no obligation
whatsoever to use it. It is simply there now, forever.

**Never says:** that it is not a race. That two marks make a line. That
anything ends.

### 9. THE COMMON — **NELL**

| | |
|---|---|
| **waits for** | whoever comes down the road |
| **believes** | nothing yet — which is why you start here |
| **register** | the plainest in the game. She is where the player learns the house voice |

**The person.** Nell leans on the field gate watching the road. She is
already in the game: the doodle-folk figure at the long fence. She
straightens when somebody comes up the road and settles again when they
are not who it is, and she has been doing that for a while.

**The three places, and what they become.**

| place | what it looks like | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| **THE CROSSROADS** *(built; has a note)* | four names on a signpost | three names and **one time**, and she has never been able to make the fourth one work |
| **the field gate** | where a person leans to watch a road | the only place in the world where all four roads are in reach at once |
| **the hay cart** *(built)* | almost done being loaded, as it has been for as long as anyone remembers | **it will be loaded the day she knows which way it is going.** That day has not come, because of the signpost |

**THE TURN.** Nell is not waiting for a person. She is standing at the
one place in this world where a choice is available, and **she has never
once been down any of the four roads**, because one of the four names
does not make sense and she will not set out on an incomplete list.
She is not a woman waiting at a gate. **She is the only person in this
world who ever had the option and could not take it**, which is the
game's own thesis, stated out loud about somebody who is not the player.

**The visible, permanent change.** You go to the far south-east corner of
the world and read the timetable. Come back to the gate holding the
fourth name and **the cart is loaded, and it is turned north.** It does
not leave — nobody crosses. It is loaded, and it is pointed, and that is
the smallest movement in the game and the largest thing that has ever
happened at that gate.

**Never says:** that 8:15 is a time. That she cannot leave. That she
could have.

*The Common is the first land and it resolves from the furthest corner
of the sheet. That is on purpose.*

## WAITS & WORK

*Waiting as an economy: scheduled, monetised, or denied outright.*

### 10. THE HARROW DOWNS — **JOAN HARROW** *(the counterweight)*

| | |
|---|---|
| **waits for** | the harvest |
| **believes** | that waiting is just work with a date on it |
| **register** | the only one that is not wry. Do not be clever in the Downs |

**The person.** Joan works. She is the only person in this world waiting
for something that actually arrives, and is therefore the only one who
is not really waiting.

**The three places, and what they become.**

| place | what it looks like | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| **the field** | work | work. It does not become anything. That is the point of the Downs |
| **THE MILL** *(built; has a note)* | sails mid-turn, and a miller owed one good gust | a building that is used. The only wait in the world with a date on it and the date comes round |
| **the picnic laid for two** *(the WORLD-SYSTEMS §10 tableau)* | one place setting put away — a small grief, and every player will read it that way | **it is put away every evening and laid out again every morning.** It is not a memorial. It is a place kept |

**THE TURN.** The second setting is not for somebody who is gone. **It
is for whoever turns up.** She has laid it every day for years in a
world where nobody can cross a border, which means she has laid it every
day for years for nobody, and she has never once thought of that as
sad — because it costs one plate and one day, and she has plenty of
both, and the harvest comes either way.

**The visible, permanent change.** There is no knowledge to bring and
nothing to work out. **You sit down.** The second setting stays out
afterwards, permanently — the only tableau in this game that gets easier
to look at.

**Never says:** that you are the first. That anyone else is waiting.
Anything at all, really. The Downs are not a talkative land.

*The story's counterweight and its conscience. Every other wait in this
document is answered by the player carrying something across a border.
This one is answered by the player stopping.*

### 11. GREYLINE CITY — **THE MAN AT THE JUNCTION**

| | |
|---|---|
| **waits for** | nothing. Everybody is going somewhere and nobody arrives |
| **believes** | that waiting is for people who have not made it |
| **register** | clipped, transactional, and it never once slows down |

**The person.** He stands still in the one land where standing still is
shameful. **He has no name and that is the point**, and it stays that
way: nothing in this game will ever name him, and the map will never
mark him.

**The three places, and what they become.**

| place | what it looks like | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| **THE JUNCTION** *(built; has a note)* | four traffic lights, all green | a city that has never once had to stop |
| **the pavement around him** | pavement | **worn.** Everybody walks around him, and the paths they take to do it have been trodden into the stone. He has been there long enough to be geography |
| **the bench, twenty units off** | street furniture | never used. Sitting down in Greyline City is a statement |

**THE TURN.** He is not waiting for somebody to arrive. **He is waiting
to be asked**, and in a land where standing still is shameful, nobody
will ask, because asking means stopping. Everyone in this city has
walked round him for years rather than spend the four seconds it would
cost to find out, and the wear on the pavement is the exact shape of
that decision, repeated about a million times.

**The visible, permanent change.** There is no knowledge to bring here
either. **You stop walking.** Stand still, near him, for long enough
that it is unmistakably a choice — and he goes and sits on the bench,
and stays sitting on it in every later save. **The worn paths stay
worn**, curving round a place where nobody is standing any more.

**Never says:** his name. What he was waiting for. That you did
anything.

### 12. THE CUBICLE MILE — **DENNIS**

| | |
|---|---|
| **waits for** | the 8:15 |
| **believes** | that a timetable is a promise, and a promise is enough |
| **register** | deadpan institutional cheer, and it never breaks. This is the house voice's home |

**The person.** The desk plate says D. HALL. He has the timetable
memorised, has never once suggested the train is not coming, and would
be embarrassed for you if you did.

**The three places, and what they become.**

| place | what it looks like | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| **THE 8:15 STOP** *(built; has a note — and the note is the premise)* | a stop, a timetable, and no track | exactly that, and everybody knows |
| **the timetable** | a schedule of departures | **a survey schedule.** The times are the hours the surveyors were due at each point down the line, and 8:15 is when they were due here, and the last entry on it is this stop because this is where the survey stopped |
| **the car park** *(built — the commuter spur's last point)* | a car park | the end of the line, and nobody who parks in it has ever thought of it that way |

**THE TURN.** Dennis knows what the timetable is. **He has always
known.** He keeps it, and reads it, and has it by heart, because it is
the only document in the world that names all twelve lands in order —
and he has never been to any of them, and **it has never once occurred
to him that they are places.** To Dennis they are stops: a list, in
sequence, that somebody once wrote down as though they belonged
together. Which they do. Which is the only reason anybody still says
them in one breath.

**The visible, permanent change.** You have walked the line — the gate,
the town, the common, main street, the spur, the car park — and you are
the only thing that ever has. Come to the stop holding the route and
**the shelter's light comes on at dusk**, and comes on at every dusk
afterwards, in every save. A stop with a light on is a stop that expects
somebody at an hour when it is dark.

*The mechanism is Session 6's, unchanged: Brim's lamps come on at dusk
on a condition, and this is the same code with a different condition.
The prompt for this session said copy it. It is copied.*

**Never says:** that the times are survey times. That the twelve are
places. That the light means anything. That anything is coming.

## 13. What each wait contributes to the turn

`STORY.md` §5: they were all waiting for **each other**, and **nobody
says it, ever.** Each wait hands the player one piece. Twelve pieces,
no assembly instructions, no acknowledgement anywhere in the game that
they are pieces of anything.

| land | the piece |
|---|---|
| GREYWEATHER | two lands were once served by one trade |
| BRIM | the thing that stopped it was a disagreement about a number |
| MAPLE COURT | one land can be seen from another, if somebody cuts a hedge |
| SPLITROCK | the river that left is running, forty units away, in somebody else's land |
| BLEACH FLATS | the water was always coming from somewhere on this sheet |
| LONGSHORE | there is a thing out there and it has a name |
| PENWOOD | the road everyone uses was one person's private caution |
| WIDE BLUE | a circle can be a line if somebody puts down a second mark |
| THE COMMON | one of the four roads is not a road |
| HARROW DOWNS | a place kept for nobody was always a place kept for anybody |
| GREYLINE CITY | somebody has been waiting to be asked, in public, for years |
