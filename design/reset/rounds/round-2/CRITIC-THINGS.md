Across fifty-four frames INKLANDS has exactly one verb that touches the world: press E and a cream card of prose appears ("the crossroads", "the milestone", "the field gate", "the home field" — twice). Everything else is locomotion or menus. The beetle-cows at frame 4 and 6 stand there and are never touched; the hay sheaves in frame 51 are "silent stops"; the well, cart, and hitching post are scenery; the notebook's JOBS tab (frame 12) is four checkboxes that all begin with READ or TAKE. Compare the goose's to-do list, where every line is a prank you perform on an object that fights back — the gardener snatches his hat, the rake floats, the radio blares — and where the honk button alone does something to every single thing on screen. The only INKLANDS objects that behave like toys are the horse (it follows you unbidden in frames 15–18, leaves hoofprints, and the mount prompt is so fiddly the player burned a dozen commands on it twice) and Nell's cap, which is a state flag, not a plaything. The cart is offered as a pushable at frame 24 — the one goose-like proposition in the strip — and then Nell takes it away. Frames 41–45 are four screenshots of a stick figure pressing into a wall with no honk, no bump, no message. Lovely prose is not a thing to do.
