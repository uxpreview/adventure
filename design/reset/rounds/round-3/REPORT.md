# INKLANDS — cold play report (round 3: the opening on the story of record)

I played from the title screen to gameSec 183 (about three game-minutes), not the full ten. I ran out of my command budget first: the middle of the session was eaten by one job that I kept fumbling (see below). Everything here is what I saw on screen; I never looked at the source.

## 1. First sixty seconds

The title screen was a pale pen-and-ink meadow: a castle at the back, a well, a signpost, a wagon with a stick figure, and the words **INKLANDS — a world in one sheet — twelve lands, one pen**, with a single button, **set out**. I clicked it.

I woke sitting on a bench, with "where you woke — THE COMMON" fading in, a prompt **STAND UP**, three buttons up top (map / notebook / sound: on) and a banner top-left: **NELL — AT THE WASHING LINE**. I pressed E, stood, and got "FOUND: THE BENCH / FOUND: THE NOTE". A little scrap was floating next to the bench; I walked to it and read it: *"back in an hour. — you. pinned to the bench with a tack, in your own handwriting, and it has been rained on more than once. nobody has taken it down."* Somebody off-screen said "Oh. You're back."

I walked over to the woman at the washing line (Nell). Pressing E seemed to do nothing at first — the same speech bubble stayed up — and I wasted a few presses and a click. Then I opened the notebook's HEARD tab and realised each E had in fact advanced her: "You said you'd only be gone an hour." / "It's been three years." / "What do I call you? — You don't know. Course you don't." A name box popped up and my E presses had typed "ee" into it. I backspaced, typed **Wren**, confirmed: "WRITTEN ON THE COVER: WREN", then "Wren, then. Right." and finally the control hint: *wasd to walk · drag to look · E to act*. Two more presses: "hold shift to run", "Mind the bull. It knows you." — and a bull walked straight into me ("Oof. It knows me."). **NEW JOB: GET IT HOME. E — GET ON THE HORSE WHEN IT COMES.** A horse trotted in from the left.

By gameSec 60 I was still trying to mount the horse. **Can I say what the game wants me to do? Yes: get on the horse, lead the bull through the field gate so Nell can shut it, and more broadly go round these lands sorting out things I left three years ago. Confidence: 8/10** for the immediate job, maybe 6/10 for the bigger shape (I only learned the "twelve things" list at gameSec 183).

## 2. What I understood

- **World.** A hand-drawn place on one sheet of paper: "twelve lands" (the map is a 3×4 grid with rivers and dashed roads; footer said "1 of 12 lands walked"). I'm in THE COMMON, next to THE CROSSROADS with a signpost pointing to BRIM, THE SEA, DOWNS and "8.15". Told by the tagline, the map, and the signpost.
- **Who I am.** Someone who left a note saying "back in an hour" and came back three years later with no memory of their own name. Told by the note and by Nell. The name I typed went on the notebook cover.
- **The goal.** Told outright at the signpost: **"TWELVE THINGS. THEN I CAN GO HOME."** — a twelve-item list, one person per land (Wick, Nell, Marget, Val, Brack, Holt, Amos, Pye, Wren, Joan, the man at the crossing, Dennis and the 8:15), "PINNED: TWELVE PLACES. ANY ORDER." Before that, the notebook's first page was "stuck to the cover. it can wait." Item 2 (Nell — get the bull home) was the one I'd just done.
- **Controls.** WASD walk (told, but only after the naming scene), shift run (told), E acts on whatever the on-screen prompt says (told), drag to look and R to recentre (told once I was on the horse), N opens the notebook (told at the signpost), H whistles the horse (told after the job). Backspace is needed to clear the name box — guessed.
- **The interact rule.** E goes to whichever prompt is showing; NPCs standing near an object steal it. Guessed, painfully.
- **The bull follows the horse; Nell shuts the gate; then get out of the way.** Told by Nell, in three separate bubbles, only after I'd already been butted twice.
- **The horse stays where you leave it; H calls it.** Told.
- **The map** is a whole-world sheet with a "you" marker and place names, but at the scale I saw it the labels piled on top of each other and it didn't help me find a gate.

## 3. What I did (gameSec)

- 0: clicked set out. 1: stood up (E). 3: read the note, put it back.
- 6–11: talked to Nell (E ×3, plus a click that did nothing). 11–13: opened notebook (LIST stuck; JOBS "nothing yet. talk to somebody."; HEARD showed four Nell lines), opened map.
- 14–21: name box appeared; cleared the "ee", typed Wren, confirmed.
- 22–27: more Nell (controls hint, "hold shift to run", "Mind the bull"), bull hit me, NEW JOB.
- 29–35: waited for the horse, pressed E at it — got Nell instead. Stepped toward it, E — Nell again.
- 37–42: ran away (shift+S) as told; bull followed and hit me again ("It knows me. That's worse."); Nell: "It knows you. Hang on —".
- 44: ran right; Nell finally explained the plan: "Get on the horse. It follows the horse. Lead it through the gate and I'll shut it."
- 46–65: seven more attempts to mount. The prompt only flipped to GET ON THE HORSE for an instant before Nell walked up and it went back to TALK TO NELL. My own character said "Closer. Nell is a step off."
- 68: mounted (walk 1.5 s, press E instantly). DONE: GET ON THE HORSE. NEXT: LEAD THE BULL THROUGH THE GATE.
- 70–77: rode up-right, hit a fence ("Solid."), Nell: "Now bring it here. Through the gate." Opened the map; couldn't read it.
- 80–83: felt along the fence pushing right; slipped through a gap around z 80–84.
- 84–93: rode around, through the gap again, out into the meadow; bull followed everywhere; nothing completed.
- 99: tried the other direction — "Not through there."
- 101–114: three more passes up and down beside the fence; kept getting pinned at x = −10.5.
- 117–121: rode far east; tried to get off (E didn't register while moving).
- 124–126: got off; found a cart, pushed it: "THE CART ROLLS" (it rolled away). Bull immediately on top of me.
- 130–138: rode back, looked around with a drag, pushed the invisible edge: **"That's a wall. Round it, then."**
- 145–150: back through the gap to the west with the bull behind me — banner cleared, **"There. That's more like you."** Notebook: *"It went in. It always did, for you."* — reward: THE HORSE.
- 153–160: Nell: "I was beginning to think you weren't coming back." "H WHISTLES THE HORSE" "Keep the horse. You always did."
- 163–171: ran west; a dog-walker asked "Did you fix the bridge?"; Nell called after me "Morrow's got a copy of your notebook. He got most of it wrong."
- 174: found THE CROSSROADS. 178: "Closer, and it says READ THE SIGNPOST." 182: read the signpost — FOUND: THE CROSSROADS, PINNED: TWELVE PLACES. ANY ORDER, banner THE LIST — VAL, THE THREE CHAIRS.
- 183: pressed N, read the twelve-item list. Stopped (out of commands).

## 4. What reacted

Things that answered me:
- Standing up, reading the note, every found place and object got a "FOUND:" / "READ:" toast at my feet.
- Nell had a new line for nearly every E press (a lot of them, and good ones), and she physically followed me around the common.
- The name I typed showed up as "Wren's notebook" and Nell used it.
- The bull walked into me on cue and kept doing it whenever I was off the horse. It really did follow the horse.
- The horse came when the job started and again when I pressed H.
- Bumping things gives a wry line from my own character: "Solid." / "Not through there." / "That's a wall. Round it, then." / "Closer, and it says GET ON THE HORSE." / "Closer, and it says READ THE SIGNPOST." I liked these a lot — the game notices what I'm trying.
- The cart rolled when pushed.
- A stranger with a dog asked a question as I ran past; Nell shouted a parting line.
- Entering the crossroads put up a place title; reading the signpost unstuck the notebook's first page and changed the top-left banner.

Things that did nothing:
- Clicking on Nell (mouse click on an NPC does nothing; only E).
- Pressing E next to the horse while Nell stood beside it — about ten times. The prompt said TALK TO NELL and that's what it did.
- Pressing E while the horse was still moving (it seems to register as "WHOA" first).
- Leading the bull east through the gap and riding well away: no completion, no comment, no gate shutting. The job only completed on a later pass in the opposite direction, and I still don't know what was different.
- The map, twice: too zoomed out to show the gate I was standing next to.

## 5. What I wanted to do and couldn't

- **Find the gate.** I could see hurdles and fence panels drawn in several places and the word THE FIELD GATE hovering over the tree, but I never worked out which gap was the gate, which side was "home", or where Nell wanted the bull. I solved it by accident after twelve minutes of riding into invisible edges.
- **Mount the horse without Nell stealing the prompt.** It needed a frame-perfect press between the horse arriving and Nell arriving.
- **Talk to the dog-walker** ("Did you fix the bridge?") — they moved off before I got there and I didn't want to spend the commands chasing.
- **Look down the well, read the gravestone, take the road to THE SEA or BRIM or the 8:15.** All right there, all unexplored.
- **Understand the camera.** After a drag the keys felt camera-relative, then after R they felt world-relative; I was never sure which.

## 6. Best and worst moments

**Best:** reading the signpost and having the notebook's stuck page come loose into "TWELVE THINGS. THEN I CAN GO HOME." — with my own chosen name sitting in slot 9 ("Wren. Give the race a finish line."). That, and the three-line arc of "Oof. It knows me." → "It knows me. That's worse." → "There. That's more like you." The writing is dry and kind.

**Worst:** the stretch from gameSec 70 to 145, riding the same fifteen metres of fence, x pinned at −10.5, the bull cheerfully following me nowhere, the map useless, and Nell repeating "Through the gate. Then get out of the way." without telling me which gate or which side. It took over half my session.

## 7. To a friend

It's a quiet hand-drawn walking game where you come back to a village three years late with no memory, a notebook and a list of twelve small favours you owe people, one per land, and you go round doing them in any order — on foot or on a horse that a woman at a washing line says was always yours. It looks like a pencil sketch that someone keeps drawing while you walk through it, and everyone talks like they know you better than you do.

Next time I open it I want to: (1) take the signpost's road to THE SEA (or find the bridge the dog-walker mentioned); (2) find Morrow and see what he got wrong in his copy of my notebook; (3) do Val's "cut the gap in the hedge", since the banner is already pointing me at Val and "the three chairs".

## 8. Scores (0–10)

- Understandable: **5** — the story and the list are crystal clear; the physical task of "which gap is the gate" was not, and the prompt-stealing made a two-second action take a minute of game time.
- Alive: **8** — Nell follows you, animals follow the horse, strangers talk as you pass, and your own character comments on walls.
- Fun: **6** — great when it's talking, exhausting when it's fencing.
- Beautiful: **9** — the ink-on-paper look, the fading place titles and the drawn footprints are lovely.
