# INKLANDS — cold player report (round 7)

*(The sub-agent was refused the Write tool; this is its final message,
saved by the session that ran the round. 2026-09-19, desktop rig, from
the title, no parameters. Played to gameSec 308.)*

## 1. First sixty seconds

The title said **INKLANDS — "a world in one sheet — twelve lands, one pen"**, over a pencil-sketch drawing of a castle wall, a well, a signpost and a stick figure standing next to a bicycle. One button: **set out**. I clicked it.

I landed on a bench with the words *"where you woke"* and **THE COMMON** fading in over the field, and a prompt in the corner: **STAND UP**. I pressed E. I stood, and a quest strip appeared top-left — **NELL — GO AND TALK TO HER** — and a speech bubble from a figure by a washing line: *"Oh. You're back."*

I walked toward her (w/d) and the game quietly taught me the rest: *"wasd to walk · drag to look · E or a click to act."* Talking to Nell gave me: *"You said you'd only be gone an hour." / "It's been three years." / "What do I call you? — You don't know. Course you don't."* — then a handwritten card: *you can't remember your name. what will you go by, for now?*

**At gameSec 60, could I say what the game wants me to do?** Yes, roughly. I'd have said: *I'm someone who walked out of this place three years ago and came back with no memory; I have a notebook, the map is blank, and I'm meant to walk twelve lands and put right the things I left undone.* By second 60 the world had already given me "twelve lands, one pen" (title), a personal notebook with my name written on the cover, a **NEW JOB: GET IT HOME**, and a counter that said **1 OF 12 KEPT**.

**Confidence: 7/10.** I knew the shape of it. What I did *not* know at 60s was whether "twelve" meant twelve places, twelve chores or twelve people — that only resolved at ~92s when the list unstuck and said *TWELVE THINGS. THEN I CAN GO HOME.*

## 4. What reacted

**Things that answered me:**

- **Standing up** put Nell's line in the air and a quest in the corner in the same beat.
- **My typed name** went on the notebook cover, and every NPC used it. Nell: *"eeeWren, then. Right."* The book is titled *eeeWren's notebook*. I gave it a stupid name and the game wore it without flinching, which was funnier than a good name would have been.
- **Running from the bull** produced *NEW JOB: GET IT HOME*, and the explanation came from a person, not a tutorial box: *"Get on the horse. The bull follows the horse."*
- **Leading the bull in** → *DONE: GET IT HOME. YOURS: THE HORSE. 1 OF 12 KEPT.* Three separate pieces of feedback for one act.
- **Nell shut the gate with me inside it** and said so: *"You're in with it. Stile's at the top of the field, north."* A joke at my expense that also taught me a mechanic.
- **H whistled the horse** from behind a closed gate to my side. Worked first try.
- **Walking anywhere new** wrote a name on the blank map — THE FAIR GROUND, THE CROSSROADS, THE MILESTONE, THE FADED FOOTPRINTS, THE BOARDWALK, THE SANDBAR — and pushed the ink counter up.
- **Cutting the hedge** left a **visible gap** in the geometry. Two hedge blocks, cut ends, daylight between them. I could see the thing I'd done from twenty paces away. That is the single best piece of feedback in the game.
- **Choosing THE GREEN** immediately changed the prose of a place I could go and read: *"the porch light at the head of it is off. the others are going off too, one a day, and nobody on this road would be so rude as to say so. the bins still go out on the right day."*
- **Val's reply** to my choice was not a thank-you: *"Light's off. It's one less thing. The street'll go dark a house at a time, you watch."*
- **Crossing Marget out** produced *CROSSED OUT: MARGET. THE NOTEBOOK DOES NOT OBJECT.* — no confirm dialog, no undo offered. Then, six game-minutes later and a mile away on a beach, unprompted: **"SOMEWHERE, MORROW WRITES MARGET INTO HIS OWN LIST."** I abandoned a debt and the world quietly handed it to someone else.
- The notebook's **HEARD** tab had logged every single line anyone had said to me, in reverse order. **CHOICES** had logged a consequence nobody had told me: *"VAL DOES NOT ASK JUNE. JUNE STAYS AT HER OWN GATE."*
- A bell rang at 10:00 and Nell said *"That's the bell. Morrow rings it. He's had the hour wrong for three years."*

**Things I did where nothing happened:**

- **Backspace in the name field.** I pressed it ten times, clicked into the field and pressed it seven more. The text never changed. That's how I ended up as "eeeWren" — my three E-presses to advance dialogue had already been typed into a field that had silently opened, and I couldn't take them back.
- **LEAN ON THE GATE WITH HER.** The prompt was on screen; E gave me the gate's description card instead, three times. Clicking the prompt text did the same. I never got to lean.
- **Clicking the "!" edge marker.** Nothing. It's decoration, not a button.
- **Pressing E near Val while standing on the "look up the street" spot** re-read the street description four times in a row instead of talking to her. Same failure mode as the gate.
- **space, r** on the sandbar, and walking around looking for a stone: no skim. The HUD told me *BEST SKIM: NO BEST YET — throw it at a run, over water*, which is a promise of a toy, and I could not find the door into it.
- **Walking out onto the sea.** I rode a horse off a sandbar into open blue water and nothing objected — no splash, no slowdown, no boundary. The region name changed to "ocean" and the horse kept walking on the surface.

## 5. What I wanted to do and couldn't

1. **Lean on the gate with Nell.** This is the thing I wanted most. It's the only prompt in the game so far that isn't a task — it's just standing next to someone. Two interactables share that spot and the gate's read always wins.
2. **Skim a stone.** The game told me there's a record to beat and never told me the verb.
3. **Fix my name.** A two-second problem that will follow me through the whole game.
4. **Find my way back to a named place on purpose.** The map shows twelve destinations and my own position, but nothing in the world points at them — no compass rose, no "THE PUNT is that way". I navigated by reading x/z numbers off the status line, which a real player can't do. I spent about 40 game-seconds pressed against one invisible fence with no visual hint I was inside an enclosure.
5. **Read THE NOTE.** It was labelled on the common in my first minute, it's on my map, and I never got back to it because I got swept along by the quest arrow.

## 6. Best and worst

**Best moment.** Riding onto the boardwalk at noon, having crossed a promise out of my own book ten seconds earlier out of idle curiosity, and getting: *SOMEWHERE, MORROW WRITES MARGET INTO HIS OWN LIST.* No fanfare, no penalty screen. Just — someone else picked it up. I actually sat back. Runner-up: *"the bins still go out on the right day."*

**Worst moment.** The name field. Three keystrokes meant for a dialogue box landed in a text input that had appeared without warning, backspace did nothing, and I permanently named myself "eeeWren" before the game was twenty seconds old. Every beautiful, restrained line of prose for the next five minutes had "eeeWren" in it. It's a small bug that undermines the most personal thing the game asks of you.

## 7. To a friend

> You wake up on a bench in a world drawn entirely in biro, with no memory and a notebook containing twelve promises you made to twelve people before you disappeared three years ago. You walk, ride a horse you're told you always had, and put things right — or don't, because the book is yours and it lets you scratch a promise out without asking twice.

**Three things I'd do next time:**

1. **Go and read THE NOTE on the bench** — the one pinned where I woke up, that I walked straight past.
2. **Find Wick and the old road** (#1), because *"Don't let him tell you it's nothing"* is the best sentence on the list and I want to know who wrote it about him.
3. **Work out how to skim a stone off that sandbar** and put a number in that empty BEST SKIM slot.

(And a fourth I couldn't shake: go back to Nell and lean on the gate.)
