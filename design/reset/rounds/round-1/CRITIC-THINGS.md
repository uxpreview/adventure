Across sixty-six frames I counted exactly one thing the player did to the world: PUSH THE CART, frame 9, "THE CART ROLLS," and the cart simply leaves the scene. Every other verb the game offers (READ, LEAN, LISTEN, WAIT, TALK) opens the same cream paper card and asks you to "put it back"; WAIT FOR THE BELL at the belfry re-opens the note it just showed. The bull that walks up to you in frame 8 is the one Goose-shaped moment in the strip, and there is nothing to do with it; no prompt, no reaction, the fence's joke about it arrives only on a card. The square in frames 34–35 is dense with stalls, barrels, crates, pigeons, a bench, a red banner, a dozen figures, and the player's report confirms that E and clicks on the banner, the gateway carter, and the townsperson produced nothing, and half the crowd is scenery indistinguishable from Marget. Untitled Goose Game's whole first minute is a garden where every rake, radio, and gardener answers the beak, the to-do list is a list of toys, and honk works everywhere; here the to-do list is "read the market cross," which ten minutes did not locate. The writing is lovely and none of it is a toy.
