The things in INKLANDS talk beautifully and the people barely talk at all. Across sixty-six frames the world's voice lives almost entirely in four paper notes: the signpost ("one that says 8:15, which is not a place"), the long fence ("the stile is for people who have met it"), the square ("market day here has been next week for a long time"), the belfry ("the bell splits the difference"). That is a good narrator, but it is signage. The HEARD tab at frame 66 totals four lines from two speakers, one of them (Nell) a disembodied "RUN. THE GATE. NOW." the player never located. Marget is the only person who answers a keypress; her third line is a real, specific errand, and her bubble following the player across the square is the one moment a human voice pursued them. Meanwhile the figure in the gateway, the townsperson in the back street, the crowd round the fountain, the person on the wall: pressed, clicked, silent, unlabeled. Nothing read anything back. The cart was pushed, the bull met, "BRIM'S COLOUR IS RED" learned, and no one mentioned any of it; the CHOICES tab stayed empty because no choice was ever offered. Stardew hands you thirty villagers with a line each on day one; Goodsprings has Trudy asking what you did with Joe Cobb by the second conversation. Here it is one shopkeeper and a very good brochure.
