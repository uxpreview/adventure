Inklands does have a pulse, and the strip proves it: the bull that ambles over the moment you push the cart (frames 8–9), a speck of a person standing on top of the town wall (22), a horse and handcart parked in the gateway (27–29), and then the square (34–41) — bunting, pigeons, a dog by the stalls, a crowd of a dozen ink figures, the fountain sketching its own spray in blue as you approach. Footprints trail behind you, region names fade in, Marget's bubble chases you across the square. That is more incidental life than most prototypes manage. But A Short Hike's inhabitants all answer: every bird on Hawk Peak has a line, a name, a small want, and the island's scale is felt by climbing above it and gliding back down. Here the player stood on the gateway figure and the back-street townsperson, pressed E, clicked, and got nothing (28–29, 32–33); only Marget talks. The common between the fence and the wall is three frames of bare wash with nobody in it (19–21). The camera never turns, so the "found" bench was invisible, and the crowd, however drawn, holds still while the clock the whole quest hangs on refuses to be waited through. A world is inhabited when its people are reachable and its size is walkable; this one is decorated.
