The one thing INKLANDS gets right for this pillar is the first ten frames: from the common you can see the wall of Brim, its towers and a ghostly skyline behind them, across the whole top of the screen, and frames 10 through 23 show it swelling as you run at it until it fills the frame. That is the Breath of the Wild arc, and it lands once. Then it stops. The camera never turns, tilts, or pulls back; it is bolted facing north for all 66 frames. So the player found the gate by opening the map (frames 24–25) even though its towers sat on the right edge of frame 23, walked past the clock arch at frame 27 without knowing it was the gate, and in frame 47 got a tower's shadow on the ground and a red post as the only clue where the belfry was. Inside Brim the foreground houses are drawn on top of the player (frames 31–33, 59–63) and go translucent instead of the camera stepping aside, and the report confirms the belfry bench was "found" but never seen because south does not exist on screen. BotW lets you stop, swing the stick, scope a shrine, drop a pin, and then walk to it; here a landmark is only a landmark if it happens to be north of you, and the odd modern skyline on the horizon, the one thing I most wanted to walk toward, is never named or acknowledged. Beautiful sheet, no eyes.
