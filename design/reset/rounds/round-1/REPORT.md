# INKLANDS — cold play report (round 1)

Played from the title screen with no prior knowledge. I stopped at **gameSec 216** (game clock 11:16), not 600: I ran out of my ~90-command budget first, most of it spent fighting collision in the back streets of Brim. Everything below is what I actually saw.

## 1. First sixty seconds

The title screen said **INKLANDS — "a world in one sheet — twelve lands, one pen"** with a single button, **set out**, over a pencil-and-wash meadow with a well, a cart, a signpost, and a walled town on the horizon. I clicked *set out*. I became a little stick figure in a field of dark grass; a prompt said **READ THE SIGNPOST**. I pressed E (nobody told me to; it's what I'd try) and the signpost's text opened on a paper note: Brim north, the sea west, the downs east, Maple Court south, "and one that says 8:15, which is not a place." Three toasts popped: READ, YOU HEARD OF (four place names), FOUND: THE CROSSROADS. I clicked *put it back*, opened the **map** (a twelve-square hand-drawn sheet: "1 of 12 lands walked — 0 strokes of ink"), and while it was open a speech bubble appeared on the left edge: **"RUN. THE GATE. NOW."** and a hint said *hold shift to run*. The map wouldn't close with M, Esc, or its own button — it only went away when I walked. A prompt said **PUSH THE CART**; I pressed E and the cart rolled off ("THE CART ROLLS"). A big bull wandered right up to me. I ran north and hit a fence I couldn't cross, found the gate section, and pressed E on it — nothing. Sidestepped, jumped (space) — nothing. Opened the **notebook**: "THE 8:15 WILL STOP FOR 0 OF 12 — nothing yet. talk to somebody." Its HEARD tab told me the shouter was **NELL**. Ran west, found **THE LONG FENCE** and a stile ("LEAN ON THE STILE" — its note: "the bull is on the field side. the stile is for people who have met it."), walked through it, and ran north across open ground to the town wall. At gameSec 60 I was walking along the wall looking for the gate.

**At gameSec 60, can I say what the game wants me to do?** I think it wants me to walk to all twelve lands, talk to people and do small jobs for them, so that the 8:15 (a train, presumably) will stop for each of them. **Confidence: 6/10.** The "0 of 12" counter, the "talk to somebody", and the twelve-square map all point the same way, but nothing has said it outright, and the only direct instruction (Nell's "RUN. THE GATE. NOW.") sent me at a fence I couldn't open.

## 2. What I understood

- **World:** A hand-drawn, ink-and-wash England-ish place split into twelve lands on one sheet of paper. The common with a crossroads in the middle; the Kingdom of Brim (a walled town with a market square, back streets, an orchard close, a belfry) to the north; Longshore, the Harrow Downs, Maple Court elsewhere. *Learned by reading:* the signpost, the map, and the region names that fade in at the top of the screen when you cross into a new area.
- **Goal:** The notebook literally says **"THE 8:15 WILL STOP FOR 0 OF 12"** and "talk to somebody". So: something called the 8:15 will stop for a land once you've done its job. *Told*, via the notebook. Marget gave me the first job: **THE HOUR OF BRIM** — read the market cross, then sit under the belfry until the lamps come on and tell her which clock hand agrees with them. *Told*, in dialogue. The belfry's note explained why: the two hands disagree, and the bell "splits the difference."
- **Controls:** WASD walks, **shift runs** (told by a hint), **E** interacts (guessed; the prompts are verbs like READ / PUSH / LEAN / LISTEN / TALK / WAIT with no key shown). **Map** and **notebook** are clickable buttons top-right (shown). Notes close with *put it back* (shown). Space does nothing I could find. The map closes when you walk, but it also sometimes blocks walking until you click its button again — I never worked out which it would be.
- **Things I only guessed:** that a stile is the way across a fence (real-world knowledge, and it worked); that the red circle on the archway was a clock; that the red banner in the square might be the market cross (it wasn't); that pressing E under the belfry would pass time (it didn't — it just re-reads the note).

## 4. What reacted

**Things that answered me:**
- The signpost, the fence, the square, and the belfry each opened a paper note with a distinct, funny paragraph when I pressed E, and each fired READ / FOUND toasts.
- The cart rolled away when I pushed it, with the words THE CART ROLLS.
- The bull walked over to me the moment I moved (and the fence note later told me it was "on the field side" with me — a good joke landed after the fact).
- A tiny person stood on the wall; pigeons pecked round the square; a crowd milled; a dog-sized animal trotted about near the stalls; someone had a bird on their hat.
- Marget spoke three different lines on three presses, then called after me across the square with the real instructions. Her bubble followed me.
- Region names faded in as I crossed borders (the common → the walled town → the back streets → the square → the orchard close).
- The map drew my footprints and counted "strokes of ink" (0 → 142 → 278 → 338) and "2 of 12 lands walked" after I entered Brim.
- The hint "hold shift to run" appeared exactly when I first needed it.
- The fountain drew blue ink spray lines when I got close.

**Things where nothing happened:**
- M, Esc, and the *map* button all failed to close the map the first time; only walking did.
- E and clicking on the person in the gateway, the townsperson in the back street, and the red banner in the square: nothing, no prompt.
- E on the X-braced gate in the long fence: nothing (the stile was the way).
- Space: no jump, nothing at all.
- Pressing E under the belfry to "WAIT FOR THE BELL": it re-opens the same note rather than waiting.
- Walking into several visible gaps between buildings: solid.
- The "look" label near the banner at the south gate: I never found what it was attached to.

## 5. What I wanted to do next and couldn't

- **Find the market cross.** The job tracker has said READ THE MARKET CROSS for two hundred game-seconds. It isn't on the map, it isn't the fountain, it isn't the red banner, and nothing in the square prompted me for it. I'd have kept looking if I'd had the moves.
- **Sit on the belfry bench.** The game told me I'd found it, but the camera never turns round to show the south side of the street, so I couldn't see it or get its prompt.
- **Actually wait for dusk.** Marget wants me under the belfry when the lamps come on. Time runs at roughly one game hour per hundred seconds, so that's the better part of a real hour away; I hoped WAIT FOR THE BELL or sitting would skip ahead, and it didn't.
- **Talk to the people who don't talk.** Half the figures in Brim are scenery, and there's no visual difference between them and Marget until you're on top of her and the prompt appears.
- **Close the map reliably.** Sometimes M works, sometimes only the button, sometimes only walking.

## 6. Best and worst moments

**Best:** Coming through the south gate into the square. Bunting strung between the houses, the fountain drawn in blue, stalls with red and blue awnings, a crowd of ink people and pigeons, and then the fountain's note: "market day here has been next week for a long time." That plus the fence's "the stile is for people who have met it" convinced me somebody cared about every sentence.

**Worst:** Sixty game-seconds (and a dozen precious commands) stuck against a house block in the back streets, with a visible gap between two buildings that I could not walk through, trying five different routes to reach a red post I could see through the wall — which turned out not to be interactive anyway. The invisible walls don't match the drawing, and the camera wouldn't turn to help me.

## 7. To a friend

It's a walking-and-talking game drawn entirely in pencil and watercolour, where you're a stick figure crossing a sheet of paper split into twelve little lands, doing one small favour for each so that a train called the 8:15 will stop there. Every sign and fence and fountain has a paragraph of dry, lovely writing, and the world is busy with bulls and pigeons and people, but at the moment it's easy to get physically stuck and hard to tell who you can talk to.

Three things I'd do next time:
1. Find the blasted market cross, then sit on the belfry bench through to dusk and see which hand the lamps agree with.
2. Go and find Nell — she's the one who shouted at me and I never met her.
3. Walk west to Longshore (the sea) and see what the "same red as somewhere else" is about.

## 8. Scores (0–10)

- **Understandable: 5.** The goal is stated (notebook), the prompts are clear verbs, and the writing tells you where to go. But E is never named, the map's close behaviour is inconsistent, the first "RUN. THE GATE. NOW." points at a gate you can't use, and the market cross is un-findable in the time I had.
- **Alive: 7.** The bull coming over, the figure on the wall, pigeons, the crowd, region names, footprints in ink, Marget calling after you, the fountain's spray. Undercut by the many silent figures.
- **Fun: 5.** The reading is the fun, and it's real fun. The moving-around is not: invisible walls, a camera that only faces north, and no way to pass time.
- **Beautiful: 9.** Genuinely gorgeous. The wash sky, the cross-hatched grass, the bunting, the map as a real sheet of paper with your walk inked onto it.
