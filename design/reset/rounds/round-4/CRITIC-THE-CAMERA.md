The camera keeps its promises and no more. Across 77 frames I could find no turn the player did not ask for: the yaw held steady from frame 41 to 58 while Wren ran west and back east along the town wall, and the two drags (frames 23 and 26) swung the view a clean 180° and back, with the standees (Nell, the bull, Wren) re-facing the lens each time. Movement went camera-relative, which the player found out by walking the wrong way and was never told. That is the whole of the pillar's positive evidence, and the player used the orbit twice in ninety commands. What Breath of the Wild does that this strip never does is make the horizon worth turning toward: from frame 41 on, the wall is a flat grey slab filling the top half of the frame, the gate towers are translucent panels (frame 50, "Not through there."), and the player walked under the arch twice without seeing it because the camera never lowered, never leaned toward the direction of travel, and never put the way ahead on screen; running west (frames 46–48, 53–55) leaves the destination off the left edge with Wren pinned right of centre, facing me. The one horizon that ever beckoned was the houses that appeared after the drag in frame 23, and the player dragged straight back. Nobody in this session saw a thing far off and went there; they read a map they could not read.
