The first thirty frames are a real opening and I will not pretend otherwise: the note in your own hand, Nell speaking before you reach her, the bull flattening you twice ("Oof. It knows me."), then "It went in. It always did, for you." at 1 OF 12 KEPT. And the belfry is the best thing in the strip, a choice card whose three buttons state their consequences and a scarf on the figure the instant you pick. But the pillar is the hour that teaches by playing, and A Short Hike's whole trick is that the peak is always on screen: you never spend a minute unsure which way is forward. Here the strip shows E doing nothing on TALK TO NELL for three frames, the controls line arriving only after the name box, Nell's "Get on the horse. It follows the horse" spoken after the player has already done it, and then twenty frames (39 to 58) of an identical grey wall: the whistled horse parked on the wrong side of the fence, "BRIM'S GATE IS WEST ALONG THE WALL" fired while standing at the gate, "Not through there," "Nothing to hand," and a map whose labels pile into one smudge every time it opens. The world does not open in this strip; it closes a fence and hides an arch. A Short Hike would have had Claire at the first campsite by then.
