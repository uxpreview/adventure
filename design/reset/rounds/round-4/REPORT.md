# INKLANDS — cold play, round 4

Played from the title screen to gameSec 368 (about 90 commands; stopped at the command budget, not the ten-minute mark). In-game the clock went from 9:00 to 20:23.

## 1. First sixty seconds

The title said **INKLANDS — a world in one sheet — twelve lands, one pen**, over a pencil-drawn meadow with a well, a signpost, a castle behind a wall, and a cart. One button: **set out**. I clicked it.

I was sitting on a bench in a place called THE COMMON ("where you woke"). The prompt said STAND UP but not which key. I tried E because that's what I'd try, and it worked — two "FOUND" lines popped. A note was pinned beside the bench: *"back in an hour. — you."* in my own handwriting, rained on more than once. A woman at a washing line said "Oh. You're back." then "You said you'd only be gone an hour." then "It's been three years." Pressing E on TALK TO NELL did nothing visible twice, then Enter (or time, I couldn't tell) advanced her, then a name box opened. I typed Wren. Only *after* naming myself did the game finally show me the controls: "wasd to walk · drag to look · E to act", then "hold shift to run" a few seconds later.

Then a bull ran me over (twice) while Nell said RUN from behind the gate. She told me to get on the horse; the bull follows the horse. I got on, rode it into the field, and at gameSec 53 the game said **"It went in. It always did, for you." — 1 OF 12 KEPT**, and the horse became mine.

**At gameSec 60, can I say what this game wants me to do?** Yes, mostly. I think I'm someone who left this place for "an hour" three years ago, and the game wants me to walk the twelve lands on the map and put right the small things I left undone — twelve of them, one per land, each kept in a notebook. I also think it wants me to *lean, sit, wait, and read* rather than fight. Confidence: **7/10**. What I'm unsure of is whether the twelve "kept" things are the whole point or just a counter, and who the far-off voice ("I was beginning to think you weren't coming back") belongs to.

## 2. What I understood

**World.** A single hand-drawn sheet, twelve lands. I walked two: the meadow (THE COMMON, THE FIELD GATE, THE LONG FENCE) and THE KINGDOM OF BRIM (THE SOUTH GATE, THE BACK STREETS, THE SQUARE, THE BELFRY). Learned from the title line (told), the land-name cards that fade in when you cross a border (shown), and the map's "N of 12 lands walked" footer (shown).

**Who I am.** Someone who lived here, was trusted (the bull "knows me", the horse "always did, for you"), promised to be back in an hour, and has been gone three years. Learned from the note (read), Nell (told), and the field-gate card (read): the gate's been open three years because "the one who was going to fix the catch said he would be an hour." That card is the moment the whole premise clicked.

**Goal.** Put things right, one per land; each done thing is "kept" (1 of 12, 2 of 12). Told by the counters. Jobs are given by people and tracked in the notebook JOBS tab with a "for: THE HORSE" / "for: THE RED SCARF" reward line — shown, once I opened the notebook.

**Controls.** E to act (guessed, then told at ~gameSec 18). WASD to walk (guessed, then told). Shift to run (told, as a hint after Nell's RUN line). Drag to look (told). H whistles the horse (told, on a card at gameSec 60). Movement is camera-relative, which I discovered by dragging the camera 180° and finding W now walked me the other way — never told. Map and notebook are on-screen buttons (shown). Cards close with E or "put it back" (guessed).

**How the bull job works.** Nell says "It follows the horse. Bring it in through this gate, and I'll shut it behind it." That line arrived *after* I'd already got on the horse and ridden through — the first hint I got was just "E — GET ON THE HORSE WHEN IT COMES", and then "Closer, and it says LEAN ON THE GATE WITH HER" when I pressed E at the wrong moment. The game hints in the right direction but a beat late.

**Sitting speeds time.** Learned by accident: sitting on the belfry bench pushed the clock from 11:00 to 20:15 in about 150 game-seconds, and it rained in between. Never told, but it fits the "wait at the belfry till the lamps come on" job perfectly.

## 4. What reacted

**The world answered:**
- Standing up put two FOUND lines on screen at once.
- Walking one step toward Nell made her speak unprompted ("Oh. You're back.").
- The note was in my handwriting and the game knew it.
- Naming myself wrote it on the notebook cover ("WRITTEN ON THE COVER: WREN") and the notebook title became "Wren's notebook".
- The bull physically ran me down and the game said "Oof. It knows me." and later "It knows me. That's worse." — my own character has a voice.
- Riding into the fence: "Solid." Pressing E with nothing near: "Nothing to hand. Somewhere else, then." Pushing at the wall: "Not through there." Every dead end has a line.
- Pressing E near a job at the wrong moment gave a directed hint ("Closer, and it says…") instead of nothing. Twice.
- The bull quietened and stood still the instant the job completed; Nell said "There. That's more like you."
- Whistling with H made the horse come at a trot from wherever it was.
- Footprints appear behind me and persist; I could see my own doubled tracks along the wall when I came back.
- Crossing a region border fades in a name card (THE KINGDOM OF BRIM, THE BACK STREETS, THE SQUARE).
- Sitting made time run, rain fell and stopped, the light went amber, lamps lit in windows, and the objective ticked over on its own.
- The belfry choice wrote the consequences of each option on the button, and the scarf appeared on my figure the moment I chose.
- The map's footer counts lands walked and "strides of ink"; the HEARD tab logs every line.

**I did something and nothing happened:**
- E on TALK TO NELL, twice, at gameSec 6–9. (Then it worked. I never learned why.)
- Clicking on Nell.
- Pressing E at the bull, hoping it was the horse.
- Leaning on the stile read a card but did not climb it; I just walked through the fence.
- Whistling the horse to the far side of the fence: it came and stood there. No prompt, no line about it.
- Running along the town wall for ~80 game-seconds in each direction looking for a way in, when the way in was where I'd started.
- The "WAIT FOR THE BELL" prompt at the end: I ran out of moves before I heard it.

## 5. What I wanted to do next and could not, or did not know how

- **Get the horse out of the field.** I'd just been given a horse and a whistle, and the very next thing the game did was put a fence between us with no way over I could find. I wanted to ride to Brim.
- **Find the South Gate.** The archway is drawn the same faint grey as the wall and I walked under it twice without a prompt. The map put the gate right next to my dot at a scale where "right next to" was forty strides away. The "west along the wall, on the road" hint fired when I was *already* at the road and sent me the wrong way.
- **Read the map.** Every place label in a cluster is drawn over every other one: THE COMMON / THE FIELD GATE / THE LONG FENCE / THE BENCH are one black smudge; so are BRIM SQUARE / THE MARKET CROSS / THE BELFRY / THE SOUTH GATE.
- **Know who said "I was beginning to think you weren't coming back."** It came from off-screen with no name and never came back.
- **Read the first page of the notebook.** "Stuck to the cover. It can wait." Fine — but I want it.
- **Hear the bell**, and go back to Marget to tell her which hand.
- **Talk to the other people in the square** — there were six or seven and only Marget had a name.

## 6. Best moment and worst moment

**Best:** standing up from the belfry bench at 20:15 after the rain, the whole square gone amber-grey with lamp-lit windows, and then the card: "you stood in this yard while the lamps came on, which nobody in brim has thought to do." The job was literally *sit and wait*, the game let time pass beautifully while I did, and then it told me the waiting itself was the clever thing. That, and the field-gate card revealing that I'm the one who left the gate open — two lines in and I knew who I was.

**Worst:** the town wall. About a hundred game-seconds (gameSec 86–163) of running back and forth along an identical grey wall, past the gate twice, with a hint that pointed the wrong way and a map I couldn't read. It's the only stretch where the game went silent and I felt like I was fighting it rather than exploring it.

## 7. To a friend

It's a hand-drawn, one-sheet walking game where you come back to a village after three years and everyone's slightly hurt about it, and you go land by land putting right the little things you left undone — a gate, a clock — by leaning on things, waiting, and reading the notes. Nothing attacks you except a bull that remembers you, and the writing on every card is dry and kind.

Three things I want to do next time:
1. Wait for the bell, then go tell Marget which hand was right and see the stall open.
2. Get the horse across that fence and ride to whatever the third land is — the map has a river and a round thing to the north-east I'm curious about.
3. Find whoever said "I was beginning to think you weren't coming back."

## 8. Scores (0–10)

- **Understandable: 6.** The premise, the jobs and the counters are crystal clear; the controls arrive late and the map and the South Gate cost me a quarter of the session.
- **Alive: 8.** People speak when you walk past, the bull remembers you, the horse comes when you whistle, rain comes and goes, lamps light, footprints stay.
- **Fun: 7.** The bull and the belfry were both properly satisfying. The wall was not.
- **Beautiful: 9.** Ink on paper with wash, and the dusk lamps are the best-looking thing I saw.
