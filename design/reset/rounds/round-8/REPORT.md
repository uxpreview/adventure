# Cold player report: INKLANDS (round 8, desktop, 2026-09-24, branch claude/tier-3)

*Handed back as the sub-agent's final message and saved by the session.*

## 1. First sixty seconds
The title screen said **INKLANDS**, with "a world in one sheet — twelve lands, one pen" under it and one button, "set out". I clicked it. A stick figure was sitting on a bench on a pale green common, with a castle and some tower blocks far off, and "where you woke / THE COMMON" came up. The only other words on screen were **STAND UP**, floating at the side, with no key named. I guessed E, and it worked. Straight away someone called Nell said "Oh. You're back." and a banner said "NELL — GO AND TALK TO HER". I found and read a note pinned to the bench ("back in an hour. — you… the sun has had three years at it"), which hooked me at once. Only after I put it back did the game show the controls: "wasd to walk · drag to look · E or a click to act".

Talking to Nell: she says it's been three years, I can't remember my name, and a card asks what I'll go by. I typed "kit" and it went on the notebook cover. Then "Mind the bull. It knows you." and "RUN." A big scribbled bull was right behind me, with "hold shift to run" on screen. I ran to the gate, got on a horse and brought the bull through the gate. Nell gave me the horse and said "You had twelve things written down when you went. Twelve people. It'll still be in that book under your coat."

**At gameSec 60:** I think the game wants me to go back through a list of twelve people or jobs I left undone three years ago, across the twelve lands, and find out what happened to me. **Sure: 5/10.** Nell had said "twelve people" and "the book", but at that point THE LIST page in my notebook still said "the first page is stuck to the cover. it can wait." At about 112s the list opened: "TWELVE THINGS. THEN I CAN GO HOME." with twelve named lines. After that I'd say 9/10.

## 2. What I understood
- **Goal:** do twelve things for twelve people (Wick, Nell, Marget, Val, Brack, Holt, Amos, Pye, Wren, Joan, the man at the crossing, Dennis), in any order, then go home. Nell told me, and the notebook showed it ("PINNED: TWELVE PLACES. ANY ORDER.").
- **The mystery:** I left three years ago, the night before "the gathering". The east bridge went. I wrote "I'll see to it." I learned this from my own pencil line on Amos's rain table. It was told through a found object, and it landed hard.
- **Controls:** the WASD, drag-to-look and E line was shown, but only after I'd already guessed E. Shift to run was shown during the bull chase. N for notebook was shown ("YOUR NOTEBOOK — N OPENS IT"). H whistles the horse was shown once as a toast. M for the map was a guess that worked. 1 and 2 for dialogue choices were shown as "1 ·" and "2 ·" on screen. Crossing out a line is described in the notebook ("hold a line down"). E dismounts. The horse won't go into water ("He won't take that. Round it.").
- **World:** a hand-inked world in patches. The Common, then the Harrow Downs (hedges and sheep), then the Pale (bleach flats, an oasis, tower blocks). The map fills in with place names as I find or hear about them ("3 of 12 lands walked").
- **The top-left banner** always names the current objective, and it was very useful.

## 4. What reacted
- **Things that answered me:**
  - Standing up made Nell speak at once.
  - The note, the hands, the gutter, the rain table and the oasis each opened a short, well-written card.
  - The name I typed was used right away ("kit, then. Right.").
  - The bull charged, and Nell's "RUN." came with the shift hint.
  - The horse let me on. The bull followed the horse through the gate, and Nell shut it.
  - The notebook JOBS page filled in.
  - The map grew labels as I found places.
  - Crossing into a new land brought up a big place-name title.
  - A dog began following me in the Pale, unasked.
  - Whistling brought the horse the first time.
  - The horse refused the water with its own lines.
  - The Amos choice had a result I could see ("he does not go down the track at night any more").
  - The prompt changed with context: pick up the can, fill it, throw it, put it down, wet the table.
  - The pencil line coming up on the wet board.
  - The two-way choice card that states its cost in plain words.
  - Amos's reply, then a world change: a board goes up by the road.
  - Line 7 in my notebook showed struck through.
- **Things that got nothing, or didn't work as I expected:**
  - The "STAND UP" prompt gave no key. I had to guess.
  - Pressing E past the end of Amos's choice did nothing five times. Only 1 or 2 worked, and nothing told me E was dead there.
  - `click "text=READ AMOS THE DATE"` found nothing. Clicking the box by its position worked.
  - The first time, THE LIST just said the page was stuck, with no hint about when it would open.
  - The second whistle, at the rain table, brought no horse, so I ran on foot for the rest of the session.
  - "DRINK" at the oasis only gave a description. My character didn't drink.
  - After filling the can, the objective still read "FILL THE CAN…" until I wet the table. I couldn't tell whether the can was full.
  - While a card is open I can't move. My 10-second run held nothing because a card was still up.

## 5. What I wanted to do next and couldn't
- **Find Holt at the riverhead.** The map showed it north of the Pale, but I couldn't turn "up on the map" into a direction on screen. There's no compass and no marker in the world. The camera keeps swinging with movement, so WASD directions kept changing under me, and I ran east by mistake.
- **Call the horse back.** The second whistle got nothing and I didn't know why.
- **Mark the map myself.** I'd have liked to click a place on the map and pin it.
- **Look properly at the settlement.** I saw grey tower blocks behind the rain table and wanted to go in, but didn't have time.
- **Check the lost bull choice.** I wondered what "I'll handle it (I shut the gate myself)" would have done differently.

## 6. Best and worst moment
- **Best:** pouring water on the rain table and seeing my own pencil line come up: "THE NIGHT BEFORE THE GATHERING. RAIN. ALL NIGHT… I'LL SEE TO IT. you were here. you knew." Then being asked to read it to Amos or rub it out, with the cost of each spelled out. It gave me goosebumps. The note on the bench was a close second.
- **Worst:** finding my way. The camera kept rolling and sometimes pointed at the ground, and it clipped through a hedge and the riverbank. The oasis vanished from view one second after I'd seen it. I had to guess which key meant "north". And when I rode into the bull's field, the horse was drawn so faintly behind a speech bubble that I only found it by dragging the camera around.
