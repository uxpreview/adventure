# INSPIRATION — what this game is learning from, and from whom

*The canonical list. Everything on it was already cited somewhere in
the design docs, in service of some particular argument; this file is
where it lives so that rewriting an argument cannot lose a reference.*

**How to read an entry.** Every one names **one thing we take** and
**one thing we refuse**, because a reference that is not scoped is just
a favourite. Nothing here is an authority — the same rule the repo
already applies to margins applies to all of it: *an inherited idea is
re-ratified on INKLANDS' own merits or dropped* (WORLD-SYSTEMS, the
inheritance audit).

Entries marked **[owner]** were named by the owner directly and carry
more weight than ones the project picked up while arguing with itself.

## THE BOOK

### *Invisible Cities* — Italo Calvino, 1972 **[owner, 2026-08-29]**

Marco Polo describes fifty-five cities to Kublai Khan. They are sorted
into eleven categories of five — Cities & Memory, Cities & Desire, Thin
Cities, Cities & the Dead — and each description is short, exact and
slightly fabular. The turn, when it comes, is that they are all Venice.

**We take three things, and they are structural rather than decorative:**

1. **The shape.** Twelve lands = twelve waits, in four categories of
   three, and the turn is that they are all the same wait
   (`STORY.md` §2, §5).
2. **The frame.** Polo carries the cities to a Khan who cannot go and
   see them. Our walker carries the lands to each other, because they
   cannot go and see each other — which is why *nobody crosses a border
   but the walker* is a rule of the fiction and not a rendering trick.
3. **The voice.** Short, precise, unhurried, and never explaining the
   joke. **Calvino never once says the cities are Venice.** This is the
   register for every land's own story and for the note cards.

**We refuse its abstraction.** Calvino has no plot and nobody you are
worried about. We need Joan Harrow to matter, and an ending that lands.

## THE BIG OPEN WORLDS

*All six named by the owner, 2026-08-29. The full take/refuse table with
its consequences is `QUESTS.md` §1; this is the standing record.*

### Red Dead Redemption 2 **[owner]**
**Take:** *Strangers* — multi-part, self-contained, tonally distinct
from the main line (our Tier 2). Roadside encounters, the cheapest magic
in open worlds (Tier 4). And a world with business of its own.
**Refuse:** scripted mission corridors with fail states; the honour
meter. Nothing in this game has a meter.

### Grand Theft Auto **[owner]**
**Take:** density of incidental activity, and side content in a
*different genre* from the main story — comedy against crime. That is
why our six tiers are deliberately tonally distinct rather than just
different sizes.
**Refuse:** collectibles-as-content; a map covered in icons.

### Fallout **[owner]**
**Take:** the biggest single debt on this list — **whole stories told
with nothing but the objects left in a room.** Two skeletons in a
bathtub and a teddy bear. That is not an influence for us, it is the
native mode: no faces, no dialogue, no log, no cutscenes. Also the
**unmarked quest** (Tier 5), the **deadpan institutional voice**
(Vault-Tec cheer describing something bleak — our office-park timetable
is already this), and **regions with an ideology** rather than a biome.
**Refuse:** the log, the markers, the miscellaneous-objectives list, the
Pip-Boy (a diegetic menu is still a menu), and the ash. This is a world
with a lark in it. *We take the archaeology and the deadpan; we do not
take the apocalypse.* Full section: WORLD-SYSTEMS §10.

### The Elder Scrolls V: Skyrim **[owner]**
**Take:** faction lines as **parallel campaigns completable in any
order** — the model for the twelve Waits (Tier 1). And "see a thing, go
to it", which is why curiosity here runs on sightlines.
**Refuse:** **radiant quests.** Procedurally generated filler, and
Skyrim's own worst content. We generate nothing. Its caves are the same
lesson, and are why blots-as-caves stay parked.

### The Witcher 3 **[owner]**
**Take:** the hardest bar on this list — **every side quest has a
TURN**, a reveal that recontextualises what you were doing. No filler
contracts. We hold the twelve Waits and the eight Strangers to it.
**Refuse:** the "?" map icons, which CDPR themselves call the weak part.

### Goat Simulator **[owner]**
**Take:** that a world should reward **messing about**; that some
content should exist only to delight; that a secret is allowed to be
just a joke, with no reward and no acknowledgement (Tier 5, THE
UNMARKED).
**This is not a joke entry.** It is the antidote to fifteen hours of a
game about waiting becoming ponderous, and it is the reason our tier
table has a tone column.
**Refuse:** everything else, obviously.

### Breath of the Wild
**Take:** traversal that changes the map — you move differently, so the
world is new. The argument for mounts (WORLD-SYSTEMS §4).
**Refuse:** combat, weapon breakage, and its shrine cadence.

## THE SMALL OPEN WORLDS — the original bar

*QUALITY-BAR §1: the walk people send each other.*

### Alto's Odyssey · Journey · Sable · A Short Hike
**Take:** no quest log, no markers, no urgency, and a world that feels
authored in every frame. From **Journey** in particular: a wordless
world can move somebody — which is the whole bet, since nobody here has
a face. From **Sable**: that fifteen hours of no-combat exploration is a
real and shipped thing.
**Refuse:** the assumption that small means short. *A small map is not a
short game* (WORLD-SYSTEMS §0) — ten minutes is the crossing, and the
target is hours.

## THE KNOWLEDGE GAMES — how a game runs long with no combat

*The engine behind DIRECTION.md's hours argument and WORLD-SYSTEMS §6.
Not owner-named; adopted while working out how fifteen hours is
possible without a single fight.*

### Outer Wilds
**Take:** knowledge as the **only** progression. Nothing unlocks; the
world is open from minute one; what changes is that you understand it.
Its solar system is smaller than our sheet and it is a twenty-hour game.
**Refuse:** the twenty-two-minute loop and the fail state.

### Return of the Obra Dinn
**Take:** deduction as the verb — the world holds the evidence and the
player does the assembling. And that one small, fixed location can carry
ten hours.
**Refuse:** a formal deduction UI. We have no log and are not getting one.

### Tunic
**Take:** that the thing the player is learning can be **how to read the
world**, and that the best gate is the one in their head.
**Refuse:** combat, and its difficulty as a gate.

### Chants of Sennaar
**Take:** **knowledge as literal inventory** — you learn a word, and a
door opens because you now understand it, not because a flag flipped.
This is the direct model for our content system (WORLD-SYSTEMS §6): a
name, a fact, a route, a reason.
**Refuse:** puzzle-box pacing. Our world has to be worth walking even
when you are not solving anything.

## THE LIVING WORLDS — the world changes without you

### Stardew Valley · Animal Crossing
**Take:** that a world which changes on its own — by hour, by day, by
season — **multiplies every piece of content already built**, at no
authoring cost per place. The cheapest hours available to us
(WORLD-SYSTEMS §7). And routine: you learn where people are, and when.
**Refuse:** chores, timers, an economy, and anything that punishes not
playing.

## THE PARENT PROJECT

### margins — `uxpreview/margins`
Where the whole engine came from: the ink library, the paper pass, the
hand-lettering, footprints, the walk cycle, the audio engine, and the
quality-bar discipline that runs these sessions.
**Take:** all of the above. It is why this project could start at a
running pace.
**Refuse: its authority.** margins is *a reference, not an authority*
(QUALITY-BAR §2). Its laws encode its own story, and two of them cost us
real critique rounds before anyone checked: the **flat ground**, which
was a book of flat pages and was never a decision here, and the
**two-blues forgery contract**, which encoded a plot we do not have.
Every inherited rule gets re-ratified or dropped.

## What nothing on this list gets to do

No reference, however good the game, overrides the constraints. Not one
of these gets us combat, a quest log, objective markers, a compass, XP,
loot, currency, levelling, generated content, timers, fail states,
urgency, dialogue trees, or faces.

And one rule sits above all of them, because it is the one this project
has actually drifted on: **the medium is the STYLE, never the SUBJECT**
(`STORY.md` §0). No amount of admiring somebody else's world justifies a
story about the paper.

## Adding to this list

Name the work, name **the one thing** it contributes, name what we
refuse from it, and say which file the idea is applied in. An entry that
cannot do all four is a favourite, not a reference, and belongs in a
conversation rather than in `design/`.
