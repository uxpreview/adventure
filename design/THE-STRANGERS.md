# THE STRANGERS, and the three inventories

*Session 7. `design/QUESTS.md` Tiers 2 through 5, mapped. Tier 2 is
written out, because a stranger has beats and beats need a place and an
order. **Tiers 3, 4 and 5 are INVENTORIES — one line each, and they stay
that way.** Writing them up is how a content plan turns into forty pages
nobody reads, and it is also how a world gets over-specified before it
is built.*

---

## PART ONE — THE STRANGERS *(Tier 2, ~8, cross-land)*

RDR2's word and RDR2's shape, with our own constraint doing the work:

> **A stranger is somebody who needs something from a land they cannot
> reach.**

You are the only thing that crosses, so you are the only possible
solution — and the tier exists to make that **felt** rather than
stated. Nobody ever says "you're the only one who can go there." They
just cannot, and you can, and the arithmetic is left to the player.

**Standing rules for this tier**

1. **Two or three beats, in two or three lands.** Met in one, resolved
   in another. If it resolves where it started it is an errand
   (Tier 3), and it should be demoted rather than padded.
2. **What crosses is knowledge, or one small object.** Never a person,
   never an animal, never a vehicle. Rule 1 of `STORY.md` §8.
3. **The resolution is a visible permanent change in the second land**,
   and usually a small one. Nobody thanks you. Nothing is awarded.
4. **Tonally the widest tier.** One is very funny. One is genuinely
   upsetting. The rest are spread between.

### S1 · THE SECOND OAR
**THE PENWOOD → LONGSHORE → THE PENWOOD**

| beat | where | what |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | the Penwood, off the track | **HALLOWS**, a woodcutter, has carved eleven oars over the years and every one of them is wrong. He has never seen an oar. The boat on the tarn has one and he will not go and measure it, because it is Brack's forty units and everybody keeps them |
| 2 | Longshore, Shelter Cove | Pye's boat, chained, with two oars in it. You look at them. That is the whole beat: **the knowledge is a shape** |
| 3 | the Penwood | the twelfth oar is right |

**The change:** the tarn's boat has two oars. Nobody will ever use it.
It sits on black water with a matched pair shipped, forever.

**Never says:** that it is useless. Hallows knows perfectly well.

### S2 · THE STANDING COMPLAINT — *the funny one*
**THE KINGDOM OF BRIM → CASTLE GREYWEATHER**

| beat | where | what |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Brim, the back streets | **TEG** has drafted a formal complaint about the belfry clock. It is sealed. It has been sealed for a long time, waiting for somebody going up to the castle, and nobody goes up to the castle. There is a second paragraph, added later, in the same hand but older, about how long he has been waiting to send the first paragraph |
| 2 | Greyweather, the gate | Wick receives it with complete ceremony. He reads it. He does not comment |
| 3 | Greyweather, the arch | he **nails it up beside the proclamation** — the one that is weathered past reading, by order of somebody, forbidding or possibly requiring something |

**The change:** two illegible notices on one arch. The complaint will be

Everybody involved is satisfied. Nothing whatsoever has happened.

**Never says:** that it will not be actioned. That it was ever going to
be. That this is funny.

*This is the tier's Vault-Tec slot and it works in exact proportion to
how straight everyone plays it (`STORY.md` §8 rule 3). Nobody in this
world is doing a bit. Teg is sincerely aggrieved, Wick is sincerely
diligent, and the joke is entirely structural.*

### S3 · THE ELEVEN UNITS — *the upsetting one*
**MAPLE COURT → GREYLINE CITY → MAPLE COURT** *(built end to end, Session 13)*

| beat | where | what |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Maple Court, the last house before the border | **JUNE** leaves the gate on the latch. Not the porch light — the gate. The latch plate is worn bright from being lifted and set back, every night, for years |
| 2 | Greyline City, the junction | the man who stands still. You have already seen the pavement worn into paths that curve round him |
| 3 | Maple Court, the border fence | you bring back what you saw. Not a name — he has none — just that there is somebody, and where |

**The change:** June walks to the fence at the end of the road, forty
units from where he stands, on the other side of a line neither of them
can cross, and **she stays there.** There is now a second person in this
world standing still and waiting to be asked. She is there in every
later save.

**Never says:** who they are to each other. That they are waiting for
each other. That forty units is nothing. **Especially not that.**

**AND THE GEOGRAPHY MEASURED IT, SESSION 13.** This file said *forty
units*; the world's own roads say **ninety-three**. The junction is
where mill lane actually crosses main street, at (148, 203), and the
last fence on the Maple Court side is at the border, at (56, 197); the
number was never measured because neither end existed when it was
written. Nothing about the beat changes and the number in the sentence
above is left as it was written, because it is the FEELING and not the
measurement — but a later session should not go looking for forty units
between them, and **the sentence the game must never say is now true by
a wider margin than the file thought.**

*A note on why this is allowed to hurt: the world's mercy here is real
and it is small. For years she did not know he was there. Now she does,
and she is standing as close as this world permits. That it is not
enough is the entire game, and this is the one place the game is allowed
to make the player feel the shape of it — because it never explains it,
and because it is one stranger out of eight.*

### S4 · THE LOUDEST THING
**GREYLINE CITY → THE WIDE BLUE → GREYLINE CITY**

| beat | where | what |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Greyline City, a doorway off the junction | **KAY** has never heard silence. The city hums, at every hour, and she has begun to suspect the hum is not the city |
| 2 | THE LONG WATER, sixty units from anything | it is. Out here there is nothing to hear at all, and it is not restful, and it is the loudest thing in the world |
| 3 | Greyline City, the junction, between three and four in the morning | you stand at the lights with her. All four are green. Nobody comes. **The city does hum**, and now she can hear where it stops |

**The change:** Kay stops covering the vent behind her doorway. It has
been muffled with a coat for years. The coat is on a hook now, and the
doorway is louder, and she prefers it.

**Never says:** that silence is worse. That it is better.

*The hour matters and the clock exists, so this fires only in the small
hours (WORLD-SYSTEMS §7). It is the one stranger you cannot finish by
accident.*

### S5 · HOW DEEP
**SPLITROCK CANYON → THE BLEACH FLATS → THE WIDE BLUE → SPLITROCK**

The one three-land stranger, and the tier's widest walk.

| beat | where | what |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Splitrock, the floor | **ODD** has measured the canyon: ten point eight units from lip to floor, taken twice, with a line. He would like to know whether the sea is deeper. Nobody here has seen water past ankle depth |
| 2 | the Bleach Flats, the oasis | the only other water anybody out here has heard of. It is a hand deep. Odd's line would not get wet in it |
| 3 | THE LONG WATER | you cannot see the bottom, and the line does not reach, and there is no way to tell how much further it goes |
| 4 | Splitrock, the wall | you tell him what happened to the line |

**The change:** a new chalk mark on the canyon wall, **below the floor**,
with nothing written beside it. It is a mark for a depth that cannot be
measured and it stays chalked.

**Never says:** how deep the sea is. Nobody knows and nobody in this
world ever will.

*Session 11 note, and it is the reason this entry is untouched: that
land's tear was moved forty units west into the middle of its own rect,
which is a layout-wide audit — and **the amplitude was re-tuned until
`tools/check-terrain.mjs` printed `floor -10.8` again**, to a tenth of a
unit, in the new position. Odd's line is still ten point eight units
long. A session may move a landform; it may not move a number somebody
else's errand stands on.*

### S8 · THE NAME ON THE PLINTH
**THE CUBICLE MILE → CASTLE GREYWEATHER → THE CUBICLE MILE**

The most Calvino of the eight, and the quietest.

| beat | where | what |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | the Cubicle Mile, the stop | Dennis reads the timetable's twelve stops in order, daily, from memory. One of the names he has never been sure how to say, because it is not a word anybody uses down here, and he has been saying it wrong for thirty years without knowing |

think so. He has simply learned that one of them is old.

| THE HARROW DOWNS | S6 |
| THE CUBICLE MILE | S8 ×2 |
| **THE COMMON** | **none, on purpose** |

**THE COMMON hosts no stranger.** It is where you wake up and it is Act
I's whole classroom; making it a hub would turn the crossroads into a
quest board, which is the one thing a crossroads must never become. The
Common gets its wait (Nell), its errands and its encounters, and nothing
that sends you anywhere.

**THE BLEACH FLATS is touched once, in passing**, and that is correct
for a land whose entire thesis is that the answer is elsewhere. It is
the only land in the game that is somebody else's middle beat.

## Canon settled while writing this

Two things came up that `STORY.md` does not say and that the next
session would have had to guess. Both are recorded here rather than
invented twice.

- **"The people who left" did not leave the land.** Nobody crosses a
  border — that is the engine, and a land full of emigrants would
  retract it. Maple Court's absent are people who left the ROAD: they
  are somewhere in Maple Court, or they are not, and **nobody can go and
  see**, which is the same thing here. The porch lights are on for
  people who are, as far as anybody on that road can establish, a bit
  further away than usual.
- **Objects may cross; people, animals and vehicles may not.** A
  cutting, a complaint, a measurement, the shape of an oar. The walker
  carries them because the walker is the only thing that goes. This is
  the licence Tier 2 runs on and it is deliberately narrow: **if a
  stranger's resolution needs anything with legs to change land, the
  stranger is wrong and gets rewritten.**
