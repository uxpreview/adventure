# Inklands: Story Overview

## Current Premise

The protagonist wakes on a bench beside a village road with no memory of
who he is.

A note in his handwriting says:

> BACK IN AN HOUR.\
> --- You

A nearby resident recognizes him and says:

> "You're back."

He does not know her, the village, or why people seem relieved,
suspicious, annoyed, or hurt to see him.

He discovers a notebook containing twelve promises, each pointing toward
a person, place, or unresolved problem.

## His Former Role

He was not the creator of Inklands and did not draw the world into
existence.

He traveled between regions and helped keep them connected. He
maintained routes, repaired practical problems, carried messages, helped
people complete important tasks, and linked communities.

Over time, this became unhealthy. He began to believe every problem was
his responsibility and that people would only love or need him if he
remained useful.

He tried to hold everything together alone rather than asking for help,
setting limits, or allowing others to take responsibility.

## The Disappearance and Memory Loss

The exact event is not finalized.

The leading direction is that the memory loss was not random. He
deliberately or semi-deliberately escaped, abandoned, or accepted the
loss of his memories because remembering had become unbearable.

This needs a concrete emotional event rather than a vague explanation
that he was simply overwhelmed.

The story must establish: - What happened on the day he left - Whom he
disappointed or hurt - What choice he made - Why forgetting seemed
preferable to remembering - Why he returned - Whether anyone helped
cause or preserve the memory loss

## What Happened to Inklands

While he was gone, connections between regions deteriorated in practical
and social ways: - Roads became overgrown - Bridges became unsafe -
Gates stayed shut - Lamps went out - Waterways became difficult to
cross - Railway infrastructure was abandoned - Signs became inaccurate -
Messages failed to arrive - People forgot routines, names, or
obligations

This is not magical ink fading. The world is less connected because the
systems and relationships that linked it were neglected.

## The Twelve Promises

Each promise should: - Introduce a distinct character or region -
Provide a playful or unusual activity - Reveal something about his
past - Show a consequence of his absence - Complicate the player's
understanding of him - Contribute to the mystery of why he left

The promises should escalate from local problems to an interconnected
pattern of impossible responsibility.

Not every promise needs a new tool. Some can unlock routes,
relationships, shortcuts, home-base features, traversal options, or new
understanding.

## Emotional Core

He believed:

> I have to solve everyone's problems to deserve belonging.

The story gradually teaches him:

> Some people loved him even when he was not fixing anything.

Joan's farm is an important emotional anchor. Details such as a second
plate, a familiar room, or a routine that continued in his absence
should reveal that he had a place where he could simply exist. He was
not merely useful to Joan and the others.

## Narrative Shape

### Act I: Recognition Without Memory

He meets people who know him and begins completing small promises. The
tone is playful and exploratory. Different people remember different
versions of him: helpful, irritating, reliable, exhausting, funny,
absent, and loved.

### Act II: The Cost of Holding Everything Together

The promises become interconnected. He learns that some problems were
worsened by his earlier decisions, unfinished plans, or refusal to
delegate. The question changes from "Who was I?" to "What did I do to
myself and everyone else?"

### Act III: Responsibility, Forgiveness, and Choice

He confronts the possibility that restoring everything exactly as it was
may be impossible or undesirable. The final choice concerns whether he
repeats his old pattern of control or accepts help, shared
responsibility, and change.

## Open Questions

1.  The exact cause of the memory loss
2.  Whether it was voluntary, magical, traumatic, or combined
3.  Whether a foil such as Morrow is necessary
4.  Whether the railway belongs in the ending
5.  The order and content of the twelve promises
6.  The final ending and choices
