# Inklands: Critique 3 (2026-09-16)

*Against file 06. The foundation holds. This is where it does not yet,
and the one change that locks most of the loose parts together. The
theme is untouched: you do not have to be indispensable to belong.*

## The biggest fix: make it one day

There are eight threads: the disastrous day, the unnamed emergency, the
gathering he never organised, the 8:15 with no meaning, the
communication network, Morrow, the dog, Joan's secret. They read as
parallel systems. They become one story if **the disastrous day was the
gathering.**

He organised it. Every region was coming to the Common for the first
time. It was set for 8:15, and Dennis kept the board. That morning the
footbridge went (the one the child still asks about). He said "I'll
handle it" and went to fix it alone. It took all day. Nobody else knew
how to run the gathering, because he had never let them, so it fell apart
while they waited: Joan at the table, Morrow at the bench with the dog,
the man at the crossing holding the route open for guests who never came,
every region's bell and horn and lamp calling into the gap where he
should have been. That night Joan said the line. He left.

That gives you:
- The 8:15, concrete, with no railway: the time on the board.
- The emergency, named: the bridge, still broken because nobody will
  touch his job. Morrow "fixed" it.
- The network as the thing that failed that day, not a system to restore.
- The ending, inevitable: the same gathering, three years on, run by
  everyone but him.
- Morrow's climax as a literal replay of that day.
- Twelve promises as twelve witnesses of one day.

## Emotional logic

**The timeline has a hole.** He left three years ago, Pye found him in
the water and brought him back, and he wakes after three years away.
Where was he? Fix: Pye found him last week, past the mark, *coming in,
not going out.* What happened in between is unknown to everyone, him
included, and stays unknown.

**"Someone told him the truth" should be Joan.** If it is the last thing
she said before he vanished, her later silence becomes guilt: she believes
her true sentence nearly killed him. Her arc: waited, spoke, went quiet,
speaks again.

**Take the dog off the list of broken promises.** Its rehoming is a
consequence of the day, not a promise on it.

**"Then I can go home" needs an answer.** Home is Joan's table. The
condition was his invention; the door was never conditional.

**The notebook's date.** If the twelve predate the night, Pye's eighth
pot cannot "contain something from the sea journey" as written. Keep the
old wording, give it new contents: Pye set the eighth pot after he pulled
him out, and what is in it came out of the water with him. Say this for
every promise that touches the night.

## Characters, pacing, network, ending

**Habits, not just a flaw.** He straightens signs when idle; the sit verb
takes effort; "I'll handle it" is a dialogue option always available and
more expensive every time. The arc as a button.

**Morrow has the dog.** One caretaker, not two. Age him: twelve then,
fifteen now.

**Order the reveals.** 4 to 6 show *that* he left on purpose (the lake,
footprints toward the coast); 7 to 9 show *how* (Pye); 10 to 12 show
*why* (Joan says the line again). The man at the crossing is gated on
step two.

**The middle needs a comedy.** Amos: the date it last rained is the day of
the gathering, the one day nobody was in the fields.

**The network, as one relay.** Every land has its own way of calling, in
its own idiom, and none reaches the next land: the castle's bell, Brim's
belfry, the suburb's porch lights, the surfers' hand sign, the barista
calling names, the Vikings' horn, the lights over the desert. He was the
relay. Restored as a side effect of the promises (cut the hedge and the
porch lights show from the green; ring the bell at the right hour and the
castle hears it), never as a checklist. The finale: the call for the
gathering passing land to land in one continuous shot, bell to horn to
lamp to shout. The visual ending the train used to be, from things that
exist.

**The gathering is on the Common, around the bench.** "You coming?" is
asked from fifty feet away. The final image is him on the bench watching
the thing he broke happen without him.

**Cut "leave honestly."** It undercuts the theme (the resolution is
belonging) and breaks the final image ("in a minute" answers an
invitation to stay). Keep two endings as one gesture: he rewrites the
first page. He typed his name at the start; he types the first line at
the end, in his own words, hand-lettered, and it becomes the title
screen. Then he keeps the notebook or hands it to Morrow. The player
writes the ending instead of picking it.

**Bring the name back at the end.** Joan tells him his real one when she
tells him the rest. The rewritten first page can carry either.

**One rule for the whole doc:** nobody in Inklands ever says the theme.
"Accept limits" and "self-forgiveness" are for us. In the game the theme
is said once, by Joan, as an accusation, not a lesson.
