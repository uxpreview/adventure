# Inklands: Opening Sequence

## Opening Goal

Make the player feel the mystery before explaining it.

Establish: - The playful tone - The protagonist's amnesia - The fact
that everyone knows him - The notebook and twelve promises - The
connected mixed-era world - His instinct to be useful - A hint that he
placed impossible expectations on himself

Do not explain the entire backstory.

## Scene 1: The Bench

He wakes on a bench beside a village road.

He has: - A half-eaten sandwich - One untied shoe - A notebook beneath
his coat - A note pinned to the bench

The note says:

> BACK IN AN HOUR.\
> --- You

He does not remember writing it.

## Scene 2: The First Recognition

A woman hanging laundry notices him.

> "Oh. You're back."

Possible responses: - "Do I know you?" - "Where am I?" - "Was I gone?" -
"Is this your sandwich?"

She is concerned but not terrified.

> "You said you'd only be gone an hour."

The hour is his own promise, not a global countdown.

## Scene 3: The First Small Task

A goose has gotten into her kitchen and refuses to leave. It has knocked
over potatoes and behaves as though it owns the house.

This introduces walking, moving objects, chasing or redirecting animals,
and light environmental problem-solving.

Afterward, she says:

> "There. That's more like you."

Then:

> "I was beginning to think you weren't coming back."

The emotional shift should be subtle.

## Scene 4: The Village Knows Him

As he walks through the village: - A child asks, "Did you fix the
bridge?" - A shopkeeper says, "You still owe me three buttons." - A dog
recognizes him, then becomes confused when he does not recognize it. -
Someone begins calling him by a name, but the moment is interrupted.

Each encounter shows a different version of him: someone dependable,
irritating, missed, hurt, or loved.

He realizes he has a history here but no access to it.

## Scene 5: The Notebook

The first page says:

> TWELVE THINGS.\
> THEN I CAN GO HOME.

The first promise might read:

> **1. WICK**\
> Find out why the old road is closed.\
> Don't let him tell you it's nothing.

He asks who Wick is.

The woman replies:

> "You know Wick."

Then:

> "You used to."

This suggests he has forgotten relationships, not just facts.

## Scene 6: First View of Inklands

Wick's problem leads to the edge of the village, where a blocked road,
fallen sign, collapsed footbridge, or stuck gate prevents travel.

After solving it, the player reaches a viewpoint and sees: - A castle -
A modern suburb - An office tower - A railway toward the woods - A
market road leading somewhere stranger

The mixed-era world is normal to its inhabitants. The reveal should feel
wondrous, not like a lore puzzle.

## Scene 7: The Second Note

At the bench, someone has left a note:

> YOU DON'T HAVE TO DO ALL TWELVE.

On the back, in his handwriting:

> YES, I DO.

This foreshadows his former belief that everything depended on him.

## Scene 8: The Forward Pull

A distant bell rings. A sign points toward the next region. The notebook
opens to the second promise.

The player has a clear next step, while the larger mystery remains
unresolved.

## Opening Principles

-   Start with a funny human-scale problem.
-   Let confusion create the exposition.
-   Reveal the world through movement.
-   Show emotional histories with the protagonist.
-   Make the player curious about his past.
-   Keep the cause of the memory loss hidden.
-   Foreshadow his compulsive responsibility.
