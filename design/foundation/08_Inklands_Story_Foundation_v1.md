## 1. The game

Inklands is a third-person open-world exploration game drawn in ballpoint
and watercolour, played in a browser on a phone or a laptop. One
continuous sheet, twelve places from twelve centuries a few minutes'
walk apart, and nobody who lives there finds that odd. You walk, ride
(horse, bicycle, rowboat, office chair), talk to people who answer in
hand-lettered bubbles, do small jobs, and mess with things. No combat, no
death, no classes, no skill tree.

The paper-and-ink look is the style of the game and never its subject.
No story about ink, pages, drawing, or who drew it.

Tone: playful, absurd, warm, melancholy, sincere. Humour comes from people
and the world's odd logic. Deadpan. Nobody in Inklands is doing a bit.

Rules of the fiction:
1. Nobody says the theme. Joan says it once, as an accusation, on the
   night he left, and once more at the end when she tells him.
2. Stakes are specific and small: a bridge, a lamp, a bell, a name.
3. Every promise ends with a visible, permanent change and a choice with
   a loser on both sides. The world reads choices back within a minute.
4. The world explains people, not itself. Twelve centuries on one road is
   never explained.

## 2. The theme, and how it is told

**You do not have to be indispensable to belong.**

Told through:
- **His habits.** He straightens signs when idle. The sit verb takes
  effort (a held press, a visible fidget) until the end. He cannot pass a
  broken thing.
- **"I'll handle it."** A dialogue option available in nearly every
  conversation. It always works. Each use costs something visible: the
  person who offered stops offering, sits down, or waits. The cost
  compounds through the game and peaks at the gathering.
- **Twelve people who remember a different man.** Reliable, exhausting,
  funny, absent, loved.
- **A dog that has another person now.**
- **A kid doing his job his way.**

## 4. Three years ago: the day

The first gathering of all twelve lands, on the Common, at 8:15 in the
evening. He organised every part of it. He was to walk the circuit at
noon and confirm to each land, and each land would send its call.

It rained the night before. The only rain anyone remembers. The river
rose and took the plank bridge on the east road, which six lands would
cross.

- **Dawn.** He wakes at Joan's. She lays the second plate. "Sit down
  tonight. Just once." He says he will. He gives Morrow (twelve) the
  bench and the table to mind till 8:15, with the dog. "Back in an hour."
- **Seven.** The bull gets out through the gate he had promised Nell he
  would fix. "Later."
- **Eight.** The bridge. Amos arrives with cans for the feast and offers
  to hold the plank. "I'll handle it." Joan sends word: take Holt, he
  knows water. "I'll handle it." Morrow runs down to help. "Wait at the
  bench." Three refusals before nine.
- **Noon.** Nobody has the confirm, so nobody calls. Marget will not ring
  without knowing the hour. Wick waits for the bell. Val's street will not
  light first. Dennis stands at the board. The man at the crossing holds
  the city's lights green from six regardless. Pye's boat waits at the
  cove. The lantern at the wood gate stays dark. Wren's fleet sails with
  nobody at the bend to call the finish. The Vikings, promised they would
  land for the first time in four hundred years, roar at an empty shore.
- **Dusk.** The bridge is finished. His best work. Nobody crosses it.
- **8:15.** One long table on the Common, laid. The bull in the middle of
  it, eating the bread. Morrow asleep on the bench with the dog. Joan
  clearing up alone.
- **Night.** He rights the chairs. Goes for the bull. Starts to say how he
  will make it up. Joan: "Sit down." He cannot. Then: **"You always come
  when there's something to fix. You never come when there's nothing you
  can do."** She puts the second plate away and goes home.

He writes two notes. "I'M NOT COMING BACK" he cannot leave, and puts in
his coat. "BACK IN AN HOUR. — You" he pins to the bench beside the
sleeping kid. He takes the notebook, because he cannot leave that either.
He walks west past the well, down to the cove, takes Pye's waiting boat,
and rows out past the mark.

## 5. The three years

The Vikings' longship has been beyond the mark for four hundred years and
cannot land. They took him out of the water and could not put him
ashore. He rowed with them, every day, round the mark, in the regatta: he
was the crew of the boat that has been last since anyone was counting and
is gaining. Three years among the only people in the world he could not
help, and they liked him. He never said "I'll handle it" once, because
there was nothing to handle.

Nobody ashore knew. Nobody goes past the mark, and the Vikings cannot
come in to say.

## 6. Last week

Morrow, fifteen, rang Brim's bell at the wrong hour, trying to call a
gathering he could not run. He heard it from the fleet and went over the
side toward it. Pye found him inside the mark, coming in, cold and
half-drowned, in Pye's own boat. The near-drowning took his memory of
everything before the sea. Pye took the coat and the notebook out of the
water with him.

Pye told Joan. Joan and Pye laid him on the bench at night, under the
note nobody had taken down. Joan's reasons, given only at the end: "If
you'd woken in my kitchen you'd have fixed the kitchen," and she wanted
him to read his own note before anyone told him anything. She kept the
name. Pye kept the coat, and what was in it, in an eighth pot set on a
bearing nobody rows, unread, because Pye does not read other people's
letters and would not decide for him.

## 7. The opening

The title screen is the Common at dusk from the ridge, the whole world in
frame, one long table, one bench.

1. **The bench.** He wakes. A half-eaten sandwich, one untied shoe, the
   notebook under his coat, the note pinned above him: BACK IN AN HOUR.
   — You.
2. **Nell**, hanging laundry: "Oh. You're back." / "Do I know you?" /
   "You said you'd only be gone an hour." A beat. "It's been three years."
3. **The name.** She asks what to call him, since he plainly does not
   know. The player types one. It is lettered onto the notebook cover.
4. **The bull.** It is loose on the Common and it knows him. It comes.
   RUN. Nell whistles the horse and hands him the reins. GET IT HOME:
   the bull follows the horse; Nell stands in the open gate; she shuts it.
   The horse is his. First job, four minutes, and the shape of his whole
   story: a thing everyone waited for him to deal with.
5. **The village knows him.** Three encounters on the walk back, no more:
   the child (now Morrow, fifteen, who does not stop): "Did you fix the
   bridge?" Marget's messenger: three buttons. The dog, which comes, then
   hesitates, then goes back to Morrow.
6. **The notebook.** First page: TWELVE THINGS. THEN I CAN GO HOME. Below
   it, twelve lines in his hand. One is crossed out. The map opens with
   twelve places pinned.
7. **The second note**, on the bench when he returns: YOU DON'T HAVE TO
   DO ALL TWELVE. On the back, in his handwriting, older: YES, I DO. (Hers.
   From before. He does not know that yet.)
8. **The pull.** From the green, the castle on the skyline, Brim's wall,
   the sea to the west, the office towers to the south-east. Everything
   he will ever go to is in frame. The bell rings the wrong hour.

## 8. The notebook

Written the week before the gathering: twelve things that had to be true
for twelve lands to come. His order is preserved. The player may do them
in any order; later lines need routes the earlier ones open, and the
reveal ladder is gated on knowledge, not on count.

The first page: **TWELVE THINGS. THEN I CAN GO HOME.** Home is Joan's
table. The condition was his invention. The door was never conditional.

The twelve, verbatim:

1. Wick. Find out why the old road is closed. Don't let him tell you it's nothing.
2. Nell. Get the bull home.
3. Marget. I owe her. Pay it.
4. Val. Cut the gap in the hedge.
5. Brack. Go and look in the lake.
6. Holt. Find out how much water.
7. Amos. When did it last rain?
8. Pye. Row the eighth pot out.
9. Wren. Give the race a finish line.
10. ~~Joan. Be there for the harvest.~~
11. The man at the crossing. Come back.
12. Dennis. The 8:15. Don't miss it.

The player can cross a line out. The notebook does not object. What the
world does about it is the content.

## 9. The twelve promises

Each entry: the old meaning (what it was for, the week before), what
happened that day, the task now, the turn, the choice, what it reveals,
what it reconnects, what it unlocks.

### Tier 1: the capable helper (1 to 3). Literal, funny, plainly good.

**1. WICK. The old road.** *Old meaning:* the king's road down from the
castle had to be open for the castle to come. *That day:* Wick waited for
the bell, lit the braziers alone at dusk, and chained the road the next
morning. He has lit them every night since. *Now:* the road is chained.
Wick says it's nothing. It is not nothing: he closed it so nobody would
see him waiting. *Turn:* the braziers are not for the king. They are the
castle's call, and he has been sending it nightly to a bell that never
rings. *Choice:* open the road, or leave it and carry his messages
yourself (the old way; the world reads it back). *Reconnects:* castle to
Brim by sight. *Unlocks:* the castle hall as home base.

**2. NELL. The bull.** The opening. *That day:* the gate he did not fix.
*Turn:* Nell could always pen it herself. She stopped when he started.
*Unlocks:* the horse.

**3. MARGET. The debt.** *Old meaning:* she fronted the stalls for the
gathering. *That day:* the bell was never rung; the stalls have stood set
and unopened for three years. *Now:* she wants paying and he has nothing.
*Turn:* the debt is not money. She covered for him the week before when
he could not do everything, and never said so. It is paid by getting the
market called: wait in the belfry yard until the lamps come on and one
hand agrees with them. *Choice:* which hand is right. One person in Brim
is wrong forever. *Reconnects:* the bell, rung at a known hour.
*Unlocks:* WAIT (passing time).

### Tier 2: the consequences of his methods (4 to 6). He left on purpose.

**4. VAL. The hedge.** *Old meaning:* the court and the green argued over
who would host the overflow; he promised to settle it at three. *That
day:* he did not come. The hedge was planted closed. *Now:* the three
chairs face a hedge. *Turn:* the gap lets the porch lights be seen from
the Common, and reopens the argument. *Choice:* cut it and take a side,
or leave it. *Reveals:* the first faded footprints, his, going west
through the Common the night he left. *Reconnects:* porch lights to the
green. *Unlocks:* the hedge clippers (overgrown paths everywhere).

**5. BRACK. The lake.** *Old meaning:* the lantern at the wood gate had to
be lit for the wood folk to come. *That day:* it was not. *Now:* Brack
will not go within forty paces of the tarn. *Turn:* the footprints go to
the water's edge and do not come back, and the lantern is on the bank,
where he set it down to go on in the dark. He went through the wood to
the coast that night. Something in the water took the lantern's light
and has been ticking for it since. *Choice:* light the lantern and leave
it at the gate (the wood's call), or carry it (a night toy, and the wood
stays dark). *Reveals:* that he left, on foot, on purpose. *Unlocks:* the
lantern; night in the wood.

**6. HOLT. The water.** *Old meaning:* Holt's canyon had a channel he had
rigged so the canyon could spare water for the feast. *That day:* the
channel was his alone; when he left, nobody could maintain it; the
canyon dried. *Now:* Holt wants him to fix it. *Turn:* what Holt needs is
the Flats, who have the oasis, and the two lands have not spoken since.
*Choice:* fix the channel yourself (works; nobody learns it) or walk
Holt to Amos. *Reveals:* Amos saw him at the bridge that morning and was
refused. *Reconnects:* the echo, which comes back right when the Flats
answer. *Unlocks:* the boat on the canyon river.

### Tier 3: the list is wrong (7 to 9). How he left.

**7. AMOS. The rain.** The comedy. *Old meaning:* Amos had asked, for the
feast, whether the ground would hold. *That day:* it rained. *Now:* Amos
wants the date it last rained to win an argument with the Downs about a
harvest. *Turn:* the rainfall table has one entry, in his own hand, and
it is the night before the gathering. The only rain in memory took the
bridge. *Choice:* give Amos the date (he wins the argument; Joan loses
it) or keep it. *Reconnects:* the lights over the pan. *Unlocks:* the
kite, for wind on the flats.

**8. PYE. The eighth pot.** *Old meaning:* a marker further out than the
seven, so guests coming by sea could find the cove. *That day:* Pye's boat
waited for guests; that night it was taken. *Now:* the pot is set, on a
bearing Pye does not row. *Turn:* it holds his coat, and in the coat the
note he could not leave: I'M NOT COMING BACK, in his handwriting. Pye's
line: "I found you past the mark. Coming in, not going out." *Choice:*
read it, or drop it back in. *Reveals:* how he left, in his own words,
without a memory. *Reconnects:* the horn on the point.

**9. WREN. The finish line.** *Old meaning:* the race was to finish at the
river bend at 8:15, and the Vikings were promised they could land.
*That day:* nobody called it; the fleet went round; the Vikings roared at
nothing. *Now:* Wren wants a finish. *Turn:* rowing out to set the second
mark, the last boat comes alongside and the Vikings know him. He rowed
with them for three years. *Choice:* set the finish at the bend under the
old rules (the fleet ends; the Vikings stay out) or write a new rule
that lets the longship in. *Reveals:* where he was. *Reconnects:* the
bell buoy and the longship's horn. *Unlocks:* the sea.

### Tier 4: the people (10 to 12). Why.

**10. JOAN. Be there.** The line is crossed out already, in his hand,
because he was there for every harvest. The promise he broke is not
written. *Now:* the second plate is laid. *Task:* SIT DOWN. The sit verb
costs effort here more than anywhere. *Turn:* she does not need anything
fixed and will not give him anything to fix. If he tries, she waits.
*Reveals:* nothing yet. She does not tell him until the gathering.
*Unlocks:* rest. This is home, and the game does not say so.

**11. THE MAN AT THE CROSSING. Come back.** *That day:* he held the city's
lights green from six for a crowd that never came, and has stood there
since. *Now:* gated on the eighth pot. *Task:* not arriving. Telling him
the truth: that he left, and why he did not say. *Choice:* tell him, or
hold the lights with him (the old way: two men standing still). *Turn:*
when told, he goes and sits on the bench twenty units off, and the city,
for the first time, stops. *Reconnects:* the barista calls the name.

**12. DENNIS. The 8:15.** *Old meaning:* the time. Dennis kept the board.
*That day:* he stood at it. He has kept the time on it for three years,
and the sprint pinned him up as a persona. *Now:* the board. *Task:* set
the date for the next gathering, and then do not walk the circuit. *Turn:*
the call goes without him (§10). *Reconnects:* everything. *Unlocks:* the
gathering.

## 10. The relay

Every land has its own way of calling and none reaches the next. He was
the relay. The promises reconnect them as side effects, never as a
system to switch on: cut the hedge and the porch lights show from the
green; ring the bell at a known hour and the castle hears it; answer the
canyon from the Flats and the echo comes back right.

When Dennis's date is set, the call passes once, in one continuous shot:
Morrow shouts down the well. Marget rings the hour. Wick lights on the
bell. Val's street lights up. The barista calls it. Dennis's board; the
tannoy. The lights over the pan. The echo comes back right. Joan hears
it and starts walking. Brack lights the lantern at the wood gate. The
horn on the point. The longship answers; the bell buoy rings. The well
answers last, on its delay.

Any land whose promise was not kept stays dark in the chain, and the
shot shows it.

## 11. Morrow

Fifteen. Twelve when he was told to wait at the bench. Kept the dog. Kept
the note up. Copied the notebook from memory, wrong in places, with
additions. Has been doing the job for three years, alone, his way:
"Already handled." He fixed the bridge badly and nobody uses it. He rang
the bell at the wrong hour and it brought the man back, which he does not
know.

His fear: that the return makes three years meaningless. His flaw: the
same one. He refuses help and says so in the same words.

He is a parallel quest-giver, sometimes ahead of the player, sometimes
having made it worse. He knows the note and the bench and the bull. He
does not know the sea.

## 12. The dog

Lives with Morrow. Recognises him and goes back to Morrow. Over the game,
through ordinary things (walking old routes, sitting, stopping), it
spends time with both. It rests near him when he stops trying to do
something, and never when he is doing something. It notices things and
finds paths. It is not a quest marker. At the end it moves between them
at the table.

## 13. Joan

The only person in the world who never needed him. She laid a second plate
every day for three years. She said the line. She believes it nearly
killed him, which is why she went quiet and why she laid him on the bench
and not at her table. She has his name and gives it at the end. The game
does not decide whether she was right to wait. She does not apologise.
She explains.

## 14. The endgame

**The day of.** Morrow has taken charge. At seven the bull gets out.
Morrow goes after it alone, leaving the bell, the table, and the guests
at the bridge. "You'll take over. It's mine." The day begins to fail
exactly as before.

**The response.** "I'll handle it" is available everywhere and cheap. If
the player does it all: the gathering happens, half the table is empty,
Morrow is not on the bench, Joan does not say "sit down," and the
rewritten page reads as what it is. Nothing says so.

The other way: take Morrow's list and hand each line to a person, on
foot, which is his old job done once more with a different message: *you
do it.* Marget rings the bell herself. Nell pens her own bull. Pye rows
the guests. Wick comes down the hill without a banner. Val's street
lights up first. Then Morrow, cornered at the river bend with the bull.
Do not take the rope. Hold the gate. "I said I'd handle it." "I know.
Hold the rope." The bull goes home with both of them.

**8:15.** Every road has people on it. The longship beaches at the bend,
the first time in four hundred years. The man at the crossing has left
the crossing and the city's lights are red. Dennis brings the board. The
sprint bring their sticky notes. The dog moves between Morrow and him. He
rights a chair. Joan: "Sit down." He does, and it costs nothing.

**Joan tells him.** The day. That she said it, and what. That Pye found
him. That she laid him on the bench under his own note: "If you'd woken
in my kitchen you'd have fixed the kitchen." His name, said whole, once.
If he has the note from the pot he can give it to her. She reads it.
"Well. You were wrong."

**The bench.** Later, the gathering behind him. He opens the notebook to
TWELVE THINGS. THEN I CAN GO HOME, crosses it out, and the player types
the new first line. It is hand-lettered as they type. Three faint pencil
suggestions can be ignored (I CAME BACK / ASK FIRST / DON'T DO IT ALONE).
Morrow sits at the other end with the dog. The player keeps the notebook
or hands it over; if handed, Morrow reads the line. Last verb: TAKE THE
NOTE DOWN.

From the table: "You coming?"
"Yeah. In a minute."

The camera pulls back over the Common and the whole map at night, lit
land by land, dark where a promise was not kept. The title screen
re-letters with the new first line, and his name.

## 15. Side content

Plenty that is not the twelve. Some fun, some strange, some touching,
some meaning nothing. Toys: stone skimming, the paper plane, the office
chair, the bicycle time trial, the lantern at night, the bell that
answers the horn. Twelve stamps. Hats. Errands and strangers as in the
archive, re-keyed to the world after the day. Rule: side content is
never secretly about his trauma.

## 16. Cut

The railway as story. Ink, pages, the drawing as subject. The twelve
waits and the turn nobody says. "Leave honestly." A separate caretaker
for the dog. A network of seven systems. The Ink Key. The goose.
