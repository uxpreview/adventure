# Inklands: Core Vision

## Purpose

This document defines the foundational identity of *Inklands*. These
principles should remain stable unless a proposed change clearly
strengthens the game without losing its identity.

## Core Concept

*Inklands* is a third-person, open-world exploration game about a person
who wakes up without his memories in a strange, hand-drawn world where
nearly everyone seems to know him.

He has returned after being absent for an unknown amount of time. He
discovers that he previously traveled between regions, maintained
connections, helped people, and made twelve promises.

The central mystery is both: - What happened to him? - Why did he leave,
and why did he believe he had to fix everything?

## World

-   One continuous open world with no loading screens between regions.
-   Distinct areas inspired by different places, periods, and social
    environments.
-   A castle, market town, village green, modern suburb, pine forest,
    dry canyon, farm, desert, beach, open water, downtown coffee cart,
    and office park may all exist in the same world.
-   The mixed-era quality should feel normal to inhabitants.
-   Roads, paths, bridges, waterways, railways, gates, signs, and other
    routes should make the world feel physically connected.

## Visual Identity

-   Ballpoint-pen linework
-   Watercolor-like color
-   Hand-drawn environmental details
-   Hand-lettered dialogue or speech bubbles
-   Whimsical illustrated presentation

The paper-and-ink appearance is a visual style, not necessarily a
literal supernatural system.

## Tone

Balance: - Playfulness - Absurdity - Warmth - Mystery - Melancholy -
Emotional sincerity

Humor should come from characters, situations, and the world's odd
logic.

## Gameplay Identity

The game focuses on: - Walking and exploring - Meeting memorable
characters - Completing small jobs and promises - Solving environmental
problems - Traversing unusual regions - Discovering relationships and
history - Collecting odd objects - Small minigames - Reconnecting places
and people

There is no combat-centered loop, no death-centered gameplay loop, no
player-created class, and no traditional skill-tree identity. The
protagonist is predefined.

## Protagonist

He begins with no memory of: - His name - His relationships - His
responsibilities - Why he left - Why he has returned

He should not feel like an empty player avatar. His personality, habits,
past decisions, and flaws should emerge through the world and its
people.

## Central Themes

-   Responsibility versus control
-   Being useful versus being loved
-   The burden of being needed
-   Trying to solve every problem alone
-   Repairing something versus accepting change
-   Memory, identity, and self-forgiveness
-   Belonging that does not need to be earned through constant work

## Story Priorities

1.  A strong central mystery
2.  Memorable characters
3.  A meaningful emotional arc
4.  A connected, explorable world
5.  Escalation from funny local problems to intimate but consequential
    choices

Avoid a generic "save the world" plot. Keep the stakes specific and
personal.
